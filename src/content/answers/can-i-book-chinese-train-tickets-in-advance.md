Two routes, and the choice is a genuine trade-off rather than an obvious one.

## The official app: 12306

**China Railway's own app.** It now has a full English mode, accepts foreign passport numbers as traveller ID, and takes international cards through Alipay.

- **Cheapest** — face value, no mark-up
- **Full inventory** — you see every train and every class, including services resellers do not list
- **Registration is the friction** — enter your passport number exactly as printed. A mismatch stops you at the gate on the day, which is the worst possible moment to discover a typo

## The reseller: Trip.com

A service fee of roughly ¥15–30 per ticket buys a friendlier interface, English customer support and easier refunds. If 12306's verification gives you trouble — and it sometimes does for foreign numbers — this is the pragmatic fallback rather than a compromise.

## When tickets go on sale

The pre-sale window has been adjusted repeatedly. Plan on tickets becoming available around **two weeks before departure** and confirm on 12306 rather than trusting any blog, including this one, on the exact number of days.

What matters more than the precise window:

- **Ordinary routes on ordinary days** rarely sell out. Booking a few days ahead is fine.
- **Popular routes around public holidays sell out within minutes** of release. Around Chinese New Year, the October Golden Week and the early-May holiday, be on the app the moment tickets open.
- **Beijing–Shanghai runs dozens of times a day.** Missing one specific service is usually not a crisis.

## What you actually receive

Nothing physical. Chinese rail is fully electronic and **your passport is the ticket** — you scan it at the station gate and again at the platform. There is no counter to visit and nothing to print.

## Choosing a class

Second class is 3+2 reclining seating with power sockets and is what most Chinese travellers use. First class is 2+2 and about 60% more. Business class is a lie-flat seat at roughly three times second class.

For a 4–6 hour journey, second class is genuinely fine. Spend the difference on food.
