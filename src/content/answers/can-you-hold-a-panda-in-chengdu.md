This is the most common thing people hope for in Chengdu and it has not been available for years, so it is worth knowing before you build a day — or a budget — around it.

## What changed

The paid photo sessions, in which a visitor sat with a young panda for a few minutes, were **suspended in 2018** across the programmes that had offered them. They have not returned. The volunteer programmes that still operate involve preparing food, cleaning enclosures and observing — **not** contact with the animals.

The reasoning is the obvious one. Giant pandas are a conservation programme rather than an attraction, close human contact carries a disease risk in both directions, and repeated handling is stressful for a young animal.

## What is still on offer

**The Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding** is open daily and is where nearly everyone goes. You see pandas at close range across a barrier, in enclosures rather than cages, and it is genuinely good — but the pandas are most active early. [Arrive at opening](/answers/what-time-should-i-arrive-at-the-chengdu-panda-base/) rather than mid-morning, which is the difference between watching animals eat and watching animals sleep.

**Volunteer programmes** run at some of the breeding centres outside the city, and involve real work rather than a photo opportunity. They are a longer, more expensive and more interesting day than the base. Availability moves around: some sites have closed for construction and upgrades in recent years, so confirm the specific base is open before you book anything non-refundable.

**Red pandas** wander semi-freely at the Chengdu base, and are — unpopular opinion — the better animal.

## If someone offers you a panda photo

Treat it as a warning sign about the whole booking. Operators still advertising a "panda hug" are either selling an experience that no longer exists, or selling access to an animal outside the conservation programme, which is not something to fund.

The same goes for any packaged tour whose headline is contact. Read what the itinerary actually includes rather than what the photograph on the listing implies.

## Setting expectations properly

You will see pandas, close, doing very little, in large numbers, and it will be better than you expect. What you will not do is touch one — and after an hour of watching them you will probably stop wanting to, which is a reasonable outcome for a conservation programme to produce.

Give it a half-day, go at opening, and pair it with the rest of the city rather than treating the pandas as the whole reason for coming. Chengdu is the place most travellers say they wish they had given more days, and the pandas are a morning of it.
