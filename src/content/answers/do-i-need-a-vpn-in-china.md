This is the question most often answered badly, because the honest answer is conditional and most guides give a flat yes.

The condition is your SIM card. Mainland China filters a large part of the foreign internet — [Google in every form](/answers/is-google-blocked-in-china/), WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook, X, YouTube, most Western news. But that filtering applies to traffic on a **Chinese** network. A foreign SIM roaming into China generally tunnels its data back to your home carrier, and therefore reaches everything normally.

## The decision, laid out

| How you connect | Reaches the open internet? | VPN needed? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home SIM, roaming | Usually yes | No |
| Foreign travel eSIM | Usually yes — confirm it routes internationally | No |
| Chinese SIM (China Unicom, China Mobile) | No | Yes |
| Hotel or café Wi-Fi | No | Yes |

So the practical answer for most short-trip visitors is: buy a foreign travel eSIM, and the firewall stops being your problem.

## Install one anyway, before you fly

Even if you do not expect to need it, set one up at home. This is the part that catches people out: **VPN provider websites are themselves blocked from inside China, and the apps are removed from the Chinese app stores.** You cannot download or purchase one after you land. A working installation costs you nothing to carry and saves the trip if your roaming fails or you end up on hotel Wi-Fi.

Two providers rather than one is worth it. Reliability fluctuates, and connections are noticeably throttled around politically sensitive dates — the annual Two Sessions in March, major anniversaries.

## On legality

Operating an unlicensed VPN service in China is illegal, and commercial use requires a licensed connection. Personal use by a foreign visitor to reach their own email and social media sits in a long-standing grey area: technically unauthorised, universally practised, and not something tourists are prosecuted for. Many international hotels provide VPN access as a matter of course.

Use ordinary judgement, do not publicise it, and do not use it for anything you would not do openly.

## What works without a VPN anyway

More than people expect. Apple services all work — iMessage, iCloud, Apple Maps, FaceTime, the App Store. So do Microsoft services, Bing, Zoom, Amazon, Booking.com, Trip.com, and almost every bank's app. Amap and Apple Maps both give accurate Chinese navigation in English.

If you can live without Google and Instagram for a fortnight, you can travel China with no VPN and a Chinese SIM perfectly happily.
