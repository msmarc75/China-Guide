This is the single most common way a Beijing itinerary breaks. Visitors arrive expecting a queue and a kiosk, and find neither.

## How the booking actually works

Tickets are released **seven days before the visit date, at 20:00 Beijing time**, and are valid only for the day booked. There is no same-day sale and no ticket office to fall back on. Peak weekends and holiday periods can clear in minutes.

Booking is **real-name throughout**: you enter the passport number of every visitor, and each person must carry that same passport to get in. A friend cannot book on your behalf using their own details, and the name on the booking has to match the document at the gate.

## The four official channels, and only these

The Palace Museum recognises four booking routes:

- the **Palace Museum visitor-services WeChat mini-programme**
- the **official website**
- the **official Palace Museum app**
- the **national culture-and-tourism unified booking platform**

Everything else is a reseller. The museum states plainly that it has authorised no third-party sellers, which matters because agency listings for "skip-the-line Forbidden City tickets" are abundant and are buying through the same public system you can use yourself.

## The morning and afternoon slots catch people out

Each day is split into **two separate sessions, and they are not interchangeable**:

- **Morning session** — you must pass the ticket check by **12:00**. Arrive later and the booking simply lapses.
- **Afternoon session** — you cannot enter before **11:00**.

So a morning ticket is not "a ticket you can use whenever that day". If your flight lands at 11:30 and you hold a morning slot, you have lost it.

## What to do if it is sold out

Nothing about the system rewards persistence at the gate, so redirect the day instead:

- **Refresh at 20:00 exactly** on the seven-day mark. This is when inventory appears, and it is the only moment with real availability for a popular date.
- **Watch for released cancellations.** People do cancel, and returned tickets reappear in the pool, though unpredictably.
- **Take the afternoon slot** if the morning has gone. The courtyards are busier but the light on the northern halls is better late in the day.
- **Climb Jingshan Park instead**, immediately north of the complex. It costs a fraction of the entry price and gives the view over the golden roofs that most people actually remember.

The separate **Treasure Gallery** and **Clock Gallery** tickets are booked on the same seven-day cycle and are worth adding when you book rather than deciding on the day.

## Book the rest of the trip the same way

The Forbidden City is the strictest example of a pattern that now runs across Chinese museums and major sights: passport-linked, timed, capped, and released on a fixed window. The Shaanxi History Museum, the Shanghai Museum and the Mogao Caves all work this way. Treating "buy the ticket when I get there" as a plan is the assumption to unlearn.
