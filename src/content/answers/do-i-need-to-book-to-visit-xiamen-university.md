Xiamen University is routinely called China's most beautiful campus, and it is a working university rather than an attraction — which is why access is rationed.

## How the booking works

Entry to the **Siming campus** is by advance booking through the **university's own official channel**, typically **one to three days ahead**, allocated in timed slots. There is no walk-up entry for visitors.

On normal working days the visiting window is narrow — admission runs only around the **middle of the day**, between roughly 12:30 and 14:00, when it interferes least with teaching. Holiday and vacation periods work differently, with wider windows and higher demand.

Bring the **passport you booked with**. Like most Chinese timed-entry systems this is real-name, and the document is checked at the gate.

## The rules move, so check the source

This is the part worth stating plainly: the university adjusts its visitor policy regularly, and it does so on its own channels rather than through travel sites. Slot counts, windows and booking lead times have all changed more than once.

Any third-party page describing the current rules — including this one — is a snapshot. **Check the university's official channel before you build a morning around it.** An agency selling "campus access" is not a substitute; there is no privileged route in.

## If you cannot get a slot

The campus is worth seeing but it is not the only thing in that part of Xiamen, and two alternatives sit immediately beside it:

- **Nanputuo Temple (南普陀寺)** is next door, is open without a booking, and is a genuinely active Buddhist temple with an excellent vegetarian restaurant.
- **The coastal path** around the campus perimeter gives you much of the setting — sea, palms, the hill behind — without entering.

Combining Nanputuo with the coast makes a good half-day whether or not the booking comes through, which is the sane way to plan it.

## Where it fits in a Xiamen trip

The campus sits on the Siming side of the city, close to the ferry piers for **Gulangyu** — the UNESCO-inscribed island, listed in 2017 under the spelling **Kulangsu**. Gulangyu has its own booking and pier constraints, and getting the right pier matters as much as getting the right ticket.

An hour up the coast is **Quanzhou**, which was the greatest port in the medieval world and is far less visited than it deserves.
