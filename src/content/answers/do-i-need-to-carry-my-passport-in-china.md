Foreign visitors often expect to leave the passport in a hotel safe and carry a photocopy. In China that will stop you at a ticket gate.

## Why it comes up so often

China runs on **real-name registration (实名制)**. Chinese residents have a national ID card that machines read in a fraction of a second; for a foreign visitor, the passport is the equivalent. That means it is not an occasional formality but the thing that unlocks ordinary transactions:

- **Hotels** scan it at check-in, which is how they register you with the local police.
- **Rail travel** uses it as the ticket itself — you scan the passport at the station gate and again at the platform. There is nothing else to show.
- **SIM cards** require it, with the registration tied to your name.
- **Museums and major sights** increasingly book on passport number and check the document at entry. The Forbidden City, the Shaanxi History Museum and the Mogao Caves all work this way.
- **Payment apps** ask for it during identity verification.

## The practical compromise

Carry the original, and keep a good photograph as backup.

The original is what a station gate or a hotel desk needs, and a photocopy will not scan. But a clear photo of the photo page and your visa or entry stamp, saved offline on your phone, covers the casual cases — a hotel wanting details while you are out, a form to fill in, a bar checking age.

Practical habits that reduce the risk:

- **Keep it on your body, not in a day bag** you might set down.
- **Photograph it before you travel** and save the image somewhere reachable without a signal, alongside your hotel addresses in Chinese characters.
- **Do not hand the original over as a deposit.** Hotels scan and return it; anyone wanting to hold it is doing something unusual.

## If you lose it

Report it to the local **Public Security Bureau** and get the report, then contact your embassy or consulate for an emergency travel document. You will also need a replacement visa or entry endorsement from the exit-entry administration before you can leave, which takes time — this is not a same-day problem.

The police report matters because your registration history is tied to the document. Gaps cause friction later, particularly around visa extensions.

## Registration follows the same logic

The passport is also what makes police registration work. Stay in a licensed hotel and it happens automatically at check-in. Stay in a private home or apartment and you must register yourself at the neighbourhood police station within 24 hours, with the passport in hand — see [registering with the police in China](/answers/do-i-need-to-register-with-police-in-china/).
