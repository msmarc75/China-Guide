This surprises almost every first-time visitor, because no Western metro system does it. It is completely routine, applies to every passenger equally, and is nothing to be anxious about.

## What happens

You queue briefly at the station entrance. Bags go on a belt through an X-ray machine — handbags and daypacks included, not just large luggage. A guard may wave a handheld wand over you.

If you are carrying a drink, expect to be asked to drink some of it. This is a check for flammable liquids, and holding up a sealed factory bottle is usually enough.

Then you collect your bag and continue to the ticket gates. The whole thing takes moments when quiet.

The scanner is the first of two rulebooks: the second governs the carriage, where [eating and playing audio out loud are both on the prohibited list](/guides/riding-the-chinese-metro/).

## What it means in practice

**Allow extra time at rush hour and at big interchanges.** At the busiest stations the queue is the slowest part of the journey. If you are catching a train with a fixed departure, this is the step people forget to budget for.

**Do not carry anything obviously prohibited.** Flammable liquids, large knives and fireworks are the categories that matter — and fireworks are barred from the system whatever the city has decided about [setting them off in the street](/guides/fireworks-in-china/), because the two questions run under different rules. Ordinary travel possessions are fine — laptops, cameras, tripods, a folding knife in checked-style luggage is where judgement is needed, but nothing a tourist normally carries will cause a problem.

**Luggage is allowed.** You can take a suitcase on the metro, and plenty of people do. It goes through the scanner like everything else.

:::warn Railway stations are a much bigger version of the same thing
Intercity train stations have full airport-style screening plus a passport identity check, and the queues are far longer. That is a different exercise from the metro, and needs proper time built in.
:::

## Where else you will see it

The same screening appears at intercity railway stations, airports obviously, and at the entrances to many major tourist sites, government buildings and large shopping centres. Getting used to it early makes the rest of the trip smoother.

Carry your passport. It is required for train travel and hotel check-in, and occasionally asked for at checkpoints.
