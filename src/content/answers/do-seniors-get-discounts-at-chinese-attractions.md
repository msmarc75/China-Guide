Concessions for older visitors are a statutory obligation in China, not a courtesy each attraction chooses. **Article 59 of the Law on the Protection of the Rights and Interests of the Elderly** requires that museums, galleries, science and memorial museums, public libraries, cultural centres, theatres, sports venues, parks and tourist sites "open free of charge or at a discount to older people".

That settles whether a concession exists. It does not settle the two things you actually need to know.

## The age is set by the province, not the country

The National Development and Reform Commission requires scenic areas on government-set or government-guided pricing to give concessions to older visitors, but leaves each province to fix the qualifying age. The practical result is that the same person qualifies in one province and not in the next.

- **Most provinces set the threshold at 65 or 70.**
- **A minority have lowered it to 60.** Chinese reporting disagrees about exactly which — published lists of the 60-and-over provinces do not match each other — so treat any specific list, including one you find here, as unreliable and check the province you are actually visiting.

Fujian is a documented example of the common pattern: from 2022, over-65s enter scenic areas free, and pay half fare on shuttle buses and cable cars at state-invested scenic areas. That second half matters more than it sounds, because in Chinese scenic areas [the transport inside the park is frequently a bigger line item than the gate](/plan/china-trip-cost-budget/).

## The document is the part that decides it for a foreigner

This is where the concession is won or lost, and the wording differs between provinces.

- Where a rule says **凭有效证件** (*píng yǒuxiào zhèngjiàn*) — "on presentation of valid identification" — a foreign passport is valid identification, and it shows your date of birth. Fujian's rule is worded this way.
- Where a rule says **凭身份证** (*píng shēnfènzhèng*) — "on presentation of an identity card" — it names the PRC resident identity card specifically, and a passport is not that document.

Nothing in the national law restricts the concession to Chinese citizens, and gate staff at major sites are generally used to reading a passport. But a scheme written around the resident ID card may be administered around it too, particularly at a provincial scheme aimed at its own residents.

The honest advice is therefore to **present your passport and expect it to work at large well-known sites, while budgeting as though it will not.** Do not build a trip's budget on the concession.

## How this differs from the disability concession

Worth knowing if you have read the other side of this: China's disability concessions are gated on a PRC disability certificate that a foreign visitor [cannot obtain at all](/guides/accessible-travel-in-china/), which makes them genuinely unavailable to you. The age concession is not like that. Age is a fact your passport already proves, so the obstacle here is administrative wording rather than a document you can never hold.

## Practical points at the gate

- **Bring the passport itself**, not a photograph. It is the document being checked, and it is the one you [need to be carrying anyway](/answers/do-i-need-to-carry-my-passport-in-china/).
- **Concession signs are usually in Chinese only.** If you cannot read the board, show your passport at the ticket window and let them look.
- **Free entry still often needs the booking.** Many Chinese museums and sights are free but require a timed advance reservation against your passport number, and a concession does not remove that step.
- **Ask about the shuttle and the cable car separately.** They are priced separately from the gate and are concessioned separately too.
