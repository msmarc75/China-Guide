Yes, as a category. Students are named explicitly in national policy, and where the concession applies the standard rate is **half the adult ticket**. Whether *your* student card claims it is a separate question, and the honest answer is that it depends on wording you can check before you go.

## Students are a named category nationally

The National Development and Reform Commission's notice on state-owned scenic area ticket pricing — **发改办价格** (*fā gǎi bàn jiàgé*, NDRC Office price notice) 〔2020〕568号, of 27 July 2020 — requires sites on government pricing to provide fare reductions and preferential treatment to a listed set of groups:

> 对现役军人、残疾军人、烈属、因公牺牲军人遗属、病故军人遗属、现役军人家属，以及未成年人、老年人、学生、残疾人、宗教人士等，应按规定切实提供门票减免等优待。

Serving military personnel, disabled veterans, martyrs' families and the bereaved families of service members, together with **minors, older people, students, disabled people and religious personnel**, are to be given ticket reductions and concessions as provided.

So the concession is not a favour each site invents. It sits in the same national framework as [the senior concession](/answers/do-seniors-get-discounts-at-chinese-attractions/), and it is why concession boards at Chinese gates list several categories rather than one.

## The rate, and the wording that decides it

The national notice sets the obligation; provinces write the detail. Fujian is a documented example of the common pattern — the same province the senior answer uses, for the same reason, that its rules are published plainly. Its measures for scenic area ticket pricing grant half price:

> 对6周岁～18周岁（含18周岁）未成年人、全日制大学本科及以下学历在校学生、60周岁（含60周岁）至69周岁的老年人…凭有效证件实行半票。

Minors from 6 to 18 inclusive, **full-time students studying at bachelor's level or below**, and people aged 60 to 69, on presentation of **valid identification**, pay half fare.

Two things matter in that sentence.

**The rate is half, not free.** Free entry in these schemes tends to sit with the youngest children, the over-65s and the military categories; students get the half-price band.

**It says 凭有效证件** (*píng yǒuxiào zhèngjiàn*, "on presentation of valid identification") — not 凭学生证 (*píng xuéshēngzhèng*, "on presentation of a student card"), which would name the Chinese student booklet specifically. That distinction is exactly the one that decides whether a foreign visitor can claim the senior concession, and it works the same way here.

## What that means in practice

Be realistic about it. A student concession is easier to write into a rule than to claim at a window, because the thing being verified is enrolment rather than a fact printed in your passport — age, which the passport proves, is why the senior concession travels better.

- **Carry your university card**, with the current academic year visible on it. An international student card is worth having for the same reason, though nothing in the rules we have seen names one.
- **Expect the concession where you are enrolled in China.** Foreign students at Chinese institutions hold the standard **学生证** (*xuéshēngzhèng*, student card) and are in the ordinary case of the rule rather than the awkward one.
- **Ask, but budget the full fare.** The advice here matches the senior page: present the card, and [plan the trip as though it will not be accepted](/plan/china-trip-cost-budget/).
- **The gate ticket is not the whole cost.** Shuttle buses and cable cars inside a Chinese site are [priced and concessioned separately](/guides/chinese-scenic-areas/), and are often the larger number.
- **A concession does not replace a booking.** Where a site requires advance reservation against a passport number, that step still applies.

Only one province's wording is quoted above, deliberately. Provinces differ, and a rule written around a Chinese student card will be administered around one — so check the province you are visiting rather than assuming Fujian's phrasing travels.
