The rail link makes this one of the easiest international borders anywhere, which is precisely why the visa arithmetic catches people out.

## The train

Services run from **Guangzhou South** to **Hong Kong West Kowloon** in about **50 minutes**. Immigration for both sides is done inside West Kowloon under a co-location arrangement, so you clear mainland exit and Hong Kong entry in the same building rather than at a land crossing.

Book it like any other Chinese high-speed service, through 12306 or an agency, and arrive with the usual station buffer — West Kowloon services are subject to the same gate-closing discipline as domestic trains.

## The part that actually needs thinking about

**Hong Kong is a separate immigration zone.** Crossing into it means:

- You **leave mainland China**, using up that entry. Returning to the mainland afterwards requires a fresh entry — a multiple-entry visa, or renewed visa-free eligibility.
- A **single-entry Chinese visa does not survive the trip**. Go to Hong Kong on one and you cannot come back — the [full explanation of why Hong Kong is a separate immigration zone](/answers/is-hong-kong-part-of-china-for-visa-purposes/) covers Macau and Taiwan too.
- **Most nationalities enter Hong Kong visa-free** on arrival — British citizens for 180 days, and EU, US, Canadian and Australian passport holders for 90 — so the Hong Kong side is rarely the problem.

## Why this is useful under the transit scheme

If you are travelling on **240-hour visa-free transit**, Hong Kong counts as a **third region**. That means a routing of, say, New York → Beijing → Hong Kong satisfies the onward-ticket requirement that a return to your origin country would not. It is one of the things that makes that scheme far more usable than the word "transit" suggests.

The [visa guide](/guides/china-visa-guide/) sets out which route applies to your passport, and it is worth being certain before you book the return leg rather than after.

## The other ways across

**Shenzhen and walk.** High-speed rail to Shenzhen North, then metro to Futian Checkpoint or Lo Wu, and cross on foot. Slower and more steps, but cheaper, and it lets you break the journey in Shenzhen.

**Ferry.** Services run from Guangdong ports, and there is the 55 km Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau bridge with cross-border coaches if you are heading that way anyway.

**Flying** makes no sense over this distance and adds two airport transfers to a 50-minute train.

## Different money, different plugs, different internet

Once across, several assumptions change at once. Hong Kong uses its own currency, **UK-style type G plugs** rather than the mainland's type A and I, and an **unfiltered internet** — Google, WhatsApp and Instagram all work normally with no VPN.
