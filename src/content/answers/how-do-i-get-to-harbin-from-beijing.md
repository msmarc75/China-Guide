Harbin is 1,200 km northeast of Beijing, and until recently that was an overnight sleeper. The high-speed line has made it a comfortable half-day.

## The train

The **Beijing–Harbin high-speed railway** runs 1,198 km and has operated at 350 km/h on the Beijing–Shenyang section since July 2025. The fastest scheduled services cover **Beijing Chaoyang to Harbin West in 4 hours 33 minutes**, and there are around twenty departures a day, so the timetable is not a constraint.

**Check the departure station.** These are Beijing **Chaoyang** services — a station many visitors have never heard of and will not be staying near. Beijing has several major stations and booking apps sort by journey time rather than by which one is convenient for your hotel, so read the station name before you pay and budget metro time to reach it.

At the far end, **Harbin West** is the high-speed station rather than Harbin station in the older centre. Both are in the city; they are not the same place.

## Flying

About **two hours** in the air, plus two airport transfers and security at both ends. Door to door the advantage over rail is smaller than the flight time suggests, and in a Harbin winter the weather risk runs the other way — the train is the more reliable option in January.

## Going for the ice festival

Most people making this journey are going for the **Harbin International Ice and Snow Sculpture Festival**, which runs from late December to late February — [the official 5 January opening is a ceremony, not the gate](/answers/when-is-the-harbin-ice-festival/). Two things follow from that.

**Book earlier than feels necessary.** This is a domestic tourism peak, and rail seats and hotel rooms both tighten in January.

**Prepare for the cold properly.** January averages around **−18°C**, which is a different category of cold from a European or North American winter city — it freezes eyelashes, drains phone batteries within minutes of exposure, and makes the walk from the station something to plan rather than endure. The festival's main venue is rebuilt at a different scale each winter, so treat any area figure you read as belonging to a particular year.

## What else is up there

Harbin is not only the ice. It grew out of the **Chinese Eastern Railway**, built by Russia in the 1890s, and had a large Russian population for decades — including tens of thousands of White Russian refugees after 1917. The architecture, the bread, the sausage and the vodka all survive, and the city is worth a day in its own right in any season.
