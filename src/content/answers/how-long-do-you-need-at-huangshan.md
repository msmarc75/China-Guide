The honest answer is that a day trip up Huangshan is a long, expensive walk in whatever weather you happen to get. The mountain gives up its best in the twenty minutes either side of dawn and dusk, and both require sleeping on top.

## Why one night changes everything

The *yúnhǎi* — the sea of clouds that fills the valleys and made this mountain the subject of Chinese landscape painting for twelve centuries — is a morning phenomenon, most likely after rain and in the colder months. Day visitors arrive after it has burned off and leave before the light turns again. That is the whole argument.

Sunset from the western viewpoints and sunrise from the ridge near the summit hotels are also the only times the paths are quiet. By ten in the morning the mountain is busy in a way that changes the experience considerably.

## The two-day shape

**Day one:** get to the mountain foot, ride one of the three cable cars up — Yungu from the east, Yuping from the south, Taiping from the north — and spend the afternoon on the summit path network. Beginning-to-Believe Peak, the Flying-over Rock, Bright Summit. Watch sunset, then eat in your hotel because there is nowhere else.

**Day two:** up before dawn for sunrise, then the **West Sea Grand Canyon** if it is open — a plank path cut into a cliff face, the most spectacular section on the mountain, and it needs four to five hours. Descend by a different cable car from the one you came up on, so you see new ground.

If the canyon is closed — it shuts in winter and after heavy rain — day two is shorter, and you can be off the mountain by early afternoon.

## Book the summit hotel first, not last

There are only a handful of hotels up there, they are expensive for what they are, and they sell out **months** ahead in autumn and around holidays. Dormitory beds are the cheaper option and exist in most of them.

This is the reverse of how people usually plan a Chinese trip. Fix the summit night, then build the rest of the itinerary around the date you got — not the other way round, or you will find yourself doing the day trip you were trying to avoid.

## Three days, if you have them

The valley below is the point of the third day. **Hongcun** and **Xidi** are UNESCO-listed Huizhou merchant villages of white walls and black tiles, reached by bus from Huangshan North station, and Hongcun in particular is transformed by staying overnight — the lanes belong to the residents at dawn and to coach parties by ten.

That station puts the mountain **1h30 from Hangzhou and 2h30 from Shanghai**, which is what makes three days realistic from either city. Avoid the national holiday weeks while you are choosing dates: the summit paths become one-way human queues, and a weekday outside holidays is a different mountain.
