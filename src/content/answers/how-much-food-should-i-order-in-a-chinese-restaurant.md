A Chinese menu is not a list of individual meals. Everything arrives in the centre and everyone eats from all of it, so the unit of ordering is the table rather than the person.

## The rule of thumb

**Number of diners, plus one.** Four people order five dishes; six people order seven. Then add:

- **Rice** (米饭, *mǐfàn*), ordered by the bowl and priced per bowl. It is not automatic and it is not included.
- **A soup**, if the meal is a proper one. In much of China soup is drunk through or after the meal rather than before it.
- **Cold dishes** (凉菜, *liángcài*) at the start if you want them — these are small and do not count towards the main tally.

Dishes arrive as they are cooked rather than together, which is normal and not a sign that something has gone wrong.

## Balance matters more than quantity

A table of five stir-fries is a worse meal than a table of three plus a soup and a cold dish. The convention is to spread the order across:

- one or two **meat or fish** dishes,
- one or two **vegetable** dishes,
- something with a **different texture or temperature** — a cold dish, a braise, a soup,
- a **staple**: rice, noodles, or in the north, dumplings or bread.

Ordering four things in chilli oil is the commonest way to make a good restaurant seem monotonous.

## If you are eating alone

This is the case the system genuinely does not fit, and it is worth planning around rather than fighting. A solo diner ordering from the main dish list gets a portion built for three.

Order instead from the sections built for one person: **noodle dishes** (面, *miàn*), **dumplings** sold by weight or by the basket, **rice bowls** with a single topping (盖饭, *gàifàn*), and the small-eats or snack section. Every Chinese city has plenty of shops that do exactly one of these things well, and they are frequently the better meal anyway.

## Leftovers are fine

Over-ordering slightly is normal and even mildly generous when you are hosting, and there is no shame in a doggy bag — ask to 打包 (*dǎbāo*), which every restaurant does routinely. What is worth avoiding is the wholesale over-order that leaves half the table untouched, which reads as waste rather than generosity.

## Two things that catch people out

**Prices are per dish, not per person**, so a menu that looks expensive often is not once it is divided by four.

**Portion sizes vary enormously by region and by restaurant type.** Northern portions run larger than southern ones, and a neighbourhood restaurant serves more for the money than a mall one. Order conservatively on the first round — you can always add a dish, and adding one mid-meal is completely normal.
