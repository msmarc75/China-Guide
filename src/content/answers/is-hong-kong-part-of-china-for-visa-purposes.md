Politically Hong Kong is part of China. For a border officer it is not, and that distinction is what strands people.

## What actually happens at the border

Crossing from the mainland into Hong Kong means:

- **Exiting mainland China**, which uses up that entry.
- **Entering Hong Kong** under its own rules, where most nationalities are admitted visa-free on arrival — British citizens for 180 days, and EU, US, Canadian and Australian passport holders for 90.
- **Re-entering the mainland afterwards requires a fresh entry**, which means a multiple-entry visa or renewed visa-free eligibility.

Macau works the same way. So does the crossing between Hong Kong and Macau.

## The mistake that ends a trip

**A single-entry Chinese visa does not survive the trip.** Go to Hong Kong on one, and you cannot come back. Every year people plan Beijing → Shanghai → Hong Kong → Guilin and discover the problem at the Hong Kong side of the border, by which point the mainland visa is spent.

If your itinerary returns to the mainland after Hong Kong, you need one of:

- a **multiple-entry visa**, requested at application time
- **unilateral visa-free eligibility**, which now covers fifty nationalities for 30 days and renews on each entry
- **240-hour transit eligibility**, subject to its own rules

Check which applies before you book the return leg, not after.

## Where it works in your favour

Under the **240-hour visa-free transit** scheme, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan count as **third regions**. That means a routing of New York → Beijing → Hong Kong satisfies the onward-ticket requirement that a return to your origin country would not — the single detail that makes that scheme genuinely usable for Americans and Canadians.

So the same separation that costs you an entry on one itinerary unlocks an entire visa route on another. It depends entirely on which direction you are travelling.

## What else changes at the line

The border is not only administrative. Crossing it changes:

- **Currency** — the Hong Kong dollar, not the renminbi, and Alipay's mainland transport and payment codes do not simply carry over.
- **Plugs** — UK-style type G at 220 V, against the mainland's type A and I.
- **The internet** — unfiltered. Google, WhatsApp and Instagram all work with no VPN, which is why Hong Kong is often the first place a traveller catches up on email.

## Getting across

The usual route from the mainland is **high-speed rail to Hong Kong West Kowloon**, where both sets of immigration are handled inside the terminus — about 50 minutes from Guangzhou South. See [getting from Guangzhou to Hong Kong](/answers/how-do-i-get-from-guangzhou-to-hong-kong/).
