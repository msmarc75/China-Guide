The Maglev is a genuine engineering landmark — magnetically levitated, frictionless, and quicker than anything else carrying passengers on rails. The question is whether it is a good way to get from the airport to your hotel, and that is a different question.

## What it actually does

It runs between Pudong Airport and **Longyang Road** station. That is the whole line. At Longyang Road you change to Line 2 or Line 16 to continue into the city.

So the trip is: Maglev, then a change, then a metro ride. Against Metro Line 2 straight through from the airport, the door-to-door difference is much smaller than the speed suggests — and if you are carrying luggage, the change costs you some of what the speed gained.

## When it makes sense

**As an experience.** Watching the speed display climb is genuinely a thing to have done, and it takes very little time. If you enjoy trains at all, ride it.

**If you are heading to Pudong anyway.** Staying in Lujiazui or on the east bank makes Longyang Road a sensible interchange rather than a detour.

**Travelling light.** Without heavy bags the change is trivial.

## When to skip it

**Heading to Puxi with luggage.** Line 2 runs from the airport to People's Square and the Bund area without a change. Simplicity beats speed after a long-haul flight.

**Late arrivals.** The Maglev does not run as late as the metro, so check it is operating before you plan around it.

:::warn It is a separate ticket
The Maglev is not part of the metro network and needs its own fare, though a discount is generally available on presentation of a same-day air ticket. Ask at the counter rather than assuming.
:::

## The honest summary

Ride it once, ideally on the way *out* to the airport when you are not tired and not deciding anything. Take Line 2 on the way in.
