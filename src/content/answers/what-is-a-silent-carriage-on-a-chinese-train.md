A **静音车厢** (*jìngyīn chēxiāng*, silent carriage) is an opt-in quiet coach on Chinese high-speed services. You select it when you book, and in exchange for a quieter journey you accept an undertaking to keep the noise down yourself.

## What the railway actually changes

It is not simply a coach where people are asked to be considerate. The operator adjusts the coach itself:

- **Announcement and audio-visual volume is turned down** for that carriage.
- **Standard silent signage and service cards** mark it, so nobody boards unaware.
- **Staff switch to a low voice** when working the coach.
- **Disposable earplugs are carried** for passengers who want them.

The stated operating principle is voluntary compliance, shared upkeep and moderate intervention — passengers choose it, passengers maintain it, and staff step in only when needed. In practice that means it works because everyone in the coach opted in, not because it is policed.

## How to book one

Two things to look for, and the first is easy to scroll past.

**The marker is a single character.** Services offering the option carry **静** (*jìng*, quiet) on the 12306 booking screen. That is the whole visual cue.

**You have to agree to the undertaking.** Buying a silent-carriage seat requires accepting the silent agreement at the point of purchase — you cannot end up in one by accident, and you cannot opt out of the expectation once you are there. Booking flows also offer a preference option asking the system to allocate you a silent-carriage seat.

Everything else about buying the ticket is unchanged, including [the real-name requirement and the document check](/guides/transport-in-china/) that applies to any Chinese rail booking.

## Where you will find them

The service began as a pilot in **December 2020** on the Beijing–Shanghai and Chengdu–Chongqing high-speed lines, then spread line by line — Beijing–Guangzhou, Zhengzhou–Chongqing, Beijing–Harbin, Shanghai–Kunming, Xi'an–Chengdu, Guiyang–Nanning, and the cross-border services to Hong Kong.

**Since 1 February 2026 it covers far more of the network**: all D-prefix and G-prefix distributed-power EMU services, with one exception below.

How many coaches a given train has depends on its formation. An eight- or sixteen-car single set has one silent carriage; a seventeen-car long-formation Fuxing has two; a coupled set has one in each of its front and rear units.

## The exception is the one you might most want

**Sleeper EMU services are excluded.** The February extension covers D and G services *except* the sleeper trains.

That is worth knowing precisely because a night train is where noise matters most — and where [the choice between a four-berth compartment with a door and an open six-berth bay](/guides/sleeper-trains-in-china/) is doing the job a silent carriage would otherwise do. On a sleeper, privacy is bought with the berth class, not with a quiet coach.

## Is it worth choosing?

Yes, at no extra cost, if you want to read or work. The fare is the ordinary fare for that class — the silent carriage is an allocation preference rather than a paid upgrade.

Worth noticing how differently the metro solves the same problem: there the ban on [playing audio out loud is a national rule applied to every carriage](/guides/riding-the-chinese-metro/), rather than something you opt into for one coach.

Two honest caveats. It is quieter, not silent: this is a shared coach on a working railway, and children travel in it like anyone else. And it constrains you as much as it protects you, so if you expect to take a long call, book an ordinary coach and use [the headphones you packed anyway](/plan/china-packing-list/).
