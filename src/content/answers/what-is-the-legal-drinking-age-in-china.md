**China does not set a minimum age for drinking alcohol.** What it sets is a ban on **selling** alcohol to anyone under eighteen — a duty on the seller, backed by a sign the shop must display and a fine only the shop can pay.

The distinction sounds pedantic and is not. It explains the thing every visitor notices, which is that nobody at a Chinese restaurant table asks anybody's age.

## Where the rule actually lives

Not in a licensing act. It is in the **中华人民共和国未成年人保护法** (*Zhōnghuá Rénmín Gònghéguó wèi chéngniánrén bǎohù fǎ*, Law of the People's Republic of China on the Protection of Minors), comprehensively revised on **17 October 2020** at the 22nd session of the 13th National People's Congress Standing Committee, promulgated as **Presidential Order No. 57**, and **in force since 1 June 2021**. **Article 2** fixes who counts: 本法所称未成年人是指未满十八周岁的公民 — a minor is a citizen who has not completed eighteen years.

**Article 59** is the operative one, and it does four things at once:

> 学校、幼儿园周边不得设置烟、酒、彩票销售网点。禁止向未成年人销售烟、酒、彩票或者兑付彩票奖金。烟、酒和彩票经营者应当在显著位置设置不向未成年人销售烟、酒或者彩票的标志；对难以判明是否是未成年人的，应当要求其出示身份证件。任何人不得在学校、幼儿园和其他未成年人集中活动的公共场所吸烟、饮酒。

No sales outlets for tobacco, alcohol or lottery tickets **around schools and kindergartens**. **No selling** any of the three to a minor, or paying out a lottery prize to one. Sellers **must display, in a conspicuous position, a sign that they do not sell tobacco, alcohol or lottery tickets to minors** — and where they cannot tell whether someone is a minor, they **must ask for identity documents**. And nobody at all may **smoke or drink in schools, kindergartens or other public places where minors gather.**

Read it again and notice who is bound. Three of those four sentences bind the shop. Only the last binds you.

## Two things that follow

**The penalty is the shop's, and it is large.** Article 123 puts breaches of Article 59's first paragraph in the hands of the culture and tourism, market regulation, tobacco monopoly and public security departments: an order to correct within a time limit, a warning, confiscation of unlawful gains and a fine of up to **¥50,000** — and for refusal to correct or a serious case, suspension for rectification or revocation of the business licence or permit, with a fine of **¥50,000 to ¥500,000**. There is no corresponding penalty aimed at a young person who drinks.

**The sign is the twenty-third time this site has found a rule printed in plain sight.** It sits at the till of convenience shops and supermarkets, and it is there because the statute requires it in a conspicuous position, not because the chain decided to be careful. If it is missing, that is a compliance failure you can see from the queue.

## What to actually do

- **Carry ID if you look young.** The law tells the seller to ask when age is unclear, so a passport in the bag settles it. There is no Chinese equivalent of a proof-of-age card scheme for visitors.
- **Do not assume a bar will check.** Most will not, because the risk sits with off-licence retail far more than with restaurants — but the duty is the same one.
- **The place matters more than the age.** The one prohibition that binds everybody is about **where**: not on school and kindergarten premises, and not in other public places where minors gather.
- **Tobacco runs on this same article**, which is why [the smoking rules read alike](/guides/smoking-and-vaping-in-china/) though enforcement differs — and [the tattoo rules copy the sentence wholesale](/culture/tattoos-in-china/).
- **Nothing here touches drink-driving**, which is a separate and far harsher regime with no tolerance for a first drink.
- **Hong Kong and Macau legislate alcohol separately** under their own ordinances, so neither the age nor the seller duties described here apply to a purchase made there.

## Is enforcement light?

At a small shop, often. In a chain supermarket with a till prompt, much less so. That is a statement about practice, not about the rule — and [whatever you have been told about a Chinese drinking age](/food/chinese-tea-and-drinks/), the rule is about who may sell.
