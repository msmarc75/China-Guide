This is the most consequential piece of timing advice for a Chengdu trip, and it is routinely ignored because it does not sound like a rule.

## Why the hour matters so much

Giant pandas spend most of the day asleep. Their active window is the **morning feed**, when they come out for bamboo, climb, and behave like animals rather than furniture. By **10:00** most of them have eaten and settled.

Two things therefore happen if you arrive late. The pandas are less interesting, and the enclosures are more crowded — coach groups begin arriving from around **09:00**, so the worst viewing conditions coincide exactly with the least activity.

Arriving at the **07:30 opening** gives you roughly two hours of both good behaviour and thin crowds. It is a genuinely different visit.

## Do the nursery first

Once inside, use the **electric shuttle** to reach the nursery area first and then walk back through the enclosures on foot. The nursery is where the youngest animals are and it is the part that fills first, so seeing it before the arriving groups reach it is worth the shuttle fare.

Walking back rather than shuttling both ways also means you pass the **red pandas**, which wander semi-freely and are — unpopular opinion — the better animal.

## Getting there in time

The base is about 10 km north of the centre. Two routes work:

- **Metro line 3 to Panda Avenue** (熊猫大道站), which carries the alternate name Panda Base South Gate, then the shuttle. The station exit connects directly.
- **A DiDi**, roughly 30 minutes from the centre.

For a 07:30 arrival, either means leaving well before the city wakes up. This is the one Chengdu morning worth setting an alarm for.

## What you cannot do

**Hold a panda.** Keeper photo and hugging arrangements have been discontinued across Chinese panda facilities, and any operator advertising them is selling something that no longer exists.

What does still exist is the **volunteer programme at Dujiangyan**, about 60 km away, where you help prepare food and clean enclosures. That is closer contact than any viewing platform offers, and it is a different sort of day — quieter, longer, and not a substitute for the main base.

## If you cannot make the morning

Go anyway, but adjust expectations rather than the plan: late afternoon is second-best, some animals stir again before closing, and the site is pleasant to walk regardless. What does not work is treating a midday visit as equivalent and then concluding pandas are boring.
