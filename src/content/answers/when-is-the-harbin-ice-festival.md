Almost every English page on this gives 5 January as the answer and stops there, which sends people to Harbin later than they need to go and sometimes causes them to miss it entirely.

## The two dates that matter

**5 January** is the official opening of the festival. It has been the fixed date for decades, and since 2000 the opening ceremony has been held at Ice and Snow World. It is a date in a programme rather than the day the ice appears.

**Mid-to-late December** is when **Ice and Snow World** (哈尔滨冰雪大世界, *Hā'ěrbīn Bīngxuě Dà Shìjiè*) normally opens to visitors — a trial opening once the ice is thick enough to build with. An 18 December opening is on record for one recent season.

The park is built from ice cut out of the **Songhua River**, so the schedule follows the river rather than the calendar. A cold early winter brings it forward; a mild one pushes it back. Nothing about the dates is guaranteed until the ice is thick enough.

## How long the season lasts

Roughly a month for the festival proper, extended to **fifty or sixty days** when the weather allows, and the ice structures themselves can stand outdoors for over two months. Closing dates in early to mid-March are on record.

That gives a practical window of **late December to late February**, with January the safest month and the coldest.

## What that means for booking

- **Do not wait for 5 January.** Late December visits get the park with fewer domestic visitors, because the Chinese school holidays and Spring Festival crowds come later.
- **Avoid the Spring Festival week itself** unless you want the crowds — the [date moves each year](/answers/when-is-chinese-new-year/) between 21 January and 21 February and lands squarely inside the season.
- **Confirm the current season's opening before booking flights.** The park announces it when the ice allows, and it is the one date nobody can give you a year in advance.

## The cold is the real planning problem

Harbin in January sits far below freezing for weeks at a time, and Ice and Snow World is at its best after dark when the ice is lit — which is also when it is coldest. This is not a place to improvise clothing.

The city rents heavy coats, and the practical approach is layers plus something genuinely rated for the temperature, proper boots, and gloves you can keep on. Phone batteries die fast in that cold; keep one in an inside pocket.

Harbin is also worth a day beyond the ice: the Russian-era architecture along Central Street and the Saint Sophia Cathedral are the reason the city looks unlike anywhere else in China.
