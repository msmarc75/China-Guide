This is the single most expensive booking mistake on the Silk Road route, and it happens because both stations legitimately serve the Dunhuang area.

## The two stations

**Dunhuang station (敦煌站)** sits about **10 km from the town**, on the Dunhuang branch line. It is a conventional railway rather than a high-speed one, so journeys take longer — but it puts you a short taxi ride from your hotel.

**Liuyuan South (柳园南站)** is on the Lanzhou–Xinjiang high-speed line, about **130 km away** in a different county. Arriving there means a road transfer of roughly two hours before you see Dunhuang at all.

## Why booking apps steer you wrong

Apps rank results by journey time. A high-speed train to Liuyuan South looks faster than a conventional train to Dunhuang, so it appears at the top — and the app has no way of knowing that the fast option adds a two-hour drive at the far end.

The station name does not help either. Liuyuan is frequently described in English sources as "the station for Dunhuang", which is true in the sense that it is the nearest high-speed stop and false in every sense that matters to your afternoon.

**Read the station name on the ticket before you pay.** This is a general rule for Chinese rail — large cities routinely have several stations — but Dunhuang is where the gap between them is largest.

## When Liuyuan South is the right choice

It is not always wrong. If you are travelling the Lanzhou–Urumqi corridor and Dunhuang is one stop among several, the high-speed line may genuinely suit the route better, and the transfer is a known quantity rather than a surprise.

What you cannot do is book it by accident and expect to be in town for dinner. If you choose it, arrange the onward transfer in advance — this is not a place to improvise a taxi at 22:00.

## Plan the caves around the train, not the other way round

Whichever station you use, the **Mogao Caves** are the fixed point. Tickets are real-name, capped and released up to 30 days ahead through the Dunhuang Academy's own channels, with no walk-up option — see [booking the Mogao Caves](/answers/do-i-need-to-book-mogao-caves-tickets-in-advance/).

Get the cave slot first, then build the rail booking around it. Doing it the other way produces the situation where you have a train and no tickets, or tickets and a two-hour transfer eating the morning.

## The rest of the region

Dunhuang is also the base for the singing sand dunes, Crescent Lake and the Han frontier passes at Yumen and Yangguan, all of which are road trips from the town rather than the station.
