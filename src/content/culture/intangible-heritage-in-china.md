This phrase turns up all over this site. [Seal engraving](/culture/seals-and-chops-in-china/) is intangible cultural heritage. So are [kunqu and Peking opera](/culture/chinese-opera-and-performance/), [acupuncture](/culture/chinese-medicine-in-china/), [weiqi and Chinese chess](/culture/weiqi-and-chinese-chess/) and [the Dong grand song of southeast Guizhou](/destinations/guizhou/). Even Chengdu's ear cleaners went through an application.

Seven pages use it as a property of something else and none of them says what it is. Two of them go further and promise that somebody will explain it: [the heritage page](/culture/chinese-heritage-protection/) ends its checklist by saying there is a second list, for things that are not objects, and the board games page calls itself the intangible half of a system whose tangible half was already covered.

Here is that half. It is a statute, a hierarchy of lists — and a second register that most foreign accounts never mention, of **named living people**.

## It has its own law, and the law is not the list

The **中华人民共和国非物质文化遗产法** (*Zhōnghuá Rénmín Gònghéguó fēi wùzhì wénhuà yíchǎn fǎ*, Law of the People's Republic of China on Intangible Cultural Heritage) was adopted on **25 February 2011** at the 19th session of the Standing Committee of the 11th National People's Congress, promulgated as **Presidential Order No. 42**, and has been **in force since 1 June 2011**. Six chapters, forty-five articles.

**Article 2** defines the subject as the traditional cultural expressions handed down through generations by the peoples of China and regarded by them as part of their cultural heritage, together with the objects and places associated with those expressions. It then lists what counts, in six headings:

> （一）传统口头文学以及作为其载体的语言；（二）传统美术、书法、音乐、舞蹈、戏剧、曲艺和杂技；（三）传统技艺、医药和历法；（四）传统礼仪、节庆等民俗；（五）传统体育和游艺；（六）其他非物质文化遗产。

**Traditional oral literature and the language that carries it; traditional fine art, calligraphy, music, dance, drama, quyi and acrobatics; traditional craft, medicine and calendars; traditional rites, festivals and other folk customs; traditional sport and games; and anything else.**

:::note The law and the list do not use the same drawers
Read the second and fifth headings again. In the statute, **acrobatics sits with the arts** and **sport sits with games**.

The national list is arranged differently. This site has already met one of its categories directly — [weiqi and Chinese chess are listed under category six, 传统体育、游艺与杂技](/culture/weiqi-and-chinese-chess/), which puts **sport, games and acrobatics together** in a single drawer the law does not have.

So the definition in the statute and the classification used to organise the register are two different schemes. If you are trying to work out why a particular practice is filed where it is, look at the list's own categories rather than at Article 2.
:::

## Four tiers, made by four different bodies

**Article 18** builds the register from the top. The **State Council** establishes the national list of representative items, for practices of major historical, literary, artistic or scientific value. **Provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities** establish their own lists for items of corresponding value in their territory. Below those, cities and counties run lists of their own.

The consequence is that "intangible cultural heritage" in China is a statement about **which register**, and the registers are not equivalent. A county-level item and a national one carry the same words in English and very different weight.

The national list is built in batches, and this site has already met two of them: seal engraving entered in **2006**, in the first batch, and [the board games in the second batch of 7 June 2008](/culture/weiqi-and-chinese-chess/), which ran to 510 items with 147 extension items to the first.

## The second list is of people

Now the part that has no equivalent in the tangible system, and it is the twenty-second time this site has found a status question already answered by a document.

China does not only list the practice. It recognises **代表性传承人** (*dàibiǎoxìng chuánchéngrén*, representative bearers) — named individuals who carry a listed item — and it does so under a ministerial instrument with conditions, obligations and an annual review.

The **国家级非物质文化遗产代表性传承人认定与管理办法** (Measures for the Recognition and Administration of National Representative Bearers of Intangible Cultural Heritage) were approved at the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's executive meeting on **12 November 2019**, issued as **Ministry Order No. 3**, and have been **in force since 1 March 2020**.

**Article 8** sets the conditions. A Chinese citizen may apply or be nominated who has long practised the transmission of the item and commands its knowledge and core techniques; who is representative in the field and influential in the area; who plays an important role in transmission and actively trains successors; and who is 爱国敬业，遵纪守法，德艺双馨 — patriotic and dedicated, law-abiding, and of both good character and high skill.

**Article 18** attaches four obligations: to carry out transmission activity and train successors, to keep the relevant objects and materials safely, to cooperate with the authorities in surveys of intangible heritage, and to take part in public-interest promotion.

**Article 20** makes it annual. The provincial culture and tourism department must assess each bearer's discharge of those obligations, and their use of the transmission subsidy, **by 30 June each year** for the year before.

:::warn A bearer title can be taken away, and the grounds are listed
This is the part that changes how you read the word "master". Article 22:

> 有下列情形之一的，经省级文化和旅游主管部门核实后，文化和旅游部取消国家级非物质文化遗产代表性传承人资格，并予以公布：（一）丧失中华人民共和国国籍的；（二）采取弄虚作假等不正当手段取得资格的；（三）无正当理由不履行义务，累计两次评估不合格的；（四）违反法律法规或者违背社会公德，造成重大不良社会影响的；（五）自愿放弃或者其他应当取消国家级非物质文化遗产代表性传承人资格的情形。

The ministry withdraws the title, and publishes the withdrawal, where the holder **loses Chinese nationality**; obtained the title by **fraud or other improper means**; **fails without good reason to discharge the obligations and fails the assessment twice in total**; **breaks the law or offends public morality in a way causing serious adverse social effect**; or gives it up voluntarily, or any other case where it should be withdrawn.

Two consecutive failures is the one to notice. **The title is not an honour conferred once; it is a qualification with an annual condition attached.**
:::

## What that means when you meet one

- **Ask which list, and which level.** "Intangible cultural heritage" alone does not distinguish a national item from a county one.
- **The bearer title is about a person, not a workshop.** It attaches to an individual and to a specific listed item, not to a shop, a brand or a family business — and a shop trading on a relative's title is making a weaker claim than it sounds.
- **A demonstration is not automatically the listed practice.** The register protects a transmitted practice; a paid performance of it in a scenic area may or may not be the thing that was listed, and the same distinction the site draws about [ticketed minority villages](/culture/chinas-ethnic-minorities/) applies here.
- **The register is public.** Items and bearers are published, which is what makes any of this checkable rather than atmospheric.

## The international layer, and where China stands

UNESCO's lists are a separate system, and several pages here already set out individual inscriptions — [seal engraving](/culture/seals-and-chops-in-china/), [kunqu and Peking opera](/culture/chinese-opera-and-performance/), [acupuncture](/culture/chinese-medicine-in-china/), [the Dong grand song](/destinations/guizhou/). Being on a Chinese list and being on a UNESCO one are different facts, and the domestic listing usually comes first.

The most recent addition is one every visitor meets. **春节——中国人庆祝传统新年的社会实践** — *Spring Festival, the social practices of the Chinese people in celebration of the traditional new year* — was inscribed on **4 December 2024**, at the 19th session of the intergovernmental committee in **Asunción, Paraguay**. [The festival itself has a page here](/culture/chinese-festivals-and-holidays/), and the inscription does not change a single thing about how it works — but it does explain why 2025 coverage of it suddenly used the phrase.

With that inscription the culture ministry put China's total across the UNESCO intangible heritage lists and register at **44 items**, describing the number as 总数居世界第一, the highest in the world. That was the position stated in December 2024.

## Checked for newer, and there is a draft that is not yet a rule

The 2011 law stands. On **27 June 2025** the Ministry of Culture and Tourism published a **revision draft for public comment** — a substantial one, moving from the current six chapters and forty-five articles to seven chapters and seventy, with a widened definition and expanded protective measures.

**A draft out for comment is not a rule.** I found no sign that it has been adopted, so what is in force is the 2011 law as it stands, together with the 2020 bearer measures. If the revision passes it will change the framework rather than the four tiers described here.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is intangible cultural heritage in China?

A statutory category, defined in Article 2 of the 2011 Law on Intangible Cultural Heritage: traditional cultural expressions handed down through generations and regarded as part of a people's heritage, plus the associated objects and places. The law groups them under six headings running from oral literature to folk customs, sport and games.

### How many levels of intangible heritage list does China have?

Four. The State Council makes the national list; provinces, autonomous regions and municipalities make provincial lists; cities and counties run their own below those. The same English phrase covers all four, so the level is the thing worth asking about.

### What is a Chinese intangible heritage bearer?

A named individual recognised as carrying a listed item — 代表性传承人 in Chinese. National bearers are recognised by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism under measures in force since 1 March 2020, which set conditions for recognition, four continuing obligations, and an annual assessment by the provincial authority.

### Can a Chinese heritage bearer lose the title?

Yes, and the grounds are listed. The ministry withdraws and publishes the withdrawal where the holder loses Chinese nationality, obtained the title improperly, fails the annual assessment twice in total without good reason, breaks the law or offends public morality in a way with serious adverse social effect, or gives it up.

### Is Chinese New Year on the UNESCO list?

Yes. Spring Festival, described as the social practices of the Chinese people in celebration of the traditional new year, was inscribed on 4 December 2024 at the intergovernmental committee's 19th session in Asunción, Paraguay. The inscription recognises the practice; it changes nothing about the holiday itself.

### Which country has the most UNESCO intangible heritage items?

When Spring Festival was inscribed in December 2024, the Chinese culture ministry put China's total across the UNESCO intangible heritage lists and register at 44 items and described that as the highest in the world. That is the position as the ministry stated it at that date.
