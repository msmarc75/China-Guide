Chinese massage is one of the genuine pleasures of a trip here, and after a day of walking the Forbidden City it is the correct decision. But this is the one category where a listicle would actively harm you: the venues are poorly documented, they open and close constantly, and some proportion of shopfronts under massage signage are selling something else. So rather than name places I cannot stand behind, here is how to read the street yourself.

## What the signs mean

| Sign | Pinyin | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 按摩 | ànmó | Massage, generic — the least informative sign |
| 足疗 | zúliáo | Foot treatment: soak, then deep foot and lower-leg work |
| 足浴 | zúyù | Foot bath, usually the same thing |
| 推拿 | tuīná | Medical manipulation, the clinical tradition |
| 盲人按摩 | mángrén ànmó | Blind masseur — see below, this is the good sign |
| 养生 / 保健 | yǎngshēng / bǎojiàn | Wellness, health care — ambiguous, read the rest of the frontage |

## The blind-masseur tradition is your best signal

Massage by blind practitioners is a serious, regulated profession in China, not a charitable gesture. Medical massage by blind practitioners requires a state qualification, and Beijing operates a formal certification regime for it — without the certificate a practitioner may not work in a medical setting at all.

Practically, **盲人按摩 on a sign is the strongest single indicator that a place is exactly what it says it is**, and the practitioners are frequently excellent.

The institutional expression of this is **北京按摩医院** — the Beijing Massage Hospital, founded in 1958, run by the China Disabled Persons' Federation as a non-profit, and upgraded to a tertiary Chinese medicine hospital in September 2024. Its Xicheng campus is at **北京市西城区宝产胡同7号**, reached from **Line 4, Ping'anli, exit A**. It is a hospital rather than a spa — you go for treatment, not ambience — but it is the least ambiguous massage address in the city.

## Reading a shopfront

**Signs it is legitimate:**

- Large clear windows, brightly lit inside, and you can see the treatment chairs from the street
- A printed price board on display, in Chinese, listing treatments by duration
- Staff in matching uniform, visible and working
- Mixed customers — women, couples, older people, families
- Inside a shopping centre, a hotel, or on a normal commercial street at ground level
- Normal daytime hours as well as evening

**Signs to walk past:**

- Pink or red lighting, curtained or frosted windows, nothing visible from outside
- No price list anywhere
- Someone standing outside trying to bring you in
- Open only late at night
- An unmarked upstairs room reached through a plain door
- Staff who are all young women with no apparent treatment role

:::warn If a "special service" is offered, leave immediately
Sex work is illegal in China and these venues are raided. A foreigner caught in a raid faces detention and deportation regardless of what they had actually come in for. Do not negotiate, do not explain — pay for anything you have already had, and go.
:::

## What a session is actually like

**Foot massage.** You stay fully dressed, roll up your trousers, and sit in a large reclining chair while your feet soak in a herbal bath. Then comes deep work on the feet and lower legs, often with shoulders and neck included. Frequently a shared room with a television on. Deeply relaxing and often genuinely painful in places — the tender spots are the point.

**Body massage.** You change into loose pyjama-style clothes provided by the venue. Tuina is firm, dry, and worked through the fabric rather than with oil. Expect thumbs, elbows and forearms.

You may be offered cupping (拔罐) or gua sha (刮痧) as extras. Both leave marks for several days. Decline if you have a beach or a wedding coming up.

**Say 轻一点** — *qīng yīdiǎn*, "lighter" — if it is too much. Nobody will be offended; the strength is meant to be adjusted.

## Practical notes

**No tipping.** Not expected, and not customary.

**Pay by phone.** Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted essentially everywhere.

**The zero-risk option is your hotel.** Any decent hotel has a spa or will book you into a reputable place nearby. You give up some authenticity and pay more; you eliminate the guesswork entirely.

**Membership cards.** Chains push prepaid cards hard, and this is the most-complained-about practice in the industry. Decline politely and pay for the single session — you are not coming back next week.
