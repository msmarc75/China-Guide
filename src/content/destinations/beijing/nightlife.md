Every Beijing bar list published in English is partly wrong by the time you read it. Venues here turn over fast, and a closed bar keeps its reviews online for years. The districts, on the other hand, have been the going-out districts for a decade or more, and they are where you should point the taxi.

## What each one is

**Sanlitun** is the international nightlife centre — the densest concentration of bars, clubs and late restaurants in the city, wrapped around the Taikoo Li malls and spilling toward the Workers' Stadium. Loudest, latest, most expensive, most foreign. The Line 17 station opened under the name Gongtiyuchang and made the area far easier to reach than it used to be.

**Shichahai** is bars around a lake, in courtyard buildings and along the water. Prettier than Sanlitun and considerably more touristy, with a heavy concentration of live-music-and-shouting venues near the Silver Ingot Bridge. Walk further from the bridge and it calms down quickly.

**Nanluoguxiang** is the restored hutong strip: busy, commercial, good for a wander and a drink rather than a night out. It runs 787 metres north to south and is packed most evenings.

**Wudaoying** and **Fangjia** are the two hutongs to head for if Sanlitun sounds exhausting. Small bars, craft beer, coffee that turns into wine after dark, courtyard spaces. Both run between Andingmennei Dajie and Yonghegong Dajie, a few minutes apart, and both are reached from Yonghegong station — so you can do the Lama Temple in the afternoon and stay in the area.

Fangjia also holds **方家胡同46号**, a former factory turned cultural and creative compound with performance and bar spaces inside it.

:::warn The bar and tea-house scam works precisely here
Near Sanlitun, Nanluoguxiang and Wangfujing, a friendly stranger practising their English may invite you to a tea ceremony, an art exhibition or a nearby bar. The bill at the end is enormous and the exit is blocked until it is paid. The defence is simple: never go to a venue chosen by someone who approached you in the street. Suggest somewhere yourself, or decline.
:::

## Practical notes

**The metro stops earlier than the bars do.** Beijing's last trains run well before Sanlitun empties. Plan on DiDi for the journey home and set the destination in the app before you leave, so the language question never arises.

**Pay by phone.** Alipay and WeChat Pay work in essentially every bar. Some smaller hutong places take nothing else.

**Live music is a real scene.** Beijing has the strongest independent music culture in China. Venues change names and addresses often enough that this page will not list them — check listings on the day rather than trusting anything written months earlier. Small rooms work like small rooms; it is only [above five thousand seats that the ticket is bound to your passport and a refund scheme becomes compulsory](/guides/concerts-in-china/).

**KTV is the local default.** For most Chinese friends a night out means private-room karaoke rather than a bar. You rent the room by the hour, order in drinks and food, and take turns. If you are invited, go — it is a better window into how people actually socialise than any bar in Sanlitun. [What happens inside, why the door has a window in it and the price list you are owed](/culture/ktv-in-china/) is worth five minutes before your first one.
