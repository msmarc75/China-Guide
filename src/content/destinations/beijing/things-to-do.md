An English name gets you nowhere with a Beijing taxi driver. "The Summer Palace" means nothing; 颐和园 means everything. The table below is built to be shown rather than read aloud — pass your phone across with the Chinese column visible and the problem disappears.

Every entry has been cross-checked against at least two independent sources, including Beijing municipal and district government pages and the venues' own official sites. Where a sight has several entrances, the gate named is the one the metro station actually serves.

## How to group them

Beijing punishes zig-zagging. These cluster naturally into days.

**The central axis.** The Forbidden City, Jingshan and the National Museum sit on one line and share a morning and afternoon. Enter the palace from the south at Tiananmen and leave by the north gate, then cross the road and climb Jingshan — the view back down over the golden roofs is the photograph you came for, and it takes twenty minutes.

**The lakes.** Beihai, Prince Gong's Mansion and the Drum and Bell Towers are all within walking distance of each other around the Shichahai lakes. Beihai North and Shichahai stations bracket the area. This is the most pleasant half-day in the city on foot.

**The temples.** The Lama Temple and the Confucius Temple are a few minutes apart, both off Yonghegong station. Do them together; separately they are an awkward trip north for one building.

**Standalone.** The Temple of Heaven is south and deserves its own morning. The Summer Palace is north-west and takes most of a day once you include the journey. 798 is east, and works as a half-day when you want a break from imperial China.

:::warn The Forbidden City needs booking ahead, with your passport
Tickets are sold online in advance and the daily allocation genuinely sells out in peak season. Your passport number is tied to the booking and the passport itself is checked at the gate. This is the single most common way visitors lose a day in Beijing.
:::

:::warn Tiananmen Square needs booking too, and this catches people
Entry to the square itself is free but is now by **advance real-name reservation, made one to seven days ahead** through the official Tiananmen Square booking platform, with entry at an assigned time and gate after an ID check. Turning up without one does not work.

The published list of accepted documents names mainland, Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan identity documents; it does not spell out the route for a foreign passport, and we have not been able to confirm one from a source we trust. Check the official platform before you build a morning around the square — and note that the Palace Museum and the National Museum are booked separately from it, so a booking for one is not a booking for the other.
:::

## The Great Wall is not a metro trip

Mutianyu is in Huairou district, well outside the city, and there is no rail link. You reach it by tour bus, a hired car, or the public bus-and-transfer route. Treat it as a full day out and back, not an afternoon.

It is the section most first-time visitors should pick: restored, dramatic, densely towered, and less crowded than Badaling. For the alternatives and how each one differs, see the [Great Wall guide](/destinations/great-wall-of-china/).

## Practical notes

**Mondays.** Many museums, the Forbidden City among them, close on Mondays. Build the week around that rather than discovering it at the gate.

**Carry your passport.** It is required to enter ticketed sights, not merely to buy the ticket.

**Parks open earlier than museums.** Jingshan, Beihai and the Temple of Heaven grounds open early, and the Temple of Heaven in particular is at its best first thing, when the surrounding park fills with people exercising, singing and playing cards. That hour is closer to the real Beijing than anything inside a ticket barrier.
