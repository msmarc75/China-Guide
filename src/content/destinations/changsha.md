Changsha is the capital of Hunan, a province with an outsized reputation for chilli, stubbornness and revolutionaries — and in raw heat [Hunan out-chillies Sichuan](/answers/what-is-the-difference-between-sichuan-and-hunan-spice/), which is not what most visitors expect. Foreign visitors have mostly used it as the airport for Zhangjiajie. That undersells it badly, for two quite different reasons.

## Mawangdui

In 1972, workers digging an air-raid shelter east of the city hit a Han dynasty tomb complex. What came out of it is among the most important archaeological finds ever made in China.

The occupant of tomb one, **Lady Dai (辛追)**, died around 168 BCE and emerged nearly two thousand years later with skin still soft, joints still flexible and blood still liquid in her veins — the best-preserved ancient body ever recovered anywhere, and preserved by a method still not fully understood. Alongside her came the **T-shaped silk funeral banner**, a painted cosmology two metres long in near-perfect condition, and a plain gauze robe so fine it weighs almost nothing. Tomb three produced silk manuscripts including the earliest known copies of several classics, centuries older than any version previously known.

All of it is in the provincial museum, and **Lady Dai herself is on display** in the Mawangdui galleries. Basic admission is free, but you must book in advance through the museum's own channel, and it closes on Mondays.

:::warn The museum has changed its name twice, and English sources are behind
This institution was **湖南省博物馆** (Hunan Provincial Museum), was renamed **湖南博物院** (Hunan Museum) on 30 July 2022, and was renamed **back to 湖南省博物馆** on 12 October 2025.

Both names are still in wide circulation, including on English travel sites, maps and booking pages. If a search for one comes up empty, try the other — it is the same building on Dongfeng Road, and there is only one.
:::

## An academy that never stopped

**Yuelu Academy (岳麓书院)** was founded in 976, is traditionally counted among the four great academies of imperial China, and became a protected national monument in 1988. That much is ordinary enough.

What is not ordinary is that it is still a working part of **Hunan University** — not a museum of education but a functioning faculty that enrols students and runs research. China calls it 千年学府, the thousand-year school. Walking through courtyards where teaching has continued, on and off but never abandoned, for over a millennium is the single most affecting thing in the city.

It sits at the foot of Yuelu Mountain across the river, and the metro station is named after it.

**Orange Isle (橘子洲)** is a long thin sandbar in the middle of the Xiang river, best known outside China for the enormous stone head of the young Mao Zedong at its southern end — Mao was a student in Changsha, and the island appears in his best-known poem. It is a pleasant walk and a good view back at the city skyline.

**Taiping Street (太平街)** is the restored old commercial lane in the centre, busy every evening and the easiest place to graze.

## Why young Chinese travellers come here

This is the part guidebooks miss. Over the past few years Changsha has become one of the most visited domestic destinations in China, and the reason is largely food and drink.

**Chayan Yuese (茶颜悦色)** is the tea chain that started it. It stayed deliberately concentrated in Hunan while rivals expanded nationwide — more than half its several hundred shops are still in the province — which turned drinking it into something you travel for. Queues outside the central branches are part of the experience and are not short.

**Super Wenheyou (超级文和友)**, on Xiangjiang Middle Road, is a restaurant built as a full-scale reconstruction of a 1980s Changsha tenement block, several storeys of peeling concrete, neon and crayfish. Its out-of-province branches did not survive — the Guangzhou one closed — which is telling: the format only ever worked in the city it is about. The original is still trading and still heaving.

And **stinky tofu (臭豆腐)** here is black, fermented, deep-fried and served with chilli and garlic sauce, and it is genuinely the local version rather than the one you have had elsewhere.

Hunan food is hot in a different way from Sichuan's — dry, sharp chilli heat without the numbing peppercorn. If you have been told Sichuan is the spicy province, Hunanese people will want a word.

## Getting there and around

Changsha is on the main north–south high-speed line and is straightforward to reach from Guangzhou, Wuhan or Beijing. It is also the usual rail connection for [Zhangjiajie](/destinations/zhangjiajie/), about two and a half hours away.

**Changsha has a metro**, currently lines 1 to 6, covering roughly 218 kilometres. Line 4 serves Yuelu Academy at Hunan University station (湖南大学站); line 2 serves Orange Isle at Juzizhou station (橘子洲站). There is also a **maglev line to Huanghua airport** — the S2, formerly the Changsha Maglev Express — which is a rare thing to have and worth taking simply because you can.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Changsha worth visiting?

Yes, for two nights. The Mawangdui collection in the provincial museum is world-class and not replicated anywhere, Yuelu Academy is a thousand-year-old school still in use, and the city has become China's most talked-about food destination. Most foreign visitors pass through it for Zhangjiajie without stopping, which is a waste.

### Can you see Lady Dai's body at the museum?

Yes. The remains from Mawangdui tomb one are displayed in the museum's Mawangdui galleries. Basic admission is free but requires advance booking through the museum's own channel, and the museum closes on Mondays.

### Is it called Hunan Provincial Museum or Hunan Museum?

Both names refer to the same institution. It was renamed to Hunan Museum (湖南博物院) in July 2022 and renamed back to Hunan Provincial Museum (湖南省博物馆) in October 2025, so English listings use either. There is only one museum, on Dongfeng Road in Kaifu district.

### Does Changsha have a metro?

Yes — lines 1 to 6 are in operation, plus the S2 maglev line out to Huanghua airport. Use line 4 for Yuelu Academy and line 2 for Orange Isle.

### Is Hunan food spicier than Sichuan food?

They are hot in different ways. Hunan heat is dry and direct chilli heat; Sichuan adds the numbing tingle of the Sichuan peppercorn. Many people find Hunan food the sharper of the two — [the fuller comparison](/answers/what-is-the-difference-between-sichuan-and-hunan-spice/) covers which is actually hotter and how to order around either.

### How many days do you need in Changsha?

Two nights. One day for the museum, one for Yuelu Academy and Orange Isle across the river, with the evenings spent eating.
