Chengdu is the capital of Sichuan, a city of 21 million in a fertile basin under near-permanent cloud, and the place most travellers say they wish they had given more days. It is slower than Beijing or Shanghai, has a genuine teahouse culture that people actually use, and possesses the most influential regional cuisine in China.

## How long you need

**Two nights is the minimum; three or four is where the city becomes worthwhile.** The pandas are a morning. The rest — teahouses, temples, food, and the day trips to Leshan and Mount Qingcheng — is what you stay for.

## Giant pandas

**Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding**, 10 km north of the centre. ¥55, opens 07:30.

**Arrive at opening.** This is not optional advice. Pandas are most active in the cool early morning during their first feed and are largely asleep by 10:00. By 09:30 the site is full of tour groups and you will be looking at a distant sleeping ball of fur over three rows of heads.

Take the metro to Panda Avenue station (line 3) plus the shuttle, or a DiDi (¥50, 30 min). Inside, use the electric shuttle (¥10) to reach the nursery area first, then walk back through the enclosures.

**Dujiangyan Panda Base**, 60 km away, is quieter and runs paid volunteer programmes (typically ¥200–700) where you help prepare food and clean enclosures. Note that keeper-hug photo opportunities have been discontinued across Chinese panda facilities.

**Red pandas** wander semi-freely at the Chengdu base and are, unpopular opinion, the better animal.

## Teahouses

Sichuan teahouse culture is not a performance for tourists — it is how people spend afternoons. Bamboo chairs, a lidded *gaiwan* cup topped up endlessly, mahjong, ear cleaning with long metal picks, and hours passing.

- **Heming Teahouse** in People's Park — the famous one, in a lakeside pavilion, ¥25–60 for a cup and unlimited refills. Touristy but genuinely full of locals.
- **Wenshu Monastery teahouse** — quieter, in the courtyard of a working Buddhist monastery, with excellent vegetarian food next door.
- **Qingyang Temple** — a Taoist temple with a beautiful, almost empty tea courtyard.

The **matchmaking corner** of People's Park, where parents advertise their unmarried adult children on laminated sheets pinned to umbrellas, is a fascinating twenty minutes.

## Food

Sichuan cuisine is built on *málà* (麻辣) — the numbing tingle of Sichuan peppercorn combined with the heat of chilli. It is not simply hot; the peppercorn produces a genuine physical vibration on the lips that most first-timers find bizarre and then addictive.

Beyond hotpot:

- **Mapo tofu** — silken tofu, fermented broad bean paste, minced beef, peppercorn. The best version in the world is in Chengdu, at Chen Mapo Tofu — the restaurant descended from the 19th-century stall where the dish was created, though the flagship no longer stands on the original Wanfu Bridge site.
- **Dan dan noodles** — small bowls, sesame paste, chilli oil, preserved vegetable, minced pork
- **Fuqi feipian** — sliced beef and offal in chilli oil, the classic cold starter
- **Zhong shui jiao** — dumplings in sweet garlic soy
- **Sweet water noodles** (tian shui mian) — thick chewy noodles in sweet-spicy sauce
- **Twice-cooked pork** (huiguorou) — the everyday Sichuan home dish
- **Kung pao chicken** — the real one, with peanuts, dried chillies and a light vinegar edge, unrecognisable from the Western version

**Hotpot** is the social institution. Choose a chain like Shu Daxia or Xiaolongkan for reliability. Order the *yuanyang* split pot if you want a non-spicy half — the spicy side is genuinely spicy, and the oil is rendered beef tallow. Dip in sesame oil with garlic, which cools the heat.

**Cooking classes** in Chengdu are among the best travel activities in China: a market visit, then learning mapo tofu and kung pao chicken properly. Several English-language schools operate near the centre.

## In the city

- **Wide and Narrow Alleys (Kuanzhai Xiangzi)** — restored Qing lanes, now a food and shopping district. Commercial but attractive, and the snack stalls are good.
- **Jinli Street** — the same idea next to the Wuhou Shrine; go at night for the lanterns.
- **Wuhou Shrine** (¥50) — dedicated to Zhuge Liang and the heroes of the Three Kingdoms, whose story every Chinese visitor knows in detail.
- **Jinsha Site Museum** (¥70) — a 3,000-year-old Shu civilisation site discovered in 2001, with a gold sun-bird disc that has become the emblem of Chinese cultural heritage. Excellent and under-visited.
- **Sichuan Opera and face-changing** (*bian lian*) — masks swapped in a fraction of a second by a technique kept secret. Shufeng Yayun theatre does the tourist-friendly version well.
- **People's Park** on a Sunday — dancing, singing, tai chi, calligraphy, the works.

## Getting around

Excellent bilingual metro, ¥2–8. Tianfu International Airport (TFU) is the newer and larger of the two, 50 km out; Shuangliu (CTU) is closer. Chengdu East railway station is the main rail hub: 3 hours to Xi'an, 1 hour to Chongqing, 7–8 hours to Beijing.

Chengdu is also the main jumping-off point for **Tibet** — flights to Lhasa take 2.5 hours, and most Tibet tour permits are processed through Chengdu agencies. See our [Tibet guide](/destinations/tibet-lhasa/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What time should I arrive at the Chengdu Panda Base?

At 07:30 opening. Pandas are active during their morning feed and mostly asleep by 10:00, and tour groups arrive from about 09:00. Arriving late is the single most common Chengdu mistake.

### How many days do you need in Chengdu?

Two nights minimum, three or four to enjoy it. The pandas take a morning; the teahouses, temples, food scene and day trips to Leshan and Mount Qingcheng are what justify the extra days.

### Is Sichuan food too spicy for foreigners?

Some of it is genuinely very hot, but Sichuan cuisine is far broader than its reputation — many classic dishes are mild, sweet or sour. In hotpot restaurants, order the split *yuanyang* pot so you have a non-spicy broth alongside the spicy one.

### Can you hold a panda in Chengdu?

No. Keeper photo and hugging opportunities have been discontinued at Chinese panda facilities. Volunteer programmes at Dujiangyan still let you help prepare food and clean enclosures, which is closer contact than any viewing platform offers.

### Is Chengdu a good base for Tibet?

Yes. Chengdu has frequent flights to Lhasa (2.5 hours) and is where many agencies process Tibet Travel Permits, which foreign visitors must obtain through a registered tour operator before boarding.
