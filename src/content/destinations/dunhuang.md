Dunhuang was the last Chinese town on the road west, the place where the Silk Road split around the Taklamakan and where merchants and monks stopped before the desert. A thousand years of that traffic paid for the painted caves that make it one of the great sites of Asia.

It is also a small oasis city in the far west of Gansu, a long way from anywhere, and getting there and into the caves takes more planning than most Chinese destinations. This page front-loads that.

## The Mogao Caves: how the tickets actually work

**The Mogao Caves (莫高窟)** were inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1987 — one of a small number of properties worldwide to meet all six cultural criteria. There are 735 caves cut into the cliff, of which 492 preserve wall paintings and sculpture: some 45,000 square metres of murals and more than 2,400 painted figures, made between roughly the fourth and fourteenth centuries.

Access is tightly rationed, and this is where trips go wrong.

**Book in advance, through the official channel only.** The Dunhuang Academy sells through its own booking site and its WeChat mini-programme and states that it has authorised no third-party resellers. Booking is **real-name**: you enter passport or ID details, and you must carry the same document to get in. Tickets are released for the next day up to 30 days ahead, with the new day's allocation going on sale each morning.

**There are two main ticket types, and they are not the same experience.**

| Ticket | What it includes | Daily limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal (参观正常票) | The Digital Exhibition Centre film, plus 8 caves with a guide | 6,000 |
| Emergency (应急票) | 4 caves. **No film** | 12,000 |

The emergency ticket exists to absorb peak-season overflow and is released when the Academy activates that mode. It is a genuine fallback if the normal tickets are gone — but understand that you are getting half the caves and none of the film.

:::warn You do not choose which caves you see
Visitors are put into guided groups and taken along a route the Academy sets on the day, based on which caves are open and how the crowds are running. You cannot request particular caves. In practice cave 96 — the nine-storey pavilion with the great seated Buddha — and cave 17, the Library Cave, are on most routes, but nothing is guaranteed.

**Photography and filming inside the caves are strictly prohibited.** This is enforced, not advisory.
:::

The **Library Cave (藏经洞)** is the one with the story. Sealed around the year 1000 and reopened in 1900, it held tens of thousands of manuscripts — including the Diamond Sutra of 868, the world's oldest dated printed book, now in the British Library. How that material left China is a live subject here, and worth reading about before you go rather than after.

## The station mistake

:::warn Liuyuannan is not Dunhuang
Two stations serve this area and they are not interchangeable. **Dunhuang station (敦煌站)** is roughly ten kilometres east of town. **Liuyuannan (柳园南站)**, the high-speed stop, is in a different county about **130 kilometres away**, across empty desert.

Booking software will happily sell you the Liuyuannan ticket because it is faster on paper. If you take it, budget for a two-hour road transfer at the other end. Check which station your ticket names before you buy.
:::

## How long you need

**Three nights is the comfortable answer, two if you are moving fast.** Mogao takes a half day and cannot be rushed. The dunes are an evening. The frontier passes are a long half day west into the desert, and are for people who want the Han frontier rather than another temple.

**Mingsha Shan and Crescent Lake (鸣沙山月牙泉)** are five kilometres south of town: a crescent-shaped spring-fed pool sitting in a bowl of enormous dunes that hum when sand slides down them. Go for the last hours of light.

**Yumen Pass (玉门关)** and **Yangguan (阳关)** were the two Han gateways west, and they are the reason a lot of Tang poetry is about leaving. What survives is rammed earth in open gravel desert, which is either deeply atmospheric or a pile of mud depending on what you brought to it.

:::warn Only one of the two passes is World Heritage
English writing treats Yumen Pass and Yangguan as a matched pair. In heritage terms they are not. The **Yumen Pass ruins** were inscribed in 2014 as part of the transnational Silk Roads: Chang'an–Tianshan Corridor listing, together with the **Xuanquanzhi (悬泉置遗址)** post-station site nearby. Yangguan is not among the inscribed sites. Both are still worth seeing — but if you have time for one and want the World Heritage site, it is Yumen.
:::

Note that the Mogao Caves are a World Heritage site in their own right from 1987, separately from the Silk Roads listing.

## Food

The **night market** is the evening. Lamb skewers, hand-pulled noodles, apricot juice pressed cold, and 驴肉黄面 — donkey meat with fine yellow noodles, which is the local speciality and better than it sounds to a squeamish reader. Dunhuang is small enough that the market is where everyone ends up.

## Getting there and around

**Dunhuang has no metro** and is far too small to need one; the town has only a handful of bus routes. Use taxis, and hire a car or join a tour for the desert sites, which are too far apart to reach otherwise.

Dunhuang has its own airport east of town with flights to Lanzhou, Xi'an, Beijing and Urumqi, and its own railway station with sleeper and fast services. Read the station warning above before booking rail.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to book Mogao Caves tickets in advance?

Yes. Tickets are sold only through the Dunhuang Academy's official booking site and WeChat mini-programme, are real-name — you enter ID or passport details and must carry that document — and are released up to 30 days ahead. There is no walk-up ticket office to rely on, and the Academy states it has authorised no third-party sellers. [Booking the Mogao Caves](/answers/do-i-need-to-book-mogao-caves-tickets-in-advance/) explains what a visit consists of and why you cannot pick your caves.

### What is the difference between the normal and emergency Mogao tickets?

The normal ticket includes the Digital Exhibition Centre film and a guided visit to eight caves, capped at 6,000 a day. The emergency ticket, released in peak season to handle overflow, covers four caves and does not include the film, capped at 12,000 a day.

### Can I choose which caves to see, or take photos?

No to both. Groups follow a route the Academy sets on the day depending on which caves are open, and photography and filming inside the caves are strictly prohibited.

### Does Dunhuang have a metro?

No. Dunhuang is a small oasis city with a handful of bus routes. Get around by taxi, and use a hired car or a tour for the desert sites.

### Which train station should I book for Dunhuang?

Dunhuang station (敦煌站), about ten kilometres from town. Liuyuannan (柳园南站) is a high-speed station roughly 130 kilometres away in a different county, and reaching town from there needs a long road transfer.

### How many days do you need in Dunhuang?

Two to three nights: half a day for Mogao, an evening for the dunes, and a half day for the frontier passes if the Han frontier interests you.
