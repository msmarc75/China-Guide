Guangzhou — Canton to the 19th-century traders — has been China's principal southern port since the Tang dynasty, and was for a long period the only port where foreigners were permitted to do business at all. That history produced the Cantonese diaspora, and therefore most of what the world outside China thinks "Chinese food" is.

The city itself is a sprawling subtropical megacity of 19 million. There are sights, and they are fine. The reason to come is the eating.

## Food

Cantonese cooking prizes freshness and restraint: steaming, quick stir-frying, clear broths, minimal spice, and an obsession with the natural flavour of the ingredient. It is the least aggressive of China's major cuisines and, done well, the most technically demanding.

**Yum cha and dim sum.** Morning tea is the institution. Go between 07:00 and 11:00. It is a three-hour morning rather than a meal, and [there is a per-person charge on the bill that has to be displayed](/food/yum-cha-in-china/).

- **Tao Tao Ju** and **Guangzhou Restaurant** — the old grand houses, busy and traditional
- **Bing Sheng** — modern, consistent, excellent
- **Dian Dou De** — reliable mid-range chain
- Order: har gow (prawn dumplings), siu mai, char siu bao, cheung fun (rice noodle rolls), lo mai gai (sticky rice in lotus leaf), egg tarts, phoenix claws

**Roast meats (siu mei).** Roast goose, char siu, crisp-skinned pork belly, soy chicken. Look for a shop with birds hanging in the window and a queue.

**Wonton noodles and congee.** Breakfast staples. Congee with preserved egg and pork, or fish slices.

**Seafood.** Steamed whole fish with ginger and spring onion is the benchmark dish of the cuisine.

**Late-night streets.** Wénmíng Lù (文明路) and the streets around Beijing Road for congee, sweet soups, barbecue and clay pot rice. Guangzhou's late-night streets turn over faster than most of this site's recommendations — Baoye Road, for years the standard answer to this question, had its stalls served notice to clear in early 2023 for a commercial redevelopment, so treat any list more than a year or two old as a starting point rather than a plan.

## Sights

**Shamian Island** — a sandbank leased to Britain and France after the Opium Wars, covered in colonial buildings, plane trees and quiet lanes. The most pleasant hour in Guangzhou.

**Chen Clan Ancestral Hall** (¥10) — an 1894 clan academy with extraordinarily elaborate wood, brick, stone and ceramic decoration. The best single building in the city.

**Nanyue King Museum** (¥12) — the tomb of a 2nd-century BCE king discovered in 1983, with a jade burial suit. Small, superb. Note that the institution was reorganised in September 2021: the old Museum of the Western Han Mausoleum of the Nanyue King merged with the Nanyue Palace Museum, and the combined body now has two separate sites — the tomb, on Jiefang North Road, and the palace ruins, on Beijing Road. Older listings name only one of them, so check which you are navigating to.

**Canton Tower** (¥150–350) — 604 m, with a bubble tram around the rim for the brave.

**Sacred Heart Cathedral** — an all-granite French Gothic cathedral from 1888.

**Yuexiu Park** — the Five Rams statue, the city symbol, and a surviving section of the Ming city wall.

**Liwan and Xiguan** — old Cantonese lanes, arcade houses and Enning Road, now partly gentrified but still the most characterful district.

## Practicalities

- **Shenzhen** is half an hour away and has [a guide of its own](/destinations/shenzhen/), which matters most if you are continuing to Hong Kong: seven passenger crossings are operating and only one of them stays open all night.
- **Getting there:** Guangzhou South is a major rail hub — 50 min to Hong Kong, 2h20 to Guilin, 8h to Beijing, 7h to Shanghai. Baiyun (CAN) is one of China's biggest airports.
- **Season:** October to December is the best — dry and mild. Summer is hot, humid and typhoon-prone; the rainy season runs April to September.
- **Days needed:** two nights for the food and the main sights.
- **Canton Fair:** twice a year (April–May and October–November), when hotel prices across the city double or triple and rooms become scarce. Check the dates before booking — and note that it runs in [three phases split by product category](/guides/business-travel-in-china/), so the week you want depends on your sector.
- **Language:** Cantonese is the local language, with Mandarin universally understood — one of the [places where Mandarin is the shared language rather than the first one](/answers/is-mandarin-spoken-everywhere-in-china/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Guangzhou worth visiting?

For food, unequivocally — it is the home of Cantonese cuisine and the best place on earth for dim sum and roast meats. As a sightseeing city it is modest, with Shamian Island and the Chen Clan Ancestral Hall as the standouts. Two nights is the right length.

### Where should I eat dim sum in Guangzhou?

Tao Tao Ju and Guangzhou Restaurant for the traditional grand-house experience, Bing Sheng for a modern and consistently excellent version, and Dian Dou De as a reliable mid-range chain. Go between 07:00 and 11:00, when yum cha actually happens.

### How do I get from Guangzhou to Hong Kong?

High-speed rail from Guangzhou South to Hong Kong West Kowloon takes about 50 minutes, with immigration handled inside the station. Note that Hong Kong is a separate immigration zone, so entering it uses a new entry against your Chinese visa — [what that means for your visa](/answers/how-do-i-get-from-guangzhou-to-hong-kong/) is the part worth checking before you book the return.

### What are the Kaiping diaolou?

Fortified multi-storey watchtowers built in the early 20th century by Chinese emigrants returning from North America and Southeast Asia, blending Chinese forms with Greek, Roman and Islamic elements. They are UNESCO-listed and about two hours from Guangzhou.

### When is the Canton Fair?

Twice a year, in April–May and October–November. During each session hotel prices across Guangzhou rise sharply and availability drops, so check the dates before booking travel.
