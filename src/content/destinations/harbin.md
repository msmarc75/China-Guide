Harbin grew out of the Chinese Eastern Railway, built by Russia in the 1890s, and for decades had a large Russian population — including tens of thousands of White Russian refugees after 1917. The architecture, the bread, the sausage and the vodka all survive. So does the cold: Harbin sits at the latitude of Vladivostok and Montreal, and January averages around −18°C.

## The Ice and Snow Festival

Officially the **Harbin International Ice and Snow Sculpture Festival**, running from late December to late February, and the largest event of its kind in the world. It has three main venues:

**Ice and Snow World (冰雪大世界)** — ¥330 in peak season. A purpose-built city of full-scale buildings — towers, palaces, temples, slides — carved from blocks cut out of the frozen Songhua River and lit from inside with coloured LEDs. It covers around 800,000 m². **Go at dusk**, arriving around 16:00, so you see it in daylight and then lit. It gets colder as the evening goes on; most people last two to three hours.

**Sun Island Snow Sculpture Art Expo** — ¥240. Monumental snow sculpture rather than ice, seen best in daylight.

**Zhaolin Park ice lanterns** — ¥150, smaller, older tradition, more intimate.

**Songhua River** — frozen solid, used as a public space: ice slides, horse carts, sledges and locals swimming through cut holes.

:::warn Dressing for −25°C
This is colder than most visitors have experienced. Thermal base layers, fleece mid-layer, a genuine down parka, insulated boots (not fashion boots), thick socks, a hat covering the ears, a scarf over the face and two pairs of gloves.

Phone batteries die within minutes in this cold — keep the phone against your body and carry a power bank in an inside pocket. Camera batteries the same. Hand warmers are sold everywhere in Harbin and are worth buying in quantity.
:::

## The Russian city

**Central Street (Zhongyang Dajie)** — 1.4 km of cobbles lined with Art Nouveau, Baroque and Renaissance-revival buildings from 1900–1930. Pedestrianised, and lined with bakeries selling Russian-style bread and Modern ice cream, which people eat outdoors in −20°C because that is the tradition.

**Saint Sophia Cathedral** — a Russian Orthodox cathedral from 1907, now a museum of the city's architecture. The most photographed building in northeast China.

**Jewish Harbin** — the city had the largest Jewish community in East Asia in the early 20th century, around 20,000 people. The Main Synagogue and the New Synagogue survive, the latter as a museum.

**Unit 731 Museum** — the site of Japan's biological warfare research facility, where thousands of prisoners were killed in experiments between 1935 and 1945. A serious, sober museum. Free, and a difficult but important visit.

## Outside winter

Harbin in summer is green, mild and pleasant — around 25°C when Beijing and Shanghai are unbearable — and is a domestic summer escape. There is no ice festival, but the river beaches, the Russian architecture and the food remain.

**Yabuli** (2.5 hours) is China's largest ski resort. **China Snow Town (Xuexiang)** is heavily commercialised but photogenic. **Changbaishan**, on the North Korean border in Jilin, has the Heaven Lake crater and good skiing.

## Food

Northeastern (Dongbei) cuisine is hearty and generous: **guobaorou** (sweet-and-sour fried pork, invented in Harbin for Russian diners), **di san xian** (potato, aubergine and pepper), **suancai** pickled cabbage stew, huge dumplings, and grilled skewers. Russian influence gives you **red sausage (hongchang)**, rye bread and *kvass*.

## Practicalities

- **Getting there:** 4h30 from Beijing by high-speed rail, or a 2-hour flight. Harbin Taiping (HRB) airport.
- **Days needed:** two or three nights for the festival and the city.
- **Book early.** Hotel prices in January double or triple, and the good ones sell out months ahead.
- **Getting around:** metro plus taxis. Walking outdoors between sights in January is limited by cold, not distance.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the Harbin Ice Festival?

It officially runs from late December to late February, with the main Ice and Snow World venue usually opening around 25 December and closing when the ice becomes unsafe in late February. Early to mid-January is the most reliable period.

### How cold is Harbin in winter?

January averages around −18°C with lows near −30°C. This requires genuine cold-weather clothing: thermal layers, a down parka, insulated boots and face covering. Phone and camera batteries fail within minutes outdoors.

### How much is Harbin Ice and Snow World?

Around ¥330 in peak season for the main Ice and Snow World venue. Sun Island's snow sculpture park is about ¥240 and Zhaolin Park's ice lanterns about ¥150. Combined tickets are sometimes available.

### Is Harbin worth visiting outside winter?

Yes, if modestly. Summer is mild and green — a domestic escape from southern heat — and the Russian architecture, Jewish heritage sites and Unit 731 museum are all year-round. But the ice festival is the main reason most people come.

### How do I get to Harbin from Beijing?

By high-speed rail in about 4 hours 30 minutes, or by air in roughly two hours. Rail is comfortable and drops you in the city centre.
