Huangshan is the mountain in the paintings. Granite peaks rising abruptly from a valley, pines growing horizontally out of rock faces, and the *yúnhǎi* — the sea of clouds — filling the valleys below the summits after rain. Chinese landscape art has been arguing with this mountain for twelve centuries.

## How to do it

**Two days with a night on the mountain** is the standard and correct approach. Day trips are possible and mean you miss both sunset and sunrise, which are the entire point — the [longer answer on how the two days actually break down](/answers/how-long-do-you-need-at-huangshan/) covers where the third day goes if you have one.

**Entry:** ¥190 (¥150 in winter). Cable cars ¥80–90 each way.

### Getting up

Three cable cars serve the mountain: **Yungu** (east, to the White Goose Ridge), **Yuping** (south, to Jade Screen), and **Taiping** (north). Most people go up Yungu and down Yuping, or vice versa.

**Walking up** takes about three hours by the eastern steps from Yungu or four hours by the western route — thousands of stone steps, well maintained, and genuinely hard on the knees. Everything on the mountain, including your bags if you pay for a porter, goes up by human labour on shoulder poles.

### The main routes on top

The summit area is a network of paved paths connecting viewpoints:

- **Bright Summit (Guangming Ding)**, 1,860 m — the central high point, and the second-highest on the mountain
- **Lotus Peak**, 1,864 m, the true summit, and **Celestial Capital Peak**, 1,810 m — the two famous climbs, closed on rotation for vegetation recovery. Note that Celestial Capital is the third of the three main peaks by height, not the second, despite being the one that looks hardest
- **Beginning-to-Believe Peak** — the classic pine-and-rock composition
- **Flying-over Rock** — a balanced boulder that appears on every poster
- **West Sea Grand Canyon** — the most spectacular section, a plank path cut into a cliff, with a monorail at the bottom. Allow 4–5 hours; check it is open, as it closes in winter and after heavy rain

### Sunrise

Stay in one of the summit hotels — Beihai, Xihai, Baiyun, Shilin — and walk out before dawn. **Book months ahead**; there are few rooms, they are expensive for what they are (¥800–1,500 for basic), and they sell out. Dormitory beds exist and are cheaper.

The sea of clouds is a weather event, not a guarantee. It is most likely after rain, in autumn and winter, and in the early morning.

:::tip What to bring up
It is 10–15°C colder on the summit than in the valley, and windy. Hotels rent thick coats. Bring water (prices triple on the mountain, because everything is carried up by hand), a torch for the pre-dawn walk, and shoes with grip — the granite steps are slick when wet.
:::

## The Huizhou villages

The valley below Huangshan was, in the Ming and Qing, the home of the Huizhou merchants, who grew rich on salt and tea and built white-walled, black-tiled villages that are now UNESCO World Heritage sites.

**Hongcun** (¥104) — laid out in the shape of an ox, with a water system running through every house, a crescent pond at the centre and a lake at the entrance. The opening bamboo-forest scenes of *Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon* were filmed nearby. Beautiful, and crowded with art students sketching. Stay overnight to have the lanes to yourself at dawn.

**Xidi** (¥104) — larger, older, more residential, slightly fewer visitors.

**Chengkan**, **Nanping** and **Tangmo** — smaller, less restored, almost empty.

**Tunxi Old Street** in Huangshan City — a well-preserved Song-era commercial street; a pleasant evening.

## Practicalities

- **Getting there:** Huangshan North railway station — 2h30 from Shanghai, 1h30 from Hangzhou, 3h from Nanjing, 6h from Beijing. From the station, buses run to Tangkou at the mountain foot (1 hour) and to Hongcun.
- **Season:** October and November for clarity and colour; April and May for green and mist. Winter snow on the pines is spectacular and the mountain stays open, with fewer visitors. Avoid public holidays entirely — the summit paths become one-way human queues.
- **Crowds:** this is one of China's most visited mountains. Weekdays outside holidays are dramatically better.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do you need at Huangshan?

Two days with one night in a summit hotel, which is what allows you to see both sunset and sunrise. Add a night below the mountain for Hongcun and Xidi, making a three-day trip from Shanghai or Hangzhou.

### Do you have to hike up Huangshan?

No. Three cable cars serve the mountain for ¥80–90 each way. Walking up takes three to four hours on stone steps and is hard on the knees; many visitors ride up and walk between summit viewpoints, which is where the scenery is anyway.

### Is it worth staying overnight on Huangshan?

Yes — sunrise and sunset from the summit are the reason to come, and day-trippers miss both. Summit hotels are basic and expensive at ¥800–1,500 a night and must be booked months in advance.

### When is the best time to visit Huangshan?

October and November for clear air and autumn colour, April and May for mist and green. Winter offers snow-covered pines and low crowds. Avoid Chinese public holidays, when the paths become congested to the point of gridlock.

### What is the sea of clouds at Huangshan?

A cloud inversion that fills the valleys below the summits while the peaks stand clear above it. It is most common after rain, in autumn and winter, and in the early morning — but it is weather, not a scheduled attraction.
