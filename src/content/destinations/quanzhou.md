For three centuries Quanzhou was the busiest port in the world. Arab, Persian and Indian merchants settled here; Marco Polo passed through and reported a harbour of extraordinary scale; and the city accumulated a religious landscape unlike anywhere else in China — Buddhist, Islamic, Hindu, Manichaean and Christian communities trading in the same few streets.

Then the harbour silted, the Ming turned inward, and Quanzhou spent six hundred years being quietly forgotten. In July 2021 UNESCO inscribed it as **Quanzhou: Emporium of the World in Song-Yuan China**, a serial property of twenty-two component sites and China's fifty-sixth World Heritage entry. Anglophone travel writing has still barely noticed.

## How long you need

**Two nights, three if you want the outlying sites.** The old town is compact and walkable; the components scattered across Jinjiang and Luojiang need a taxi and a half-day each.

**Kaiyuan Temple** is the centrepiece: founded in the Tang, twin stone pagodas, and — in the rear hall — a set of columns carved with Hindu motifs, salvaged from a Tamil temple that once stood in the city. That detail alone tells you what Quanzhou was.

**Qingjing Mosque** on Tumen Street dates to 1009 and is among the oldest surviving mosques in China, built in stone by merchants from Persia and the Arab world.

**Cao'an** is the one that should be famous. It holds **the only surviving Manichaean statue in the world** — a stone Mani carved in 1339, in a small hall on the southern slope of Mount Huabiao. Manichaeism was extinguished almost everywhere; this is the physical evidence that it reached the Chinese coast and survived there in hiding.

**Luoyang Bridge**, built 1053–1059, runs 834 metres on 46 stone piers across the river mouth and was China's first sea-crossing stone beam bridge. It is still walkable.

:::warn Quanzhou has no metro
Nothing on this list is reached by rail. The old town is genuinely walkable, tourist bus lines run through it, and everything outside the centre needs a taxi or a bus. Budget for that rather than assuming a network exists.
:::

## Food

Quanzhou is a UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy, and the cooking is Minnan — southern Fujian — rather than anything a visitor will recognise from Beijing or Sichuan. Oyster omelettes, peanut soup, ginger-duck, and a dense repertoire of noodle and seafood dishes eaten in small shopfronts rather than restaurants. It is one of the strongest food cities in China and the least written about.

## Getting there and around

High-speed rail from [Xiamen](/destinations/xiamen/) takes well under an hour, and from **Fuzhou** a little longer, which makes Quanzhou an easy addition to a Fujian trip rather than a special expedition. The station sits outside the old town; take a taxi in.

Pairing the two is the sensible way to do Fujian. Quanzhou was the great port of the Song and Yuan and Xiamen the one that mattered from the Ming onwards, so between them they cover most of Chinese maritime history — and Xiamen has the airport, the beaches and the World Heritage island, while Quanzhou has the older and stranger material.

Once there, walk. The old town's grid of stone lanes is the point, and the density of temples, mosques and merchant houses only registers on foot.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Quanzhou worth visiting?

If you have any interest in maritime history, religious architecture or Chinese cities that have not been rebuilt for tourism, yes. It offers a UNESCO World Heritage property with a fraction of the crowds at comparable sites, and an old town where ordinary life still happens around the monuments.

### How many days do you need in Quanzhou?

Two nights covers the old town properly. A third lets you reach Cao'an and Luoyang Bridge without rushing.

### Does Quanzhou have a metro?

No. The city has no metro system. The old town is walkable, tourist bus routes serve the centre, and outlying sites need a taxi.

### What is Quanzhou famous for?

Being the greatest port of Song and Yuan China — the eastern terminus of the maritime Silk Road — and for the religious diversity that trade produced, including the world's only surviving Manichaean statue.

### How do I get to Quanzhou?

By high-speed rail from [Xiamen](/destinations/xiamen/) or Fuzhou. Both are short hops, and Xiamen's airport is the usual international entry point for this part of Fujian.
