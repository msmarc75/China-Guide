Shenzhen turns up on twenty-two pages of this site and never once as a destination. It is the city named when the point is that indoor smoking bans are real somewhere, that a run of cities dropped their attraction bookings, that a hotel is unlikely to refuse a foreign passport, that Mandarin is understood but not spoken at home. In the Guangzhou guide it is a single row in a day-trips table.

That is a strange way to treat a first-tier city of the National Bureau of Statistics' own reckoning, twenty minutes by rail from Hong Kong, with the largest electronics market on earth in the middle of it.

## It was a legal act before it was a city

Start here, because it explains the shape of everything else.

On **26 August 1980**, the 15th session of the Standing Committee of the 5th National People's Congress passed the **广东省经济特区条例** (*Guǎngdōng Shěng jīngjì tèqū tiáolì*, Guangdong Province Special Economic Zone Regulations), and a special economic zone was marked out inside what was then Bao'an county. The area carved out was **327.5 square kilometres** — a fraction of the modern city.

The Guangzhou guide is right that the fishing-village story is a myth. What it does not say is the sharper thing: **the zone was a boundary, and the boundary was policed.** For thirty years Shenzhen had an internal line, with the special zone inside it and ordinary Guangdong outside, and people still say 关内 and 关外 — *guān nèi* and *guān wài*, inside and outside the gate — to mean the old central districts and the rest.

That ended by decree. **国函〔2010〕45号** (*guó hán*, State Council reply no. 45 of 2010), dated **27 May 2010** and effective **1 July 2010**, says it in one line:

> 同意将深圳经济特区范围扩大到深圳全市，将宝安、龙岗两区纳入特区范围。

**The Special Economic Zone is extended to the whole city, bringing the districts of Bao'an and Longgang inside it.** Two districts that had spent three decades outside the line were inside it overnight. If a Shenzhen conversation turns on *guānnèi* versus *guānwài*, that is what is being referred to, and it has been legally meaningless since 2010.

## The border, which is the practical part

Most visitors reach Shenzhen from Hong Kong, and this is where published guidance is least reliable, because crossings change their hours and some have been rebuilt.

These are the passenger crossings and their opening hours **as published by the Shenzhen Municipal Port Affairs Office**:

| Crossing | Hours |
| --- | --- |
| **Huanggang** (皇岗) | 00:00–24:00 |
| **Luohu** (罗湖) | 06:30–24:00 |
| **Shenzhen Bay** (深圳湾), passengers | 06:30–24:00 |
| **Futian** (福田) | 06:30–22:30 |
| **West Kowloon high-speed rail** | 06:30–23:30 |
| **Liantang** (莲塘) | 07:00–22:00 |
| **Wenjindu** (文锦渡) | 07:00–22:00 |

:::tip One crossing never closes
**Huanggang is the only land crossing open around the clock**, which makes it the answer to every late-night version of this question. Miss the last train at Luohu and Huanggang is what remains.

**Shatoujiao is listed as under reconstruction** rather than as operating, so ignore any older guidance that routes you through it. Shekou's cruise terminal runs its own hours for the ferries.
:::

Two warnings that have nothing to do with timetables. Crossing here is an **immigration crossing, not a domestic transfer** — the same point [the Hong Kong guide makes about your Chinese visa](/destinations/hong-kong/), and it consumes an entry. And the payment systems do not survive the walk: [an Octopus card buys nothing on this side](/answers/what-is-an-octopus-card-in-hong-kong/).

## What the city is actually for

Shenzhen has almost no old town, which frees it from the obligation to pretend. Its subjects are commerce, design and the outdoors.

**Huaqiangbei** is the reason most people come, and [this site already calls it the world's largest electronics market](/guides/shopping-in-china/) — components, repairs, phone parts, whole floors of one kind of connector. It is worth an hour as spectacle whether or not you buy anything.

**OCT and the museum district** in Nanshan hold the contemporary art and design cluster, converted from factory buildings. This is where Shenzhen's claim to be a design city is actually visible.

**The coast** is the part outsiders never expect. Dapeng peninsula, in the far east of the city, has beaches, hiking and the Ming-dynasty **Dapeng Fortress** — a walled garrison town that gives Shenzhen its literary name, Peng City. It is a long way from the centre and it is the day that makes the trip feel like more than shopping.

**Lianhuashan Park** gives the standard skyline view over the civic axis, free and open, and is the cheapest good photograph in the city.

**Museums and galleries.** Shenzhen was one of the cities that lifted reservation requirements across a large block of venues in 2024 — [133 of them at once, the widest release of any city](/guides/booking-chinese-attractions/) — so spontaneous museum-going works better here than in Beijing.

## Practicalities

- **Days needed:** one full day from Hong Kong; two nights if you want Dapeng and the coast.
- **Season:** October to December, as everywhere on this coast. Summer is hot, humid and inside the typhoon belt.
- **Language:** Mandarin, more than in Guangzhou. Shenzhen is a migrant city built by people from every province, and Mandarin is the shared language rather than the local one — the reverse of [the Cantonese-first pattern next door](/answers/is-mandarin-spoken-everywhere-in-china/).
- **Smoking:** this is one of the few Chinese cities where an indoor no-smoking sign means it, and [the rules reach e-cigarettes and some outdoor waiting areas](/guides/smoking-and-vaping-in-china/), which is unusually broad.
- **Getting there without Hong Kong:** Shenzhen North is thirty minutes from Guangzhou South by high-speed rail and on the main line to the rest of the country. Bao'an is the airport.
- **Hong Kong and Macau are separate jurisdictions.** Nothing on this page about Chinese rules describes either of them.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Shenzhen worth visiting?

For a day from Hong Kong, yes — it is twenty minutes away, and Huaqiangbei alone justifies the trip for anyone interested in how things are made. As a standalone destination it is thinner than Guangzhou or Beijing, because it has almost no pre-1980 fabric. Two nights covers the city and the Dapeng coast.

### Which border crossing should I use from Hong Kong to Shenzhen?

Luohu and Futian are the two the metro reaches most conveniently, and the West Kowloon high-speed rail crossing is the fastest overall. Huanggang is the one to know about, because it is the only land crossing open 00:00–24:00 and therefore the only option late at night. Hours are published by the Shenzhen Municipal Port Affairs Office and are worth rechecking, since crossings have been rebuilt and rescheduled repeatedly.

### Do I need a visa to go from Hong Kong to Shenzhen?

You need whatever mainland China requires of your nationality, because this is an international crossing rather than a domestic move. Entering Shenzhen from Hong Kong uses an entry against your Chinese visa or visa-free allowance, which is the same trap [that catches people going the other way](/answers/is-hong-kong-part-of-china-for-visa-purposes/).

### Was Shenzhen really a fishing village?

No. It was Bao'an county, with a population in the hundreds of thousands, and a special economic zone of 327.5 square kilometres was marked out inside it by a resolution of the National People's Congress Standing Committee on 26 August 1980. The zone was extended to cover the whole city on 1 July 2010.

### What does guannei and guanwai mean in Shenzhen?

Inside and outside the old special-zone boundary. Until 2010 the zone covered only part of the city and the line between them was policed, so locals distinguished the central districts from Bao'an and Longgang beyond. The State Council extended the zone citywide with effect from 1 July 2010, so the distinction is now habit rather than law.

### Is Huaqiangbei worth going to if I am not buying anything?

Yes, as spectacle. It is the largest electronics market in the world, arranged as floor after floor of specialist stalls, and walking it tells you more about global manufacturing in an hour than any museum. Buying is a different exercise, and the general cautions about markets and bargaining apply.
