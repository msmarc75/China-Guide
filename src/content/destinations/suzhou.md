Suzhou grew rich on silk and canals, and its wealthy retired officials spent that money building gardens. Between the 11th and 19th centuries they produced the most refined tradition of private garden design in the world: compressed landscapes where a courtyard of 2,000 m² is made to feel like a mountain range through borrowed views, framed windows, rocks standing in for peaks and water for lakes.

Nine of them are UNESCO World Heritage sites.

## Which garden to visit

**Do not attempt five.** Garden fatigue is real, and the design vocabulary is subtle enough that the third one in a day stops registering. Pick two — one large, one small.

| Garden | Size | Ticket | Character |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Humble Administrator's Garden** (拙政园) | Largest, 5 ha | ¥70–90 | The famous one; water-dominated, magnificent, crowded |
| **Lingering Garden** (留园) | Medium | ¥45–55 | Best architecture and rockery; excellent |
| **Master of the Nets Garden** (网师园) | Tiny | ¥30–40 | The most perfect. Go at opening or for the evening opera performances |
| **Lion Grove Garden** (狮子林) | Medium | ¥30–40 | A maze of bizarre Taihu limestone rockeries; children love it |
| **Canglang Pavilion** (沧浪亭) | Medium | ¥15–20 | Oldest (11th c.), quietest, least restored |
| **Garden of Cultivation** (艺圃) | Small | ¥10 | Almost no visitors; a local teahouse in the corner |

**The best combination:** Humble Administrator's Garden at 08:00 opening, then the Master of the Nets or the Garden of Cultivation in the late afternoon.

**Go at opening.** The gardens were designed for one family and a few guests; at 14:00 on a Saturday they hold two thousand people and the entire point collapses.

## Canals and old streets

**Pingjiang Road** — an 800-year-old canal street, restored, with teahouses, small museums and pingtan storytelling houses. Busy but genuinely lovely, especially at either end away from the middle.

**Shantang Street** — longer, less polished, better at night when the lanterns reflect in the water.

**Boat rides** on the canals (¥45–120) are pleasant and short.

**Suzhou Museum** — designed by I. M. Pei, who was from a Suzhou family, and completed in 2006. The building is a modern abstraction of a Suzhou garden and is arguably the best contemporary museum architecture in China. Free, book ahead, closed Mondays.

## Silk

Suzhou has produced silk for over 2,000 years. The **Suzhou Silk Museum** (free) covers the history and shows live silkworms and reeling. For buying, the museum shop and established shops on Pingjiang Road are more reliable than street stalls. Double-layer silk quilts are the local speciality and are genuinely excellent, if awkward to carry.

## Tiger Hill and beyond

**Tiger Hill (Huqiu)** — ¥60–80, a hill with the tomb of the king of Wu, a 47 m leaning pagoda from 961 CE, and gardens. On the edge of town; worth half a day.

**Tongli**, **Zhouzhuang** and **Luzhi** — water towns within an hour, prettier and older than Zhujiajiao near Shanghai. Tongli is the best balance of preserved and not-overrun, and contains the Retreat and Reflection Garden, itself UNESCO-listed.

## Practicalities

- **From Shanghai:** 25–30 minutes by high-speed rail from Hongqiao, ¥40, trains every few minutes. This is why Suzhou is the best day trip in China.
- **Days needed:** a long day trip works. One night is better, because it lets you take a garden at opening and see Pingjiang Road at night.
- **Getting around:** metro plus shared bikes; the old town is flat and compact.
- **Best season:** April to May (wisteria and peonies) and October to November. The gardens are designed to look good in every season, including snow.

The gardens repay knowing what they are doing — borrowed views, framed openings, lattice windows and deliberately blocked sightlines are a designed vocabulary, set out in [Chinese art and architecture](/culture/chinese-art-and-architecture/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Suzhou garden is the best?

The Humble Administrator's Garden is the largest and most famous; the Master of the Nets is the most perfectly composed. Visit two rather than five — the design vocabulary is subtle and garden fatigue sets in quickly.

### Is Suzhou a good day trip from Shanghai?

It is the best day trip in China. High-speed trains take 25 minutes and cost about ¥40, running every few minutes. An overnight is better still, since the gardens are transformed by arriving at opening time.

### How much do Suzhou gardens cost?

Between ¥10 and ¥90 each depending on size and season — the Humble Administrator's Garden is ¥70–90 in high season, the Master of the Nets ¥30–40, and the small Garden of Cultivation ¥10.

### What is Suzhou famous for?

Its nine UNESCO-listed classical gardens, its canals, and silk production going back more than two thousand years. It is also the home of Suzhou embroidery and pingtan storytelling.

### Is Suzhou worth staying overnight?

Yes, if you can. Staying lets you enter a garden at 08:00 opening before the tour groups, and see Pingjiang Road and Shantang Street lit at night — both are considerably better outside day-trip hours.
