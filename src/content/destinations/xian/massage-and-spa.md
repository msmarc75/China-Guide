Fourteen kilometres of city wall, the Muslim Quarter on foot and a day standing in front of the Terracotta Army add up to legs that have earned an hour in a reclining chair. Xi'an obliges — massage shopfronts are everywhere in the walled centre. The difficulty is that they are undocumented, they change hands constantly, and a proportion of premises using massage signage are selling something else entirely. Naming individual shops would be guesswork dressed as a recommendation.

## Reading the sign

| Sign | Pinyin | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 推拿 | tuīná | The clinical tradition — manipulation, closer to physiotherapy |
| 足疗 | zúliáo | Foot treatment: hot soak, then deep work on feet and calves |
| 足浴 | zúyù | Foot bath, in practice the same thing |
| 按摩 | ànmó | Massage with no further detail — the least informative sign |
| 盲人按摩 | mángrén ànmó | Blind masseur — the sign that tells you most |
| 养生 / 保健 | yǎngshēng / bǎojiàn | Wellness or health care — judge on everything else |

## Why blind-masseur signage is the useful signal

Blind medical massage in China runs inside a national licensing framework administered by the China Disabled Persons' Federation. A practitioner sits a national examination, and the resulting certificate is the legal document permitting them to practise medical massage in a medical setting. Holders are required to undertake continuing education to keep it.

That matters to you as a shorthand. A business advertising licensed blind masseurs has entered a regulatory system, submitted to examination and record-keeping, and has no reason to do any of that if its actual trade is something else. It is not a guarantee, but it is the strongest single indicator available from the pavement — and the practitioners are often genuinely skilled.

For treatment rather than relaxation, the **Shaanxi Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine** runs a tuina and rehabilitation department. Sources disagree on its street number and even its district — some say Xihuamen 2, others Xihuamen 4, some place it in Beilin and others in Lianhu — so the reliable description is the one they all share: **Xihuamen, immediately north of the Bell Tower**, which puts it at Zhonglou station on Lines 2 and 6. Ask for 推拿科.

## Judging a shopfront

**Reassuring:** windows you can see through, lights on, treatment chairs visible from the street; a printed list of treatments and durations on display; staff in uniform actually working; a mixed clientele including women, couples and older customers; a ground-floor unit on a commercial street, or premises inside a hotel or mall; open during the day as well as the evening.

**Walk on:** tinted or curtained glass and low red lighting; no treatment list visible anywhere; someone posted outside inviting passers-by in; an unmarked door with stairs and nothing identifiable above; staff consisting entirely of young women with no apparent treatment role; premises that open only after dark.

:::warn If anything beyond massage is offered, leave
Sex work is illegal in China and these premises are raided periodically. A foreign passport present during a raid means detention and probable deportation, whatever you actually came in for. Pay for anything already received and go, without discussion.
:::

## What the session is like

**Foot treatment.** You stay dressed, roll your trousers up and sit back while your feet soak. The therapist then works feet, ankles and calves, usually finishing with shoulders and neck. Expect a shared room and a television. Expect it to hurt in specific places — that is the intent, not a mistake.

**Tuina.** You change into loose cotton clothes the shop supplies. The work is dry and firm, delivered through the fabric with thumbs, elbows and forearms rather than oil.

Cupping (拔罐) and gua sha (刮痧) will often be offered as additions. Both mark the skin visibly for several days.

The phrase for less pressure is **轻一点** (*qīng yīdiǎn*), and asking is unremarkable.

## Practical points

**No tipping.** It is not the custom and it is not expected.

**Pay with your phone.** Alipay and WeChat Pay are accepted essentially everywhere, including small independents.

**Your hotel removes the guesswork.** Reception will book somewhere they use, which costs more and eliminates the judgement call entirely.

**Decline the prepaid card.** Chains sell memberships hard. You are not returning next month; pay for the single session.
