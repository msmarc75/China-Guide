Xi'an eats wheat, lamb and chilli — the food of the north-west rather than the rice-and-river cooking most visitors arrive expecting. Almost everything worth eating is within a short walk of the Bell Tower, and the institutions below have been feeding the city for decades.

## What to order, and how to eat it

**Lamb paomo (羊肉泡馍)** is the city's defining dish and the one with a technique attached. You are handed a dense flatbread and an empty bowl, and **you tear the bread up yourself** — into pieces the size of a fingernail, smaller than feels reasonable. The kitchen then cooks it in lamb broth. Tearing well takes fifteen minutes and locals judge you on it. Tongshengxiang and Lao Sun Jia are the two names for this; Tongshengxiang's technique is on the national intangible heritage list.

**Hulutou (葫芦头)** is the same idea with pork intestine instead of lamb. Chunfasheng has been the address for it since 1920. Better than it sounds, and a genuinely local order.

**Roujiamo (肉夹馍)** is chopped braised pork in a crisp flatbread — the sandwich China had first. Fan Ji, on Zhubashi, is the century-old name.

**The dumpling banquet (饺子宴)** at Defachang, founded 1936, runs to dozens of shapes and fillings served in sequence. Touristy, and still a genuine Xi'an institution.

**Soup dumplings (灌汤包)** at Jia San in the Muslim Quarter — a halal version of the Jiangnan dish, and the quarter's best-known name. It appears in the Ministry of Commerce's register of time-honoured brands.

**Crisp fried beef (小酥肉)** at Ding Jia on Dapiyuan — a halal single-dish shop that has done one thing for decades. The dish is pork elsewhere in China; inside the Muslim Quarter it is beef.

## Practical notes

**The Muslim Quarter is halal.** No pork and no alcohol inside it. That is why the two pork dishes above — Fan Ji's roujiamo and Chunfasheng's hulutou — are at addresses south of the quarter rather than in it, and why Ding Jia's crisp meat is beef. Do not order what the kitchen does not serve.

**Match the characters.** The famous names have imitators trading beside them, particularly along Beiyuanmen. Compare the Chinese in the table against the sign.

**Breakfast is a real meal here.** Paomo is eaten in the morning as readily as at night.
