[The page on leaving food on your plate](/answers/is-it-rude-to-leave-food-on-your-plate-in-china/) describes the Clean Plate Campaign as a decade-long push with cultural traction, which is true. What it does not say is that the campaign acquired a statute, and that the statute changes what the restaurant around you is required to do.

## One law, in force the day it was published

The instrument is the **中华人民共和国反食品浪费法** (*Zhōnghuá Rénmín Gònghéguó fǎn shípǐn làngfèi fǎ*, Anti-Food Waste Law of the People's Republic of China), passed by the Standing Committee of the National People's Congress on **29 April 2021** and **in force from the day of publication** — the same day it was passed.

It begins by defining what it is against, which is narrower than "throwing food away":

> 本法所称食品浪费，是指对可安全食用或者饮用的食品未能按照其功能目的合理利用，包括废弃、因不合理利用导致食品数量减少或者质量下降等。

**Food waste** means failing to use, according to its purpose and reasonably, food that is **safe to eat or drink** — including discarding it, and including a loss of quantity or quality caused by using it unreasonably. Food that was never safe to eat is a different problem under a different law.

## Article 7 is the one you will sit inside

:::warn Five duties on the restaurant, and a prohibition at the end
> 餐饮服务经营者应当采取下列措施，防止食品浪费：（一）建立健全食品采购、储存、加工管理制度，加强服务人员职业培训，将珍惜粮食、反对浪费纳入培训内容；（二）主动对消费者进行防止食品浪费提示提醒，在醒目位置张贴或者摆放反食品浪费标识，或者由服务人员提示说明，引导消费者按需适量点餐；（三）提升餐饮供给质量，按照标准规范制作食品，合理确定数量、分量，提供小份餐等不同规格选择；（四）提供团体用餐服务的，应当将防止食品浪费理念纳入菜单设计，按照用餐人数合理配置菜品、主食；（五）提供自助餐服务的，应当主动告知消费规则和防止食品浪费要求，提供不同规格餐具，提醒消费者适量取餐。餐饮服务经营者不得诱导、误导消费者超量点餐。

1. **Systems and training.** Purchasing, storage and processing systems, with respect for grain and opposition to waste written into staff training.
2. **The sign.** Actively remind customers, and **post or place an anti-food-waste notice in a prominent position** — or have staff say it out loud — guiding customers to order what they need.
3. **Small portions.** Determine quantity and portion size reasonably, and **offer small-portion dishes among different sizes**.
4. **Group meals.** Design the menu around the number of people eating.
5. **Buffets.** Tell customers the rules and the anti-waste requirements up front, provide **crockery in different sizes**, and remind them to take a sensible amount.

And then the closing line, which is the one with a penalty behind it: **餐饮服务经营者不得诱导、误导消费者超量点餐** — a caterer **may not induce or mislead a customer into over-ordering**.
:::

The small-portion duty is worth pausing on, because this site has already met the same idea in another instrument. [The yum cha page quotes Article 13 of the catering measures](/food/yum-cha-in-china/) on small portions, multiple sizes and marking the suggested number of diners on the menu. That is the ministry's version; this is the legislature's. When you see a Chinese menu with a portion weight and a "serves 2–3" against a dish, you are looking at a drafting idea that exists at two levels of the system at once.

## The clause that can cost you money

The article does not stop at duties. It also gives the caterer two discretions, and the second is the one visitors never expect:

> 餐饮服务经营者可以对参与"光盘行动"的消费者给予奖励

> 也可以对造成明显浪费的消费者收取处理厨余垃圾的相应费用

A caterer **may reward customers who take part in the "clean plate action"** — and **may charge a customer who causes obvious waste the corresponding cost of dealing with the kitchen waste**.

Both are 可以, *may*: a power, not a duty. Most restaurants use neither. But a buffet that tells you at the door what happens if you leave a loaded plate is not improvising a house rule; it is exercising a discretion the statute names.

## The penalties point the other way

:::key Same article, opposite directions
Read the enforcement provisions and the asymmetry is complete.

**The restaurant is the one that gets fined.** Where a caterer induces or misleads a customer into over-ordering and **obvious waste results**, the market regulation department orders correction and gives a warning; on refusal to correct, a fine of **1,000 to 10,000 yuan**.

**The diner is not fined at all.** Nothing in the articles read here penalises a customer for wasting food. The customer-facing consequence is the caterer's discretionary charge above — a commercial bill, not a public penalty.

The shape of it is worth stating plainly, because it is the reverse of what the headline suggests: the public penalty falls on the business for pushing food on you, and the diner is left to the restaurant's own terms.
:::

## The provision that surprised everyone

Article 30 reaches out of the dining room entirely:

> 违反本法规定，广播电台、电视台、网络音视频服务提供者制作、发布、传播宣扬量大多吃、暴饮暴食等浪费食品的节目或者音视频信息的，由广播电视、网信等部门按照各自职责责令改正，给予警告；拒不改正或者情节严重的，处一万元以上十万元以下罚款。

Radio stations, television stations and online audio-video service providers that **make, publish or spread programmes or audio-video content promoting eating huge quantities, binge-eating and similar food waste** are ordered to correct and warned; on refusal to correct or in serious cases, a fine of **10,000 to 100,000 yuan**.

That is the competitive-eating video genre, addressed by name in a national statute. It is also a useful reminder of the register the law is written in: this is not a nudge document.

## What the departments added

Implementation was fleshed out by a joint 公告 — **Announcement No. 20 of 2021**, dated **11 June 2021** and issued by **five bodies**: the State Council Food Safety Office, the Ministry of Education, the Ministry of Commerce, the State Administration for Market Regulation and the Government Offices Administration.

| Its requirements on caterers | In short |
| --- | --- |
| 将反对食品浪费纳入从业人员培训内容 | Waste in the staff training |
| 合理确定数量、分量，提供小份餐等不同规格选择 | Portions and small dishes |
| 提供公勺公筷，做好打包服务 | Serving spoons, and boxing up |
| 主动对消费者进行防止餐饮浪费提醒 | Remind the customer |

The third row matters at a table. **提供公勺公筷** — serving spoons and communal chopsticks — and **做好打包服务**, doing the boxing-up properly, are named as things a caterer is to provide. [Asking to 打包 (*dǎ bāo*) is already normal](/food/how-to-order-food-in-china/); what this adds is that the restaurant is expected to be set up for it.

The announcement also tells **delivery platforms** to prompt customers, **以显著方式**, in a conspicuous manner, to order a sensible amount and consume rationally — so a prompt of that kind on a Chinese ordering app is a required feature rather than a design choice.

## In practice

- **Look for the notice.** An anti-food-waste sign in a prominent position is a legal requirement, not decoration, and its absence is a compliance gap you can see.
- **Ask for the small portion.** 小份 (*xiǎo fèn*) is a size the law tells the caterer to offer, so asking for it is asking for something that should exist.
- **Use the suggested headcount on the menu.** Where a dish carries a portion weight or a "serves 2–3", that line is there because two instruments encourage it.
- **At a buffet, listen to the briefing.** Buffets are singled out: the rules and the waste requirements are to be told to you up front, which is the moment any charge for waste would be disclosed.
- **Being pushed to order more is the restaurant's risk, not yours.** Inducing over-ordering is the conduct the fine attaches to.
- **Box up what is left.** [Taking leftovers home carries no stigma](/answers/is-it-rude-to-leave-food-on-your-plate-in-china/), and the boxing service is something the departments expect the caterer to run properly.
- **Hong Kong and Macau legislate this separately**, so neither the law nor the announcement describes a meal eaten there.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it illegal to waste food in China?

Not for the diner. The Anti-Food Waste Law, in force since 29 April 2021, places duties and penalties on caterers and on media, not on customers. The one customer-facing consequence is that a caterer may charge for the cost of handling kitchen waste where a customer causes obvious waste — a commercial charge rather than a fine.

### Can a Chinese restaurant charge you for leftovers?

It may. Article 7 gives caterers a discretion to charge a customer who causes obvious waste the corresponding cost of dealing with the kitchen waste, and a matching discretion to reward customers who clean their plates. Both are powers rather than duties, and most restaurants use neither.

### What is the Clean Plate Campaign?

A long-running public campaign against food waste that acquired legal backing in 2021. The Anti-Food Waste Law names the "clean plate action" expressly, as something a caterer may reward, and turns much of the campaign's message into duties on restaurants.

### Are mukbang videos banned in China?

Content promoting eating huge quantities or binge-eating is addressed directly by Article 30 of the law. Broadcasters and online audio-video services that make, publish or spread it are ordered to correct and warned, with a fine of 10,000 to 100,000 yuan for refusing to correct or in serious cases.

### Do Chinese restaurants have to offer small portions?

The law tells caterers to determine quantity and portion size reasonably and to offer small-portion dishes among different sizes. It is drafted as a duty on the caterer rather than as a right you can enforce, but it means asking for 小份 is asking for something the rules expect to be there.

### Does the food waste law apply to buffets?

Yes, and buffets get their own sub-item. A caterer providing self-service must tell customers the rules and the anti-waste requirements up front, provide crockery in different sizes, and remind customers to take a sensible amount.
