[The mistakes page](/plan/china-travel-mistakes/) puts it bluntly: **traffic is the most likely way a tourist gets hurt in China**, and electric scooters are silent, fast, unlit at night and ride on pavements and against the flow. [The safety answer](/answers/is-china-safe-for-tourists/) says the same. And then two other pages cheerfully suggest renting one for the day.

Both halves are right, and the gap between them is that nobody has said what the rules are. There are two, they sit in different instruments, and they carry different numbers.

## The rider: fifteen kilometres an hour

The law is the **中华人民共和国道路交通安全法** (*Zhōnghuá Rénmín Gònghéguó dàolù jiāotōng ānquán fǎ*, Road Traffic Safety Law of the People's Republic of China), passed on 28 October 2003, amended in 2007 and 2011 and revised on 29 April 2021.

Article 57 places you:

> 驾驶非机动车在道路上行驶应当遵守有关交通安全的规定。非机动车应当在非机动车道内行驶；在没有非机动车道的道路上，应当靠车行道的右侧行驶。

A non-motor vehicle **must be ridden in the non-motor lane**; on a road that has none, it keeps to **the right-hand side of the carriageway**.

Article 58 is one sentence, and it is the number worth carrying:

> 残疾人机动轮椅车、电动自行车在非机动车道内行驶时，最高时速不得超过十五公里。

A disabled person's motorised wheelchair or an **electric bicycle**, when ridden in the non-motor lane, **may not exceed fifteen kilometres an hour**.

:::warn Fifteen is the rider's limit, and it is not the machine's
This is the distinction that catches people, including plenty of riders.

The **rider** is capped by statute at **15 km/h** in the lane where the rider is supposed to be. The **machine** is a separate question, governed by a national product standard rather than by the traffic law — a rented e-bike that will comfortably do more than fifteen is not evidence that fifteen is not the rule.

So the phrase "silent, fast and everywhere" that this site has used three times describes a traffic reality, not a legal permission. If you rent one, the legal speed in the cycle lane is a number most riders around you are exceeding.
:::

## The machine: a compulsory standard that changed twice in 2025

The e-bike itself answers to **GB 17761-2024**, 《电动自行车安全技术规范》, *Safety technical specification for electric bicycle*. The record on the national standards platform gives it as published **31 December 2024** and in force from **1 September 2025**, administered by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology.

A municipal market regulator's notice describes it in the terms that matter:

> 2024年12月31日发布了新修订的强制性国家标准《电动自行车安全技术规范》（GB 17761-2024），新标准将于2025年9月1日起实施

A **强制性国家标准** — a **compulsory** national standard, the category [this site sets out elsewhere as the one that binds rather than advises](/food/chinese-wine/). The same notice records that it **全部替代了GB 17761-2018**, wholly replaces the 2018 standard, and a provincial standards office adds that the 2018 version **将废止**, will be abolished.

:::key One standard, two dates, two people
A provincial industry and information technology department states the commencement in a way the standard record does not:

> 《电动自行车安全技术规范》设定了两个实施时间，其中生产环节实施时间是今年9月1日，销售环节实施时间是今年12月1日

**Two implementation dates.** The **production** stage from **1 September**, the **sales** stage from **1 December** — the page is dated 2025 and the standard's own record gives 1 September 2025 as its implementation date, so those are the 2025 dates. The manufacturer and the shop come under the new standard three months apart, which means a machine made before 1 September could still be sold until 1 December.

For a visitor the practical reading is narrow but real: an e-bike offered for hire is a machine of some age, and the standard it was built to depends on when it was made rather than on when you sat on it.
:::

One change is quotable and concrete. The same municipal notice records that the standard **将使用铅蓄电池的电动自行车整车质量上限由55kg提升到63kg** — the whole-vehicle mass ceiling for an e-bike using **lead-acid batteries** was raised from **55 kg to 63 kg**.

## Where you may not put it

The one e-bike rule this site has already documented is about the building rather than the road. [Article 37 of the rules on fire safety in high-rise civil buildings bars parking an electric bicycle, or charging one, in the public lobby, evacuation corridors, stairwells and exits](/guides/hotel-fire-safety-in-china/) — which is why charging cabinets stand outside Chinese apartment blocks and hotels rather than bikes standing inside.

That rule is about batteries, not about riding, and it applies to the building you are sleeping in.

## What these texts do not say

Two absences are worth stating rather than filling in.

**Neither article read here deals with helmets.** The traffic law provisions above place the vehicle in a lane and cap its speed; they say nothing about what a rider must wear. What a particular city requires is a separate question, and this page does not answer it city by city.

**The design-speed figure is not on this page.** The number usually quoted for a Chinese e-bike's maximum design speed belongs to the product standard rather than the traffic law, and the standard record published on the national platform carries the dates, the administering ministry and the status but not the technical clauses. Several official explanations of the standard present their detail as an image rather than as text, so the figure could not be read in Chinese from an official source in this session and is therefore omitted.

## In practice

- **Fifteen is the legal number for you.** Article 58 caps an electric bicycle at 15 km/h in the non-motor lane, whatever the machine will do.
- **Ride in the cycle lane, and keep right where there is none.** That is Article 57, and it is also the safest reading of a Chinese street.
- **Assume nothing about the traffic around you.** [Right turns on red are legal and zebra crossings are advisory to a meaningful minority of drivers](/answers/is-china-safe-for-tourists/), which is the context the cycle lane sits in.
- **Rent where the site already suggests it.** [The Yangshuo and Yulong River route](/destinations/guilin-yangshuo/) is flat, rural and the clearest case for one; the Erhai shoreline in Yunnan is the other.
- **Never charge one indoors.** The high-rise rule is national, and the notices by the lift are enforcing it.
- **Payment runs through the wallets.** A bike share needs a Chinese payment app before it needs anything else — [what actually works at a QR code](/guides/money-and-payments-in-china/) comes first.
- **Hong Kong and Macau regulate cycles and electric vehicles under their own law**, so neither the speed cap nor the standard describes a ride there.

## Checked for newer, and what I could not read

Articles 57 and 58 of the Road Traffic Safety Law were read on **the Beijing municipal transport bureau's** copy of the law and cross-checked character for character against **the State Bureau for Letters and Calls'** copy. The two agree exactly on both articles. The amendment header — passed 2003, amended 2007 and 2011, revised 2021 — comes from the Beijing copy.

For **GB 17761-2024**, the record was read on **the national standards full-text platform**, which gives the standard number, title, status, publication and implementation dates and the administering ministry. **That record carries no field labelled 标准性质** — the field that states whether a standard is compulsory or recommended — so its compulsory character is taken from **a municipal market regulator's notice**, which calls it a 强制性国家标准 in terms, and which also supplies the replacement of the 2018 standard and the lead-acid mass change. The two commencement dates come from **a provincial industry and information technology department**, and the abolition of the 2018 standard from **a provincial standards office**. Each of those three points rests on one host, named here for that reason.

Three official explanations of the standard were opened and **all three presented their technical detail as an image**, which is why the specific clause figures beyond the mass ceiling are absent above rather than paraphrased.

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast can you legally ride an e-bike in China?

Fifteen kilometres an hour in the non-motor lane. Article 58 of the Road Traffic Safety Law caps a disabled person's motorised wheelchair and an electric bicycle at that speed when ridden there. It is a limit on the rider rather than a limit on what the machine is built to do.

### Which lane do bicycles use in China?

The non-motor lane, under Article 57. Where a road has no non-motor lane, a non-motor vehicle keeps to the right-hand side of the carriageway. The article places the vehicle rather than describing the street, so where the lane exists it is the answer and where it does not the right-hand side is.

### Can tourists rent an e-bike in China?

Rental is ordinary in the places this site suggests it, notably the flat rural routes around Yangshuo. The rules that apply are the same ones that apply to anybody: the cycle lane, the 15 km/h cap, and the prohibition on charging in a building lobby or stairwell.

### What is the new Chinese e-bike standard?

GB 17761-2024, the Safety technical specification for electric bicycle, published on 31 December 2024. A municipal market regulator describes it as a compulsory national standard wholly replacing GB 17761-2018, and it raised the whole-vehicle mass ceiling for lead-acid models from 55 kg to 63 kg.

### When did the new e-bike standard take effect?

It has two dates. A provincial industry department states that the production stage came under it on 1 September 2025 and the sales stage on 1 December 2025, three months apart, so a machine made before the first date could still be sold until the second.

### Why can't you charge an electric bike indoors in China?

Because the rules on fire safety in high-rise civil buildings prohibit parking or charging one in the public lobby, evacuation corridors, stairwells and exits. A battery fire in a stairwell removes the escape route, which is why charging cabinets sit outside the building.
