Two pages here brush against Chinese cosmetics and neither explains them. The packing list observes that [sunscreen is available but often bundled with skin-whitening agents](/plan/china-packing-list/) and files it under shopping quirks. The Hainan guide notes that [Chinese travellers fly to the island specifically to buy cosmetics](/destinations/sanya-hainan/) at the duty-free malls, and moves on to the next bay.

Both observations are correct. Both have the same explanation, and it is a legal one.

## Two regimes, and a list of six

China's **化妆品监督管理条例** (*huàzhuāngpǐn jiāndū guǎnlǐ tiáolì*, Regulations on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics) was adopted at a State Council executive meeting on 3 January 2020, promulgated as **State Council Order No. 727** on **16 June 2020**, and has been **in force since 1 January 2021**. It replaced a set of hygiene rules dating from 1989.

**Article 16** does the sorting:

> 用于染发、烫发、祛斑美白、防晒、防脱发的化妆品以及宣称新功效的化妆品为特殊化妆品。特殊化妆品以外的化妆品为普通化妆品。

Cosmetics for **hair dyeing, perming, spot-lightening and whitening, sun protection, and preventing hair loss**, together with any cosmetic **claiming a new efficacy**, are **special cosmetics**. Everything else is an **ordinary cosmetic**.

Look at that list again and the packing-list observation stops being a quirk. **Sunscreen and whitening are two entries on the same short list of six.** A manufacturer that has taken a product through the stricter route for one of them has already paid the cost of the stricter route, and combining the two claims in one bottle adds nothing to the regulatory burden. The bundling is a rational response to where the legal line sits.

## Registration for one, a filing for the other

**Article 17** sets out what each regime demands:

> 特殊化妆品经国务院药品监督管理部门注册后方可生产、进口。国产普通化妆品应当在上市销售前向备案人所在地省、自治区、直辖市人民政府药品监督管理部门备案。进口普通化妆品应当在进口前向国务院药品监督管理部门备案。

- **Special cosmetics** may be produced or imported **only after registration** with the national drug regulator. Somebody has to say yes.
- **Domestic ordinary cosmetics** are **filed** with the drug regulator of the province where the filer is based, **before going on sale**.
- **Imported ordinary cosmetics** are **filed** with the national regulator **before import**.

Registration for the risky category, a filing for the rest. Once you have seen it three times you can predict it: Chinese regulation tends to gate the narrow high-risk case and let everything else in on a declaration.

## What that puts on the packet

Here is the checkable part, and it is the seventeenth time this site has found a consumer question already answered in plain sight.

**A special cosmetic carries its registration certificate number on the label.** An ordinary cosmetic gets an electronic filing record with a filing number, which is not required to be printed on the label in the same way. The regulator publishes registration and filing information for the public to search.

So the number on the box is not decoration. It tells you which of the two regimes the product went through — which, for a sunscreen or a whitening product, is the one that required somebody to approve it before it could be sold at all.

## Checked for newer, and there is something from last month

The regulation itself is stable. The Ministry of Justice's administrative-regulations database records Order 727 as **in force with no amendment history**, and I found no amending or repealing instrument.

The **procedure** underneath it is moving, and recently. On **29 July 2026** the national drug regulator issued an announcement optimising cosmetics registration and filing — among other measures, waiving proof of prior overseas marketing for qualifying new foreign products, no longer requiring firms to submit raw-material safety information files and reporting codes at registration, letting one representative product's test report cover formula-similar products from the same brand and filer, and **reducing or waiving toxicological test reports for perming products, non-oxidative hair dyes, whitening products that work by physical coverage only, and ordinary cosmetics using a new ingredient — children's cosmetics excepted.**

The announcement is explicit that it optimises procedure **within** the framework of Order 727 and does not lower quality and safety standards. **The special and ordinary classification above is unchanged by it.**

:::warn What I am not telling you, and why
English coverage often reports flatly that China has "ended animal testing for cosmetics". That is looser than the position warrants, and my retrievals disagreed about the precise scope of the earlier exemption route introduced by the drug regulator's registration and filing rules of 2021, which took effect on 1 May that year.

I could not read those conditions on an official host — the drug regulator's own site refuses requests from here — so **this page names that instrument and its dates and does not state its terms.** What is quoted above is the July 2026 announcement, whose scope I could verify: a named and limited set of product types, with children's cosmetics carved out.

If the testing question is what decides your purchase, treat published summaries as a starting point and check the current position with the regulator rather than with a retailer.
:::

## In practice

- **Read the number, not the claim.** A registration number means the product went through the approval route; that is a fact about process, not a promise about results.
- **Expect sunscreen and whitening together**, and read the ingredients if you want one without the other. This is the single most common surprise for visitors buying Chinese skincare.
- **Hainan duty-free is a domestic phenomenon.** The island's allowance is what draws Chinese shoppers to [the malls at Haitang Bay](/destinations/sanya-hainan/); it is not a route around your own country's import rules on the way home.
- **Cabin rules are a separate question entirely.** What you may carry through security is governed by [the domestic-flight liquids rules](/answers/can-i-take-liquids-on-a-chinese-domestic-flight/), which are stricter than most travellers expect and have nothing to do with any of the above.
- **Counterfeits are a shopping problem, not a labelling one**, and [the remedy if you are sold one has its own number](/guides/sold-a-fake-in-china/).
- **Hong Kong and Macau regulate cosmetics under their own law**, so neither the two regimes nor the label numbers described here apply to a product bought there.

## Frequently asked questions

### What counts as a special cosmetic in China?

Six categories, under Article 16 of the Regulations on the Supervision and Administration of Cosmetics: hair dyeing, perming, spot-lightening and whitening, sun protection, prevention of hair loss, and any cosmetic claiming a new efficacy. Everything outside that list is an ordinary cosmetic.

### Why does Chinese sunscreen contain whitening agents?

Because sun protection and spot-lightening or whitening are two entries on the same list of special cosmetics. A product that has been through the stricter registration route for one claim can carry the other without changing regime, so combining them is the commercially obvious move.

### Do cosmetics need approval to be sold in China?

Special cosmetics do: under Article 17 they may only be produced or imported after registration with the national drug regulator. Ordinary cosmetics need a filing instead — with the provincial regulator before domestic sale, or with the national regulator before import.

### What is the number on Chinese cosmetics packaging?

A special cosmetic carries its registration certificate number on the label; ordinary cosmetics receive a filing number recorded electronically. The regulator publishes registration and filing information for public search, so the number identifies which regime a product went through.

### Has China stopped animal testing on cosmetics?

The position is narrower than the common summary. A July 2026 announcement reduces or waives toxicological test reports for a named set of product types — perming products, non-oxidative hair dyes, whitening products working by physical coverage only, and ordinary cosmetics using a new ingredient, with children's cosmetics excepted. An earlier exemption route dating from 2021 exists as well, and this page does not state its conditions because they could not be verified on an official source.

### Is Chinese skincare worth buying?

That is a taste question rather than a regulatory one, and this page does not answer it. What it can tell you is how to read the box: which regime the product went through, whether the claim on the front is one of the six that require approval, and that the number is checkable.
