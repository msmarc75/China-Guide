Every page here that mentions the crossing says the same true and incomplete thing: fifty minutes from Guangzhou South, both borders cleared inside West Kowloon. [The answer page says so](/answers/how-do-i-get-from-guangzhou-to-hong-kong/), [the Hong Kong page says so](/destinations/hong-kong/), and the rail timings table says so.

What none of them says is that the ticket sits under a separate set of rules. It is bought on 12306 like any other, and after that almost nothing about it behaves like a domestic ticket.

## The instrument, and it is new

The text is the **广深港高速铁路跨境旅客运输组织规则** (*guǎng shēn gǎng gāosù tiělù kuàjìng lǚkè yùnshū zǔzhī guīzé*, Rules for the Organisation of Cross-Boundary Passenger Transport on the Guangzhou–Shenzhen–Hong Kong High-Speed Railway), issued by **China State Railway Group** and **in force from 1 April 2026**. Its Article 38 repeals the version that preceded it, the group's 2022 re-publication notice **铁客〔2022〕169号**.

Article 2 sets the scope, and it is drawn by destination rather than by line: the rules govern cross-boundary high-speed passenger transport **between passenger stations administered by China State Railway Group and Hong Kong West Kowloon station**. If West Kowloon is one end of your ticket, you are inside this rulebook. If it is not, you are not.

## The refund scale runs the other way

This is the part worth reading before you book, because it inverts the assumption a traveller brings from the domestic network.

:::warn Two ladders, and the cross-boundary one is upside down
[The domestic refund ladder on this site](/guides/missed-trains-and-refunds-in-china/) charges **nothing** eight days out and rises to 20% inside 24 hours. Here is Article 13 of the cross-boundary rules:

> 在车票开车时间前48小时内办理退票的，按车票票价的50%计算；在车票开车时间前48小时至第7天的，按车票票价的30%计算；在车票开车时间前8天及以上的，按车票票价的5%计算。

| When you refund | Cross-boundary | Domestic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **8 days or more** | **5%** | none |
| 48 hours to 7 days | **30%** | 5% |
| **Under 48 hours** | **50%** | 10–20% |

Every band is worse, and the gap widens as the date approaches. A cross-boundary ticket cancelled the day before costs **half its value**; the same cancellation on a domestic train costs a fifth.

The band structure differs too. Domestic refunds are graded at 8 days, 48 hours and 24 hours. Cross-boundary refunds know only three bands and stop caring below 48 hours — refunding forty hours out and refunding two hours out cost exactly the same.
:::

## A changed ticket becomes final

Article 12 governs changes, and it contains three restrictions that have no domestic equivalent.

**One change, and one only.** Within the pre-sale period and subject to capacity, a passenger may process a change **once**, and no change of origin or destination station is available at all.

**Then the door shuts behind you:**

> 改签后的车票不得退票。

**A ticket that has been changed may not be refunded.** Not at 5%, not at 50% — not at all. On the domestic network a change is normally free and the resulting ticket keeps its refund rights; here the change spends them.

The practical consequence is a decision rather than a sequence. If your plans are wobbling, work out whether you want the money or the flexibility **before** you touch the booking, because taking the change forecloses the refund permanently.

## The clock is thirty minutes, and it cuts both ways

Article 6 sets the selling rules. Two numbers matter:

- **The advance sale period is 15 days, including the day you buy.** 车票预售期为15天（含当天）— and the rule adds that where the railway adjusts that period, it announces the change in advance. This is the cross-boundary figure; it is not a statement about domestic tickets, whose window this site describes separately.
- **For a ticket departing from Hong Kong West Kowloon, online purchase closes 30 minutes before departure.** Other sales channels close on their own schedules.

That same thirty minutes reappears twice more. Article 12 puts the change deadline before departure generally, **but 30 minutes before departure for a ticket leaving West Kowloon**. Article 13 does the same for refunds.

So on the Hong Kong side of the journey the whole after-sales machinery — buy, change, refund — stops half an hour before the train moves. On the mainland side it runs to departure.

## Baggage is a limit, not an allowance

Article 21 is unusually specific, and there is no hold to fall back on:

| Passenger | Free baggage |
| --- | --- |
| Adult | **20 kg** |
| Child, including a child travelling free | **10 kg** |
| Diplomatic personnel | **35 kg** |

Each item's length, width and height must sum to **no more than 130 cm**, and the article states plainly that **checked baggage is not handled** — 不办理行李托运. There is no counter to hand a heavy case to. What you carry aboard is what travels.

## Identity, and one ticket per document

Articles 25 and 26 restate the real-name system in stricter terms than a domestic journey needs you to think about.

A real-name ticket must be **bought with the traveller's valid identity document**, and at entry to the station and on boarding the passenger must **produce the same document used to buy it**. Ticket, document and person must match — 票、证、人一致方可进站、乘车 — or you do not board.

And Article 26: **one valid identity document may buy only one real-name ticket for the same date and the same train**. Book for a group on one passport and the second ticket does not exist.

## Two numbers for when things go wrong

- **Lost property is held for 30 days.** Article 23 fixes the retention period in the rules themselves rather than leaving it to the station.
- **A personal-injury claim runs for three years**, counted from the day after the injury. Article 34.

Neither number is on your ticket, and neither is discoverable at the moment you need it.

## In practice

- **Decide refund or change before you touch the booking.** A change is one-way: it uses your single permitted alteration and destroys the refund right in the same movement.
- **Book the cross-boundary leg later than you would book a domestic one.** With 5% charged even eight days out, speculative booking is not free here the way it is on the domestic network — and the window is only fifteen days anyway.
- **Coming out of Hong Kong, treat 30 minutes before departure as the hard edge of everything.** Buying, changing and refunding all stop there.
- **Travel light or send it ahead.** Twenty kilos, 130 cm summed, and no checked baggage — [posting a bag onward inside China](/guides/sending-things-home-from-china/) is a real alternative if you are carrying more.
- **Passports must match the booking exactly.** One document, one ticket, one train, one date, and the same document produced at the gate.
- **This is a border, not a leg.** The train is the easy part; [what crossing into Hong Kong does to a Chinese visa or a visa-free entry](/answers/how-do-i-get-from-guangzhou-to-hong-kong/) is the part that decides whether you can come back.
- **Macau is not covered by any of this.** It has no rail link to the mainland network, and its own rules govern arrival by ferry or bridge.

The rules above were read on **China Railway 12306's own published rules page**, which carries the text in force from 1 April 2026 together with its article numbers and the repeal of the 2022 notice. I could not complete an independent cross-check. **MTR's republication of the same rules is a PDF**, which this environment reads as binary, and the English FAQ pages on MTR's high-speed rail site do not carry the fee percentages or the alteration limits. Non-official summaries of the same rules circulate with **different figures** — one gives the 50% band as running from two hours to two days, which does not match the text — so this page uses only what the operator's own rules page states.

Two further changes are reported in Chinese media coverage of the new rules and **are not used here**, because I could not confirm them on the operator's page: an extension of scope to stations between Guangzhou East and Guangmingcheng, and the figures the 30-day and three-year limits are said to have replaced.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to refund a Hong Kong high-speed rail ticket?

Under Article 13 of the cross-boundary rules in force from 1 April 2026: 5% of the fare eight days or more before departure, 30% from 48 hours to seven days before, and 50% within 48 hours. That is a different and consistently more expensive scale than the domestic one, which charges nothing eight days out.

### Can I change a train ticket to Hong Kong?

Once, and only within the advance sale period and if capacity exists. The origin and destination stations cannot be changed at all. Most importantly, a ticket that has been changed can no longer be refunded under any band, so the change and the refund are alternatives rather than a sequence.

### When do ticket sales close for a train leaving Hong Kong West Kowloon?

Thirty minutes before departure for purchases made through the 12306 website, with other channels closing on their own schedules. The same thirty-minute deadline applies to changing and to refunding a ticket departing from West Kowloon, which is earlier than the mainland-side deadline of departure itself.

### How far ahead can I book a Guangzhou to Hong Kong train?

Fifteen days, counting the day you buy, under Article 6 of the cross-boundary rules. The rules add that the railway announces any adjustment to that period in advance. This figure is stated for cross-boundary tickets and should not be read as the rule for domestic services.

### How much luggage can I take on the Hong Kong high-speed train?

Twenty kilograms for an adult, ten for a child including a child travelling free, and thirty-five for diplomatic personnel, with each item limited to 130 cm for length, width and height added together. Checked baggage is not handled on cross-boundary services, so everything travels with you in the carriage.

### Do I need the same passport I booked with?

Yes. The rules require that a real-name ticket be bought with the traveller's valid identity document and that the same document be produced at station entry and at boarding, with ticket, document and person all matching. One document may also buy only one ticket for a given train on a given date.
