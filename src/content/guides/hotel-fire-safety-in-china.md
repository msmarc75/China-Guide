Two pages on this site give the same small instruction. The accommodation guide suggests [a glance at the evacuation plan on the back of the door](/guides/accommodation-in-china/). The page on lucky numbers explains that [a floor labelled 18 may physically be the sixteenth](/culture/chinese-lucky-numbers/) and notes, in passing, that this matters for matching a room number against a fire-evacuation plan.

Neither says what the plan is or where the rules actually put it. It turns out the answer changes the advice.

## Two instruments

**The Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China**, in its **2021 amended version**, is the general layer.

**Emergency Management Department Order No. 5**, the *Provisions on Fire Safety Management of High-Rise Civil Buildings*, adopted on **28 December 2020** and **in force since 1 August 2021**, is the specific one — and since almost every Chinese city hotel is a high-rise, it is the one that describes the building you are standing in.

Article 50 sets the threshold: a **high-rise public building** is a non-single-storey public building **more than 24 metres** tall; high-rise residential means over 27 metres. A hotel of eight floors or more is comfortably inside it.

## The diagram is on the landing

**Article 42** is the provision worth knowing, and it puts the plan somewhere other than where visitors look:

> 高层民用建筑应当在每层的显著位置张贴安全疏散示意图，公共区域电子显示屏应当播放消防安全提示和消防安全知识。

A high-rise civil building must **post a safety evacuation diagram in a prominent position on every floor**, and the electronic screens in public areas must play fire-safety prompts and information.

The same article adds a duty for **high-rise public buildings** specifically: on the **ground floor**, in a prominent position, they must alert the public to fire risk and to the **locations of the exits, the evacuation routes and the firefighting equipment**.

### What is not required

I looked for a guest-room obligation and there is not one. **Order No. 5 contains no article dedicated to hotels or to guest rooms.** The card on the back of your door is ordinary commercial practice — widespread, useful, and not the thing the rules guarantee.

That is why the advice on this site is worth sharpening rather than repeating. **The document the law puts in the building is on your landing.** Look at it when you step out of the lift on arrival, which takes five seconds and is the one moment you will ever do it calmly.

## Why the landing diagram beats the key card

Here is where the two existing pages connect, and it is the practical payoff.

Chinese buildings routinely skip or relabel floors containing a four, so a room on "floor 18" can be physically the sixteenth storey. A **landing diagram is drawn to the building as built** — it shows you where the stairs are relative to where you are actually standing, without asking you to trust a number that has been marketed rather than measured.

So: read the plan on the floor, count the doors to the nearest staircase, and note whether there is a second one in the other direction. That is the whole exercise.

## Exits have to be clear, and that is enforceable

The general layer matters when something is wrong rather than when everything is normal.

**Article 16** of the Fire Protection Law requires units to install fire equipment and **set fire safety signs** to national and industry standards, to inspect and maintain them, and to **keep evacuation routes, exits and fire lanes clear**.

**Article 28** goes further and is the one to quote if you find a blocked stairwell. Nobody may damage, misappropriate, dismantle or disable fire equipment, obstruct hydrants or occupy fire separation distances, and nobody may **occupy, block or seal evacuation routes, exits or fire lanes**. It closes with a clause you can see with your own eyes: in **crowded places, doors and windows may not carry obstacles that hinder escape or rescue**.

A stairwell door chained shut, or a corridor used as storage, is not a quirk of the property. It is the specific thing two national instruments prohibit, and it is a reasonable thing to raise at the desk or to weigh when choosing to stay.

## The electric bicycles in the lobby

One more visible detail, because the signs are everywhere and the reason is rarely given.

**Article 37** prohibits **parking electric bicycles, or charging them, in the public lobby, evacuation corridors, stairwells and exits** of a high-rise civil building.

Those red notices by the lift in a Chinese apartment block or hotel are enforcing that article. The rule exists because a battery fire in a stairwell removes the escape route and the building at the same time, which is also why you will see charging cabinets outside rather than bikes inside.

## In practice

- **Look at the landing diagram on arrival.** It is required to be there and it reflects the real building.
- **Count doors to the stairs.** In smoke you will be doing this by hand, not by sight.
- **Check the stairwell door opens.** If it does not, that is the prohibited thing rather than a normal one.
- **Do not rely on the floor number.** [The label may not be the storey](/culture/chinese-lucky-numbers/), and the height matters more than the number.
- **119 is the fire number**, free from any phone including one with no SIM — [the four emergency numbers are worth memorising before you need them](/answers/what-are-the-emergency-numbers-in-china/).
- **The lighter in your bag is a separate subject entirely**, and a stricter one — [fire sources are handled as an offence at Chinese airports](/answers/can-i-take-a-lighter-on-a-plane-in-china/) rather than as a screening find.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Chinese hotels have to display an evacuation plan?

High-rise civil buildings must post a safety evacuation diagram in a prominent position on every floor, and high-rise public buildings must also flag fire risk and the location of exits, escape routes and firefighting equipment on the ground floor. The card on a guest-room door is common practice rather than a national requirement.

### Where is the fire evacuation plan in a Chinese hotel?

On the landing of each floor, in a prominent position, because that is where the rules put it. Many hotels also put a card inside the room, but the one the regulation guarantees is outside the lift.

### What is the fire emergency number in China?

119. It is free from any phone, including one with no SIM card, and it sits alongside 110 for police and 120 for an ambulance.

### Is it illegal to block a fire exit in China?

Yes. The Fire Protection Law prohibits occupying, blocking or sealing evacuation routes, exits and fire lanes, and requires them to be kept clear. In crowded places, doors and windows may not carry obstacles that hinder escape or rescue.

### Why are electric bikes banned from Chinese building lobbies?

Because the rules on high-rise civil buildings prohibit parking or charging them in the public lobby, evacuation corridors, stairwells and exits. A battery fire in a stairwell takes out the escape route, which is why charging cabinets are placed outside.

### What counts as a high-rise building in China?

A high-rise public building is a non-single-storey public building over 24 metres tall; high-rise residential means over 27 metres. Most city hotels are inside the definition, which is why these rules describe the building you are staying in.
