"Five-star" turns up on this site five times, always as a shorthand — a five-star hotel is where the porter expects a tip, where the laundry room will not be, where the lifts will work. That is a reasonable everyday use of the phrase and it is also, in China, a term of art with a standard number behind it.

Knowing what the plaque actually certifies is worth a minute, mostly because of what its **absence** does and does not mean.

## The standard, and the plaque

Chinese hotel star ratings are set by a national standard, **GB/T 14308**, *Classification and evaluation of tourist hotels*. The current edition was **published on 27 November 2023 and has been in force since 1 March 2024**, replacing the 2010 edition in full — which had itself replaced a 2003 edition. It sits under the Ministry of Culture and Tourism.

The mark itself is specified rather than left to the hotel. It is a device combining **the Great Wall and five-pointed stars**: one star for one-star, up to five stars for five-star, and — above that — **five platinum stars for the platinum five-star grade**, which is the top of the scale and the one most visitors do not know exists.

So a row of five stars is not the ceiling. If a Chinese hotel is showing you white-metal stars rather than gold ones, it is telling you something specific.

## The three-year clock

This is the part that makes the plaque more informative than a review score.

**A star mark is valid for three years.** At the end of that period the hotel is re-reviewed rather than automatically renewed, and the review looks at operating data over the preceding three years alongside the state of the building — occupancy, room rates, revenue, operating margin, and what has been spent on renovation and maintenance.

The consequence is that a star is a statement about a hotel's condition **now**, not about how good it was when it opened. And ratings are genuinely withdrawn: municipal rating committees publish notices cancelling a hotel's star qualification, in the same place and format as the notices awarding one.

## The thing to understand about GB/T

Here is the point that changes how you read the plaque, and it turns on two letters.

**GB/T** marks a *recommended* national standard rather than a mandatory one — the same distinction this site sets out for [gemstone nomenclature, where GB/T 16552 governs what a stone may be called while a separate GB standard is compulsory](/guides/buying-jade-in-china/). Star rating is on the recommended side of that line, and the rating is **applied for**: a hotel submits itself for evaluation by the rating committee.

Which means the useful inference runs one way only.

**A star tells you something. No star tells you almost nothing.** An unrated hotel has not failed an inspection; in the ordinary case it simply never entered the process. Plenty of modern, excellent, well-run Chinese hotels have no plaque at all, because the scheme was designed around a category of "tourist hotel" that a new domestic chain has no particular reason to join.

That matters directly for the advice on this site, which is to [prefer the domestic mid-market chains over the international brands](/guides/accommodation-in-china/) on price and consistency. Nothing about that advice changes. Just do not use the absence of stars as evidence against a hotel.

## What it does and does not measure

Read the star as a statement about **facilities and service process** — what the building has, what the hotel undertakes to do, and whether it is being maintained. It is not a measure of whether you will like the place, whether the beds are good, or whether the neighbourhood is right.

It is also silent on the question visitors most often need answered, which is whether the desk will actually take a foreign passport. That is a separate matter entirely, governed by [the registration duty that falls on hotels as a class](/answers/can-foreigners-stay-in-any-hotel-in-china/), and a starred hotel is not automatically a hotel that will check you in.

## How to check one

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism runs a public enquiry service for star-rated hotels through its own service portal, so a claim on a booking page is verifiable rather than something to take on trust. Provincial and municipal culture-and-tourism bureaux publish their own award, re-review and cancellation notices, which is where a recently withdrawn rating shows up first.

In practice:

- **Look at the plaque on the way in.** Count the stars and note the colour. Platinum is above gold.
- **Treat an old plaque as a question, not an answer.** Three years is the cycle; a rating is a claim about a review that has already happened.
- **Do not read "no plaque" as "not good".** It usually means the hotel never applied.
- **Ignore the word "five-star" in marketing copy** unless there is a plaque or a register entry behind it. The phrase is not protected in ordinary advertising the way the award itself is administered.
- **Ask about the passport separately.** The star has nothing to do with it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Chinese hotel star ratings official?

Yes. They are awarded against national standard GB/T 14308, whose current edition has been in force since 1 March 2024, by tourist-hotel rating committees under the Ministry of Culture and Tourism. The plaque is a specified device showing the Great Wall and a row of stars.

### What is a platinum five-star hotel in China?

The grade above five-star, shown as five platinum-coloured stars rather than five ordinary ones. It is the top of the Chinese scale, and it is the reason a row of five stars is not necessarily the highest rating on offer.

### How long is a Chinese hotel star rating valid?

Three years. After that the hotel is re-reviewed rather than renewed automatically, with the review taking in three years of operating data and what has been spent on maintaining the building. Ratings are cancelled as well as granted.

### Does a Chinese hotel without a star rating mean it is bad?

No. GB/T 14308 is a recommended standard and the rating is applied for, so an unrated hotel has usually never entered the process rather than failed it. Many good modern domestic hotels carry no plaque.

### Does a star rating mean the hotel accepts foreign guests?

No — the two are unrelated. Whether a desk will register a foreign passport is governed by the registration rules rather than by the rating, and a starred hotel can still turn you away.

### How can I check a Chinese hotel's star rating?

Through the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's public enquiry service for star-rated hotels, and through the award and cancellation notices published by provincial and municipal culture-and-tourism bureaux, which is where a withdrawn rating appears first.
