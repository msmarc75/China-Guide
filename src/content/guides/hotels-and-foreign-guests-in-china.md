A foreign visitor to China is told, correctly, that some hotels will not take them. The usual explanation — that the hotel lacks a licence to accept foreigners and is therefore legally unable to check you in — is where the account goes wrong.

The refusal happens. The reason given for it is not the rule.

## The duty is on the hotel, and it names hotels generally

The governing provision is Article 39 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law, adopted 30 June 2012 and in force since 1 July 2013:

> 外国人在中国境内旅馆住宿的，旅馆应当按照旅馆业治安管理的有关规定为其办理住宿登记，并向所在地公安机关报送外国人住宿登记信息。

Where foreigners stay in hotels in China, **the hotels shall register their accommodation** under the public-security rules for the hotel trade, and submit the registration information to the local public security organ.

Read what it does and does not say. It imposes an obligation on hotels. It does not create a category of hotel permitted to receive foreigners and another forbidden to. Shanghai's police put the consequence plainly in their own public guidance: every hotel in the city can process the registration.

## The penalty sits on the hotel too

Article 76 of the same law sets what happens when the registration is not done: a hotel that fails to carry it out is punished under the public-security penalties law, and one that fails to submit the information gets a warning, rising to a fine of between ¥1,000 and ¥5,000 where the circumstances are serious.

That is the shape of the thing. The obligation is the hotel's, the penalty is the hotel's, and you are the person it is performed on rather than the person who can get it wrong. A front desk that would rather not deal with an unfamiliar procedure is avoiding its own risk, not applying a rule about you.

## What seven ministries said in 2024

The site already notes a government push to stop hotels refusing foreign guests. It has a name, a date and a specific sentence.

**Notice of the Ministry of Commerce and six other departments on measures to facilitate accommodation for people from outside the mainland**, document **商服贸函〔2024〕324号** (*shāng fú mào hán*, the ministry's trade-in-services series, No. 324 of 2024), issued **1 July 2024** by the Ministry of Commerce, the Cyberspace Administration, the **Ministry of Public Security**, the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, the People's Bank of China, the **National Immigration Administration** and the foreign exchange administration.

Two of its provisions matter to a traveller standing in a lobby:

- **Local departments and online platforms should not use qualification requirements as a threshold** restricting accommodation businesses from receiving guests from outside the mainland.
- **Operators should not improperly publish information saying they do not accept such guests**, and platforms are told to vet what their listed merchants publish.

The notice also covers simplifying what is collected at registration, widening the ways it can be submitted, and separately improving foreign card acceptance — [the payment half of the same problem](/guides/money-and-payments-in-china/), which is why the two got better at roughly the same time.

## So what is actually happening at the desk

Something real, and worth naming accurately.

A small hotel that has never checked in a foreign passport may not know the procedure, may not have the terminal set up for it, or may simply prefer not to find out at ten at night. That is a genuine obstacle and no amount of citing a notice dissolves it.

But it is an operational obstacle rather than a legal bar, and that changes the register of what you are asking. **Asking the desk to call the local police station is not asking for an exception.** It is asking them to complete a step they are the ones obliged to complete, and it is why that request works often enough to be worth making.

## The distinction to carry

Two sentences that sound the same and are not:

- *"This hotel will not take me."* Frequently true, especially in a small city, and the only sensible response is to have a booking somewhere else.
- *"Hotels are not allowed to take me."* Not what the law says, and since July 2024 explicitly not something a department or a booking platform is supposed to enforce.

Keeping them apart costs nothing and occasionally gets you a room. It also means you can [treat the refusal as a booking problem rather than a status problem](/answers/can-foreigners-stay-in-any-hotel-in-china/), which is the correct frame for planning around it.

## What this does not change

Nothing in the practical advice moves.

- **Book through an international-facing platform** or a national chain, which is still the reliable way never to meet the problem.
- **Expect it to be live outside the big cities**, because a notice takes time to reach a county-town front desk.
- **Xinjiang is its own case.** The list of properties in practice is shorter and the checks are heavier — [that page describes a stricter regime and it still applies](/answers/can-foreigners-travel-to-xinjiang/).
- **Staying somewhere that is not a hotel is different.** There the duty moves to you, and [registering yourself at the neighbourhood police station within 24 hours](/answers/do-i-need-to-register-with-police-in-china/) is the step that replaces the passport scan at reception.
- **Do not argue.** The desk staff did not write any of this, and [the way complaints work in China](/answers/how-do-i-complain-politely-in-china/) rewards a calm request over a correct one delivered loudly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do Chinese hotels need a special licence to accept foreigners?

The law does not describe one. Article 39 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law puts the registration duty on hotels as a class, and since July 2024 a seven-department notice has told local authorities and booking platforms not to use qualification requirements as a barrier to accommodation businesses receiving guests from outside the mainland.

### Is it legal for a Chinese hotel to refuse a foreign guest?

The refusal is not something the rules provide for, and the 2024 measures point the other way — including a provision that operators should not publish that they do not accept such guests. In practice small properties still refuse, usually from unfamiliarity with the procedure rather than a prohibition.

### What should I do if a Chinese hotel refuses me?

Ask them to call the local police station, since the registration step is theirs to complete and the call often resolves an uncertainty rather than a rule. If that fails, move on — you will not win it at the counter, and having a fallback booking is worth more than being right.

### Why do hotels bother refusing if it is allowed?

Because the duty and the penalty both sit on the hotel. A hotel that mishandles the registration faces the consequences, so an unfamiliar procedure at a quiet front desk is a risk it can avoid by saying no. That is an incentive problem, not a statement about foreign guests.

### Has it got easier for foreigners to book hotels in China?

Yes, noticeably since 2024, in the large cities especially. The same package of measures also pushed foreign card acceptance and simplified registration, which is why visitors who came before and after that period describe two fairly different countries.
