The gate closes three to five minutes before departure, and it closes properly: [arriving at a Chinese station](/answers/how-early-should-i-arrive-at-a-chinese-train-station/) is a lesson most visitors learn once. The train goes without you.

Then what? The English-language internet mostly stops at the warning, which leaves a stranded traveller assuming the ticket is worthless. It very probably is not.

## You have until midnight

This is the rule worth knowing before you need it. A ticket change in China is called a **改签** (*gǎi qiān*), and China Railway's official notice on changes and refunds provides that one is free of charge 开车后在当日24时之前改签当日其他列车时 — when made, after the train has departed and before 24:00 that same day, to another train running that day.

So a missed train is not a lost ticket. It is a ticket that can be moved to any other service running that day, at no fee, provided a seat exists.

There is one hard condition, and it is the part that catches people. 12306's own guidance states 开车之后，旅客仍可改签当日其他列车，但只能在票面发站办理改签 — after departure, a passenger may still change to another train that day, **but the change can only be processed at the departure station printed on the ticket**. Not in the app, not at the next station, not by phone. If you missed the 09:40 from Shanghai Hongqiao, you must be standing in Shanghai Hongqiao to fix it.

Go to a staffed ticket counter rather than a machine, take your passport, and do it straight away — later trains that day sell out while you are deciding.

## The refund fee ladder

If you would rather have your money back than another seat, refunds are charged on a sliding scale set out in China National Railway Group's notice on refund and change fees, in force since **14 January 2021**.

| How long before departure you refund | Fee |
| --- | --- |
| 8 days or more | None |
| 48 hours to under 8 days | 5% of the fare |
| 24 to under 48 hours | 10% of the fare |
| Under 24 hours | 20% of the fare |

A minimum fee of ¥2 applies. The practical reading for a traveller is that **cancelling more than eight days out costs nothing at all**, which makes speculative booking on the day the window opens far less risky than it sounds — and the booking window is short enough that [booking early](/answers/can-i-book-chinese-train-tickets-in-advance/) rarely means booking more than about two weeks ahead in the first place.

:::warn The spring-festival exception
During 春运 (*chūn yùn*), the forty-day travel surge around Chinese New Year, tickets are refunded at the under-24-hour rate regardless of how far ahead you cancel. The 20% band applies to everything. Plan chunyun travel as though it were non-refundable.
:::

## Changing is usually free

Outside the missed-train case, a 改签 normally costs nothing. The 2021 notice lists three fee-free situations: changing more than 48 hours before departure; changing, within 48 hours of departure, to a train on the same day or earlier; and the post-departure same-day case above.

You pay only the fare difference if the new train is dearer, and are refunded it if it is cheaper. A change is almost always the better move than a refund-and-rebook, because it avoids the refund fee entirely.

## If you simply bought the wrong ticket

Since **19 January 2026**, 12306 has run a limited free-cancellation window for mistaken purchases, and China National Railway Group has said it intends to keep it as standing policy rather than a seasonal measure. Where a ticket is bought through the 12306 platform, it can be refunded online with no fee if the request is made **within 30 minutes of successful payment and more than four hours before departure**.

The limits matter as much as the rule: one such order per purchaser per calendar day, and it does not apply to tickets that have already been changed, or to waitlisted and reserved purchases. It is a remedy for fat-fingering a date, not a free option on a booking.

## When the railway is at fault

Cancellations and serious delays are treated differently and cost you nothing.

Where a train is cancelled, the ticket can be refunded with no fee, and the window runs from the announcement of the cancellation until well after the travel date rather than closing at departure time — China Railway's passenger transport rules put refunds arising from railway responsibility outside the fee schedule entirely. The same applies where a service is cut short and your boarding or alighting station falls in the cancelled section. These refunds can be processed online, at any station counter, or at a self-service machine.

Chinese long-distance rail is punctual to a degree that makes this rare, and the contrast with flying is stark: a cancelled train is refunded in full as a matter of course, while [a cancelled flight is governed by the airline's own conditions of carriage](/guides/flight-delays-in-china/) and a weather delay leaves the hotel bill with you. Weather does close high-speed lines in winter and in typhoon season on the southeast coast, and when it does, whole blocks of services disappear at once. That is the scenario worth having read about — [how the network works](/guides/transport-in-china/) explains why an alternative departure usually exists within the hour.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens if you miss a train in China?

You can change the ticket to another train running the same day, free of charge, up to 24:00 that day — but only in person at the station your ticket was departing from. Take your passport to a staffed ticket counter and do it immediately, because later services fill up.

### How much does it cost to refund a Chinese train ticket?

Nothing if you cancel eight days or more before departure. Within that: 5% of the fare from 48 hours to under 8 days, 10% from 24 to under 48 hours, and 20% under 24 hours, with a ¥2 minimum. During the spring festival travel period every refund is charged at the 20% rate.

### Can I change a Chinese train ticket for free?

Usually, yes. Changes made more than 48 hours before departure carry no fee, nor do changes made within 48 hours to a train on the same day or earlier, nor same-day changes after the train has left. You pay or recover only the difference in fare.

### Can I refund a train ticket after the train has departed?

Changing it is the better route — a same-day change after departure is free, and available until midnight at the departure station. A straightforward refund after departure is not the standard remedy, so if you have missed a train, ask for a change rather than a refund.

### What if my Chinese train is cancelled?

You are refunded in full with no fee, and the claim window stays open well beyond the travel date rather than closing at departure. It can be handled online, at any station ticket window or at a self-service machine. The same applies if the train is cut short and your station falls in the cancelled section.

### I booked the wrong date on 12306 — can I undo it?

Within 30 minutes of paying, and more than four hours before departure, yes and at no cost, under a rule 12306 introduced on 19 January 2026. It is limited to one order per purchaser per day and does not cover tickets already changed or bought from the waiting list.
