Shanghai Disneyland opened on **16 June 2016** and Universal Beijing Resort entered full operation on **20 September 2021**. Both are enormous, both are genuinely good, and both operate on a ticketing system that catches out visitors who have only ever bought a theme-park ticket at a window.

The system is the subject of this page, because it is the part that can end a day before it starts.

## Real-name ticketing is the whole problem

Chinese attractions run on real-name booking, and these two run it strictly. A ticket is not a piece of paper that admits whoever holds it — it is an entitlement attached to a named person, verified against a government identity document at the gate.

**Universal Beijing's own park rules** are explicit. Guests "must have valid tickets for entry" and "are requested to provide physical valid government ID cards for age and identification verification". For overseas guests the accepted document is a **passport or Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card**. Admission tickets are "non-transferable, void if altered" and "must be used by the same person during its period of validity", and guests must "make a reservation for desired visitation date in advance for each Park admission through Official Mobile Applications".

Shanghai Disney Resort operates the same way under its Real Name Ticketing Policy: each ticket is linked to a government ID, foreign visitors use their passport, and pricing and admission are tied to a specific date.

The parks are not a special case. Any event in China with more than 5,000 attendees runs on the same regime, which is why [a football or basketball ticket works the same way](/guides/watching-sport-in-china/).

:::warn The original document, not a photograph of it
This is the single most common way a trip to either park goes wrong. Park staff check the **original physical document** used at the time of purchase. A photocopy, a phone photo or a screenshot is not accepted, which is the opposite of the habit many visitors adopt for everyday use in China.

Bring the passport itself. It is [the document you are supposed to be carrying anyway](/answers/do-i-need-to-carry-my-passport-in-china/), and here it is not a formality.
:::

## Booking, in the order that works

1. **Install the park's own app** — Shanghai Disney Resort's, or the official application Universal Beijing designates — and register before you try to buy anything.
2. **Enter your passport name and number exactly as printed.** A mismatch is discovered at the gate, which is the worst possible moment, and it is the same failure mode as [a mistyped passport number on a rail booking](/answers/can-i-book-chinese-train-tickets-in-advance/).
3. **Pick your date.** Pricing is date-based and the ticket is bought for that date. One passport gets one ticket for one visit date.
4. **Pay** with an international card bound inside the app, or through Alipay or WeChat Pay — the same [payment set-up that everything else in China needs](/guides/money-and-payments-in-china/).

Reservation for a specific date is a separate requirement at Universal Beijing, made through the official app for each admission, rather than something the ticket purchase does for you.

## Outside food: the rule that changed, and the rule that did not

Most Chinese attractions do not let you bring your own food. Shanghai Disneyland is the well-known exception, and the reason is a court case.

The park banned outside food and drink in November 2017. In March 2019 a law student, Wang Jieying, sued after being refused entry with snacks and subjected to a bag search she considered intrusive. The case went viral. In **September 2019** the Pudong New Area court announced a settlement: the resort paid 50 yuan in compensation for the confiscated food and **revised its rules to permit visitors to bring in most food and beverages**. This was reported by Caixin, *China Daily*, *People's Daily* and Sixth Tone among others.

Universal Beijing did not change in the same way and its own rules are narrower. It prohibits food and drink "exceed[ing] self-consumption portion", alcoholic beverages, food requiring heating, and food with pungent odours, with exceptions for medical, infant and special dietary needs. In practice that means a personal snack and a drink, not a picnic — and if you travel with [a food allergy or a specific dietary requirement](/food/food-allergies-in-china/), the exception is written into the rules and worth invoking explicitly rather than assuming.

## When to go, and when not to

Both parks sit inside China's domestic holiday calendar, which is the dominant factor in how the day feels.

- **Avoid the two Golden Weeks** — the first week of October and the Spring Festival period — along with weekends and Chinese school holidays. [The holiday calendar](/plan/best-time-to-visit-china/) sets out which weeks move each year.
- **Weekdays outside holidays** are a different park entirely.
- **Summer in Shanghai and Beijing is hot and humid**, and both parks are largely outdoors.

Getting there is straightforward from either city centre: both are on the metro network, which the [Shanghai](/destinations/shanghai/) and [Beijing](/destinations/beijing/) guides cover along with everything else worth building a day around.

The rides themselves are governed separately from the ticket, and by a national regime rather than by park policy: [what a large amusement facility is, and what its operator must display and check each morning](/guides/cable-cars-and-rides-in-china/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can foreigners buy tickets for Shanghai Disneyland?

Yes. Register in the Shanghai Disney Resort app, enter your passport name and number exactly as printed, choose a date and pay by international card, Alipay or WeChat Pay. The ticket is tied to that passport and that date, and you must bring the original passport to the gate.

### Do I need my passport to enter Universal Beijing Resort?

Yes. Universal Beijing's park rules require physical government ID for verification, and for overseas guests that means a passport or Foreign Permanent Resident ID Card. A photocopy, photo or screenshot is not accepted, and the ticket is non-transferable.

### Can you bring your own food into Shanghai Disneyland?

Mostly yes, which is unusual for a Chinese attraction. The park banned outside food in November 2017, a law student sued in 2019, and a September 2019 settlement in the Pudong court led the resort to revise its rules to allow visitors to bring in most food and drink. Universal Beijing is stricter and limits it to self-consumption portions, excluding alcohol, heated food and strong-smelling food.

### Do I need to book a date in advance for Chinese theme parks?

Yes. Both parks sell date-specific admission and Universal Beijing additionally requires a reservation for the intended visit date, made in its official app for each admission. There is no equivalent of turning up and buying at the gate.

### When is the best time to visit Chinese theme parks?

Any weekday outside the October Golden Week, the Spring Festival period and Chinese school holidays. Those weeks bring domestic crowds at a scale that changes the experience completely, and hotel prices near both parks rise with them.
