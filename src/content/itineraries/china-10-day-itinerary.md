The Beijing–Xi'an–Shanghai triangle is the standard first China trip and it is standard because it works: the imperial capital, the ancient capital, and the 21st-century one, connected by high-speed rail, with the Great Wall and the Terracotta Army as bookends.

**Fly into Beijing, out of Shanghai.** An open-jaw ticket avoids a wasted backtrack day.

:::key Book in advance
- **Forbidden City** — 7 days ahead, passport-linked, sells out
- **Shaanxi History Museum**, Xi'an — free but booked 7 days ahead, and worth the effort
- **Shanghai Museum** — free, booked, closed Mondays
- **Trains** — Beijing→Xi'an and Xi'an→Shanghai on 12306 when the window opens
:::

## Days 1–4: Beijing

### Day 1 — Arrival

Airport Express into the city, check in near Qianmen or Dongsi. Walk the neighbourhood, eat Peking duck (**Siji Minfu**, book ahead), sleep early.

### Day 2 — The imperial axis

**Tiananmen Square** at opening, then the **Forbidden City** for three to four hours — take the eastern palaces, not only the central axis. Exit north and climb **Jingshan Park** for the roof view. Afternoon in **Beihai Park** and the **hutongs** around the Drum and Bell Towers, ideally by shared bike. Dinner on a side street off Nanluoguxiang.

### Day 3 — The Great Wall

**Mutianyu**, departing by 07:00. Entry ¥45, cable car ¥120 return, toboggan down. Walk west from the cable car for the quiet stretch. A hired car for the day is ¥600–800 and worth it. Back by late afternoon; a foot massage in the evening.

### Day 4 — Temple of Heaven, then the train

**07:00 at the Temple of Heaven**, for the park's morning tai chi, dancing and opera singing. Then the **Lama Temple**, then either the **Summer Palace** or the **National Museum**.

**Late afternoon: G-train to Xi'an**, 4h20–6h, ¥515. Arrive in the evening; stay inside the city walls near the Bell Tower.

## Days 5–6: Xi'an

### Day 5 — Terracotta Army

**Morning.** Out to the **Terracotta Army** (¥120) on tourist bus 5/306 from the railway station east square (¥8, one hour) or by DiDi. Go at opening. Visit in reverse order — the bronze chariots hall, then Pit 3, then Pit 2, then Pit 1 last, once the tour groups have thinned. Allow three hours plus transit.

**Afternoon.** **Shaanxi History Museum** (free, booked) — one of the best museums in China. Or **Han Yang Ling**, the Han emperor's tomb with its glass-floored excavation and third-scale figures, which is quieter and, for some, more atmospheric.

**Evening.** The **Muslim Quarter** — roujiamo, biangbiang noodles, liangpi, persimmon cakes. Eat one street back from Beiyuanmen for better food and lower prices.

### Day 6 — Walls and pagodas, then the night train or a G-train

**Morning.** **Cycle the city wall** (¥54 entry + ¥45 bike) — the full 13.7 km circuit takes about 100 minutes and is the best two hours in Xi'an. Then the **Great Mosque**, a Ming complex built entirely in Chinese architectural style.

**Afternoon.** **Big Wild Goose Pagoda**, or the **Forest of Steles**.

**Evening.** **G-train to Shanghai**, about 6 hours, ¥670. Or take an overnight sleeper and save a hotel night — though you will arrive tired.

Alternatively, spend a third night in Xi'an and cut one from Shanghai. Xi'an rewards it.

## Days 7–10: Shanghai

### Day 7 — Arrive, the Bund

Check in around the **former French Concession** or the Bund. Walk **Wukang Road** and **Anfu Road** in the afternoon. **The Bund at dusk**, then the ¥2 ferry to Pudong and back.

### Day 8 — Old Shanghai and museums

**Yu Garden** at opening (¥40) and soup dumplings. **Shanghai Museum** (free, booked, closed Mondays). Afternoon: **Tianzifang**, **Xintiandi**, or the **Shanghai Jewish Refugees Museum** in Hongkou — small, moving and largely unvisited.

**Evening:** Shanghai's restaurant scene, which is the best in mainland China.

### Day 9 — Suzhou day trip

**Suzhou**, 25 minutes by train (¥40). One classical garden at 08:00 opening — the **Humble Administrator's Garden** if you want the famous one, the **Master of the Nets** if you want the best — plus **Pingjiang Road** and the **Suzhou Museum** by I. M. Pei. Back to Shanghai by early evening.

Alternative: **Hangzhou** (45 min) for West Lake, or **Zhujiajiao** water town (1 hour by metro).

### Day 10 — Last morning, fly

**Shanghai Tower** floor 118 if the day is clear (¥180), or a final walk through the concession streets. Pudong airport is 45 minutes by metro line 2 or 7.5 minutes by Maglev from Longyang Road. Allow three hours before an international flight.

## Variations

**Swap Xi'an for Chengdu.** Pandas, Sichuan food and teahouses instead of the Terracotta Army. Chengdu is 3 hours from Xi'an and 7–8 from Beijing, so it works better in a 14-day route.

**Add Datong** between Beijing and Xi'an. 1h40 from Beijing, with the **Yungang Grottoes** and the **Hanging Monastery**, then onward to Pingyao and Xi'an. This is the connoisseur's version of the same route.

**Guilin instead of Suzhou.** If you want landscape rather than gardens, fly Shanghai–Guilin and spend three nights in Yangshuo. It costs a day of travel and is worth it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 10 days enough for China?

Yes, for a well-chosen three-city route such as Beijing, Xi'an and Shanghai. Ten days is the shortest trip that covers both imperial and modern China at a sustainable pace, with roughly one travel day in total.

### What is the best 10-day itinerary in China?

Four nights in Beijing, two in Xi'an and three in Shanghai, connected by high-speed rail, with a Suzhou day trip at the end. Fly into Beijing and out of Shanghai so you never retrace a leg.

### How long is the train from Beijing to Xi'an?

Between 4 hours 20 minutes and 6 hours on a G-train, depending on the service, costing about ¥515 in second class. Book on the official 12306 app, which has an English interface and accepts foreign passports.

### Should I add Chengdu to a 10-day China trip?

Only by dropping something else. Chengdu deserves two to three nights and is 3 hours from Xi'an, which makes it a natural fourth city on a 14-day route rather than a 10-day one.

### How much does a 10-day China trip cost?

Roughly ¥8,800 (US$1,245) per person mid-range, excluding international flights — covering hotels around ¥430 a night, meals, two long train legs, a Great Wall day and entry tickets. A backpacker version runs at about half that.
