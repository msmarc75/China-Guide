Three weeks is where a China trip becomes a proper journey rather than a highlights reel. The route below keeps the classic four cities and adds two of China's best landscapes — the karst country of Guangxi and the mountains of Yunnan.

## The route at a glance

| Days | Place | Nights |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | Beijing | 4 |
| 5–7 | Xi'an | 3 |
| 8–10 | Chengdu | 3 |
| 11–13 | Guilin & Yangshuo | 3 |
| 14–18 | Yunnan (Kunming, Dali, Lijiang) | 5 |
| 19–21 | Shanghai | 3 |

**Fly into Beijing, out of Shanghai.** Two internal flights: Chengdu→Guilin and Lijiang→Shanghai.

## Days 1–10: the classic core

Follow the [14-day itinerary](/itineraries/china-14-day-itinerary/) for Beijing, Xi'an and Chengdu exactly as written — four nights, three nights, three nights.

Highlights: the **Forbidden City** and **Mutianyu Great Wall**; the **Terracotta Army**, the **Xi'an city wall by bicycle** and the **Muslim Quarter**; the **Panda Base at 07:30**, teahouses, hotpot and a day at **Leshan** or a Sichuan cooking class.

## Days 11–13: Guilin & Yangshuo

*Fly Chengdu → Guilin (1h45).*

**Day 11.** Transfer straight to **Yangshuo** (1h20 by bus) and stay in a guesthouse along the **Yulong River** rather than in town. Afternoon cycling through the paddies.

**Day 12.** Bus to **Xingping** and take a bamboo raft or the local ferry on the **Yangdi–Xingping stretch** of the Li River — the exact view on the ¥20 note, and better value than the four-hour cruise. Climb **Laozhai Hill** above Xingping for the panorama. Afternoon: **Moon Hill** or rock climbing.

**Day 13.** **Longji rice terraces** at Longsheng (2 hours from Guilin). Dazhai/Jinkeng is the more dramatic of the two village areas. Best flooded in May–June or golden in late September. Ideally stay overnight in a village guesthouse; otherwise it is a long day.

## Days 14–18: Yunnan

*Fly Guilin → Kunming (1h20), then high-speed rail.*

**Day 14 — Kunming.** Arrive, visit the **Yunnan Provincial Museum** (free, excellent on minority cultures) and Green Lake Park. One night.

**Day 15–16 — Dali.** *Rail Kunming → Dali, 2h.* The **Bai** heartland on Erhai Lake below the 4,000 m Cangshan range. Cycle or scooter the eastern lakeshore, visit **Xizhou**'s morning market, walk the Cangshan mountainside path. Two nights.

**Day 17–18 — Lijiang.** *Rail Dali → Lijiang, 2h.* The Naxi old town, UNESCO-listed and very busy — stay in **Shuhe** or **Baisha** instead, and see the old town at dawn. Day trip to **Jade Dragon Snow Mountain** (cable car to 4,506 m; take the altitude seriously) or the **Baisha murals**. Two nights.

:::tip If you have two more days
Insert the **Tiger Leaping Gorge** trek between Lijiang and Shangri-La: two days on the high trail, 22 km, with guesthouses along the way. It is the single best thing in Yunnan and needs the time. Add **Shangri-La** (3,200 m, culturally Tibetan, no permit required) for two more nights and you have a 25-day trip.
:::

## Days 19–21: Shanghai

*Fly Lijiang → Shanghai (3h30).*

**Day 19.** Arrive, check into the **former French Concession**, walk Wukang and Anfu Roads, then **the Bund at dusk** and the ¥2 ferry.

**Day 20.** **Yu Garden** at opening, **Shanghai Museum**, then Tianzifang or the Jewish Refugees Museum.

**Day 21.** **Suzhou** day trip (25 min) for a classical garden at opening, or a slow last morning before the flight home.

## Three alternative three-week routes

### The Silk Road

**Xi'an → Lanzhou → Zhangye → Dunhuang → Turpan → Urumqi → Kashgar.** Rainbow mountains, the Mogao Caves, desert oases, Uyghur bazaars and the Pamirs. A completely different China. See the [Silk Road itinerary](/itineraries/silk-road-china-itinerary/).

### Southern landscape

**Shanghai → Huangshan → Hongcun → Zhangjiajie → Fenghuang → Guilin/Yangshuo → Hong Kong.** Mountains, Huizhou villages, the Avatar pillars and karst rivers, ending with an easy exit through Hong Kong. Book the Huangshan summit hotel before anything else on this route — [it is the one night that fixes the dates](/answers/how-long-do-you-need-at-huangshan/), and the rooms go months ahead.

### Slow east

**Beijing → Datong → Pingyao → Xi'an → Luoyang → Nanjing → Suzhou → Hangzhou → Shanghai.** Grottoes, walled cities, Ming tombs and classical gardens, entirely by rail, with almost no other foreign visitors outside the big two cities.

## Practical notes for a long trip

- **Laundry** — most hostels and many hotels offer it for ¥20–40 a load. Pack for a week, not three. [Which hotels have a machine you can use yourself](/guides/laundry-in-china/) is worth knowing before you book the mid-trip stop, because the cheap Chinese chains are likelier to than the expensive international ones.
- **Pace** — build one genuinely empty day per week. You will need it.
- **Rail booking** — the pre-sale window is around two weeks, so book legs progressively as you travel rather than all at once before departure.
- **Altitude** — Lijiang is 2,400 m and the Jade Dragon cable car reaches 4,506 m. Take it slowly.
- **Visa** — 21 days exceeds the 240-hour transit allowance, so this route needs either unilateral visa-free eligibility (30 days) or a tourist visa. See our [visa guide](/guides/china-visa-guide/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What can you see in China in three weeks?

Comfortably: Beijing, Xi'an, Chengdu, the karst landscape around Yangshuo, a Yunnan loop through Dali and Lijiang, and Shanghai. That covers imperial history, the Terracotta Army, pandas, two of China's best landscapes and the modern coast.

### Is three weeks too long for China?

No — China is the size of Europe, and three weeks is still a highlights trip. The risk in three weeks is over-scheduling rather than boredom; build in one empty day per week.

### Do I need a visa for a three-week China trip?

Yes, unless your nationality qualifies for 30-day unilateral visa-free entry. The 240-hour visa-free transit scheme only covers ten days, so it cannot support a 21-day itinerary.

### Should I fly or take trains on a long China itinerary?

Both. Take high-speed rail for anything under about five hours — Beijing–Xi'an, Xi'an–Chengdu, Kunming–Dali–Lijiang. Fly the long diagonal legs such as Chengdu to Guilin and Lijiang to Shanghai, which would otherwise consume whole days. [The reasoning behind the five-hour line](/answers/should-i-fly-or-take-the-train-between-chinese-cities/) also covers when an overnight sleeper beats both.

### What is the best three-week route in China for a second visit?

The Silk Road from Xi'an to Kashgar, which is culturally and visually a different country, or a slow eastern route through Datong, Pingyao, Luoyang, Nanjing, Suzhou and Hangzhou that concentrates on grottoes, walled cities and classical gardens.
