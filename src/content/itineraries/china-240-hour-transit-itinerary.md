This site's other itineraries treat the 240-hour scheme as a problem. The 21-day route notes that it "exceeds the 240-hour transit allowance". The southern karst route says the same at twelve days. The 14-day route offers Hong Kong as "a useful third-region exit if you are travelling under the scheme" — a hint rather than a plan.

So here is the plan. It is not a better route than [the classic triangle](/itineraries/china-10-day-itinerary/); it is that route re-cut so that every one of the scheme's constraints is satisfied by design rather than by luck.

[The visa guide sets out the rules in full](/guides/china-visa-guide/) and this page will not restate them. What it does is show what they do to a map.

## The four constraints, and what each one decides

| The rule | What it decides |
| --- | --- |
| Exit must be to a **third** country or region | Where the trip **ends** |
| 240 hours from **00:00 the day after arrival** | How many nights you really have |
| Only the **24 permitted provinces** | Which cities are even available |
| Entry and exit at a **designated port** | Which flights you may book |

Read the first row twice, because it is the one that ruins trips. A ticket home to where you came from does not qualify, however long the gap. The trip has to end somewhere else — and **Hong Kong counts as a separate region**, which turns the hardest rule into the easiest one, because you can satisfy it with [a fifty-minute train from Guangzhou](/answers/how-do-i-get-from-guangzhou-to-hong-kong/).

:::key Book before you fly
- **Onward ticket out of the mainland to a third country or region** — the airline will ask at check-in, not immigration
- **Forbidden City** — 7 days ahead, passport-linked
- **Shaanxi History Museum**, Xi'an — free, booked 7 days ahead
- **Shanghai Museum** — free, booked; the two buildings close on different days
- **Trains** — Beijing→Xi'an, Xi'an→Shanghai, Shanghai→Guangzhou on 12306
- **Guangzhou→Hong Kong** — book the through train rather than turning up
:::

## The route

**Fly into Beijing. Leave the mainland at Guangzhou, by train, into Hong Kong.** Fly home from there.

Every province on this route is inside the permitted zone with no city restriction: Beijing, Shaanxi (widened to the whole province in the December 2024 revision), Shanghai and Guangdong.

### Day 0 — Arrival

Land at Beijing. **Your 240 hours do not start until midnight**, so the rest of the arrival day is free of the clock. Say the words *240-hour visa-free transit* at check-in in your home country and have the onward booking on screen; ground staff outside Asia are the ones who get this wrong, not the border.

### Days 1–3 — Beijing

Three nights: the imperial axis, the Great Wall at Mutianyu, the Temple of Heaven at seven in the morning. [The ten-day itinerary gives the day-by-day](/itineraries/china-10-day-itinerary/) and it is unchanged here — the difference is that you have three nights rather than four, because the last night has been spent buying yourself a legal exit.

Evening of Day 3: **G-train to Xi'an**, 4h20–6h.

### Days 4–5 — Xi'an

Terracotta Army at opening on Day 4, Shaanxi History Museum in the afternoon, the Muslim Quarter in the evening. Day 5: cycle the city wall, then the **G-train to Shanghai**, about six hours.

### Days 6–8 — Shanghai

The Bund, Yu Garden, the concession streets, the Shanghai Museum, and a Suzhou day trip on Day 8 — Jiangsu is inside the zone, so the day trip is legal as well as easy.

### Day 9 — Shanghai to Guangzhou

**Morning G-train**, about seven and a half hours, or a short flight if you prefer the time back. Evening in Guangzhou: dim sum, which is [a meal with its own rules](/food/yum-cha-in-china/), and the riverside.

### Day 10 — The exit

**Train to Hong Kong**, about fifty minutes from Guangzhou South. That crossing is your third-region exit, and it must happen **before the 240 hours expire**.

Fly home from Hong Kong, or spend a few days there first — once you have left the mainland the scheme has done its job and Hong Kong's own entry rules apply instead.

:::warn Count the hours, not the days
The scheme is measured in hours from **00:00 on the day after you arrive**. Ten calendar days after that midnight is when it ends, and it ends at a moment, not at a bedtime.

This route deliberately leaves the mainland on the last day rather than the last hour. Do not plan a crossing that depends on a train running on time, and do not assume an overstay is a paperwork matter — it is not.

If your onward leg is a flight rather than the Hong Kong train, add the airport transfer to the calculation and leave a night's margin.
:::

## Variations

**Swap Xi'an for Chengdu.** Sichuan is inside the permitted zone. It costs a longer leg and it works.

**Enter at Shanghai instead.** Reverse the route and exit at Guangzhou all the same. Shanghai is the more forgiving arrival airport for a long-haul flight arriving late.

**Exit by air to Seoul, Tokyo or Bangkok** rather than to Hong Kong, if the fare home is better. The rule is a third country *or region*; both satisfy it.

**Do not** plan Pingyao into this trip. It sits in Shanxi but outside Taiyuan and Datong, which are the only two cities in that province the scheme admits you to.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can Americans visit China without a visa?

Yes, under the 240-hour visa-free transit scheme, which now applies to 57 nationalities including the United States following the addition of Kyrgyzstan and Vietnam on 20 August 2026. You may enter at any of 65 designated ports, stay up to ten days within 24 permitted provinces and municipalities, and you must hold a confirmed onward ticket to a third country or region.

### Does a return flight home satisfy the third-country rule?

No. A ticket back to the country you arrived from does not qualify, whatever the interval. The onward leg must be to a different country or region — and Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan all count as separate regions for this purpose, which is why ending the trip in Hong Kong is the simplest solution.

### When do the 240 hours actually start?

At 00:00 on the day after you arrive. Your arrival day is therefore free of the clock, and you effectively get part of that day plus ten full ones. The allowance expires at a specific hour, so plan the exit with a margin rather than to the minute.

### Can I visit Pingyao on a 240-hour transit trip?

No. Shanxi is a permitted province but admits transit visitors only to Taiyuan and Datong, and Pingyao is in neither. Jiangxi is limited to Nanchang and Jingdezhen, and Guangxi to twelve named municipalities. Everywhere on this route is inside a province with no such restriction.

### Is ten days enough for a first trip to China?

It is the same ten days the classic Beijing–Xi'an–Shanghai route uses, with one night traded for the journey to a legal exit. If you can get a visa, the standard route is slightly more relaxed. If you cannot, this is the version that works, and it adds a Cantonese evening and a Hong Kong arrival.

### What happens if I overstay the 240 hours?

Overstaying is an immigration offence rather than an administrative slip, and it is dealt with at the border on departure. Build the margin in advance: leave the mainland on the final day rather than in the final hours, and do not rely on a single connection to make the crossing.
