[The trip-length guide](/plan/how-many-days-in-china/) offers "a full ten days in Yunnan" as the alternative ten-day trip, then lists "a full Yunnan loop — Kunming, Dali, Lijiang, Shangri-La" again under three weeks, and gives Yunnan six nights minimum and eight to ten comfortable in its table. [The Yunnan guide](/destinations/yunnan/) sets out the same four stops with the nights each one needs.

None of that is an itinerary, and Yunnan is the route where having one matters most — because the order is decided for you.

## The ladder, and why it runs upward

[The Yunnan guide gives the four stops with their altitudes](/destinations/yunnan/): **Kunming 1,900 m, Dali 2,000 m, Lijiang 2,400 m, Shangri-La 3,200 m**. Read them in order and the trip is already designed.

| Stage | Roughly | Why it sits here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kunming | 1,900 m | Arrival, and already higher than most of Europe |
| Dali | 2,000 m | Barely a climb — a rest disguised as a destination |
| Lijiang | 2,400 m | The real step up, taken after three nights of adjustment |
| Shangri-La | 3,200 m | Last, always |

Every other Chinese itinerary on this site can be run backwards. This one cannot.

## The railway that made the mistake easy

Until recently the top of the ladder was reached by road, which is slow enough to be its own protection. That changed on **26 November 2023**, when the **丽江至香格里拉铁路** (Lijiang–Shangri-La railway) opened.

:::warn Four and a half hours from 1,900 m to 3,200 m
The National Railway Administration's announcement of **27 November 2023** gives the line as **全长139公里**, 139 kilometres, at a **设计时速140公里** — a design speed of 140 km/h — and describes it as a **国家Ⅰ级单线电气化铁路**, a national grade I single-track electrified railway. Xinhua's report of the same opening agrees on the length, the design speed and both journey times.

The times are the part that matters here. **丽江至香格里拉最快1小时18分可达** — Lijiang to Shangri-La in as little as **1 hour 18 minutes** — and from Kunming, **最快4小时30分**: **four and a half hours**.

So it is now entirely possible to land at Kunming in the morning and sleep at 3,200 m the same night. The railway did not change the mountain. [The Yunnan guide's advice to ascend gradually rather than arrive high](/destinations/yunnan/) was written when doing otherwise was difficult; it is now the easy option and the wrong one.

Nothing here is medical advice. The point is only that the fastest route and the sensible route are no longer the same route, and the timetable will not tell you that.
:::

## The route

**Fly into Kunming. Leave from Shangri-La, or come back down the line.**

### Day 1 — Kunming, 1 night

Land, and do very little. [Kunming is a genuinely mild city rather than only a transit point](/destinations/kunming/), and one night here is the first rung rather than a wasted evening.

### Days 2–3 — Dali, 2 nights

Train west to Dali. Erhai Lake, the old town, and the Bai villages north of it. The climb from Kunming is a hundred metres; the value of the stop is time, not elevation.

### Days 4–5 — Lijiang, 2 nights

On to Lijiang. Stay in **Shuhe** or **Baisha** rather than the old town itself, go into the old quarter early, and treat the second day as the acclimatisation day it is.

### Days 6–7 — Tiger Leaping Gorge, 1 night on the trail

Bus to Qiaotou and walk the high trail: about **22 km over two days**, with guesthouses along it, an overnight halfway, and the 28 bends as the hard hour. [The Yunnan guide describes the trek and its logistics](/destinations/yunnan/). Finish at Tina's and continue to Shangri-La by road.

### Days 8–9 — Shangri-La, 2 nights

The top of the ladder, and Tibetan in culture without a permit — [Shangri-La sits in Yunnan, outside the Tibet Autonomous Region](/answers/can-i-visit-tibetan-areas-without-a-tibet-permit/), which is the whole reason this route exists as an alternative to Tibet proper. Two nights: the monastery, the old town, the grasslands.

### Day 10 — Down, or out

Either fly out of Shangri-La, or take the train back down — 1h18 to Lijiang, 4h30 to Kunming. Descending fast is not the problem that ascending fast is.

:::key Book before you go
- **Trains on 12306** for the Kunming–Dali–Lijiang–Shangri-La corridor
- **Gorge guesthouses** in season — they are small and they fill
- **A Kunming arrival that lands early**, so the first night is a real night
- **Nothing at all for Shangri-La permits** — there are none to get
:::

## Variations

**Add Yuanyang.** [The Hani rice terraces are flooded and reflective from December to March](/destinations/yunnan/), six hours south of Kunming and worth two nights. They sit at the bottom of the ladder, so they belong at the start, before you climb.

**Add Jianshui.** A Qing merchant town with a Confucian temple, [about two hours by train from Kunming South and not from the central station](/answers/how-do-i-get-to-jianshui-from-kunming/). Also a start-of-trip addition.

**Skip the gorge.** Without the trek this is an eight-day trip, and the two nights come off cleanly.

**Continue to Laos.** Kunming has [an international service to Vientiane](/destinations/kunming/), which turns this into the first half of a longer overland route rather than a loop.

## Checked for newer, and what I could not read

The railway figures — the opening date, the 139 kilometres, the 140 km/h design speed, the line classification and both journey times — were read on the **National Railway Administration's** own announcement and cross-checked against **Xinhua's** report of the same opening. The two agree on every number quoted.

**Two things I have deliberately not stated.** I could not confirm the Kunming–Dali and Dali–Lijiang running times on an official host — the figures circulating come from ticketing and media sites — so this page gives no leg times for that stretch beyond the officially published Kunming–Shangri-La total. And the altitudes above are the ones this site's Yunnan guide already carries, presented as approximate; I have not verified them against a Chinese government source, and nothing on this page turns on the exact metre.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need in Yunnan?

Ten covers the classic Kunming–Dali–Lijiang–Shangri-La route including the two-day Tiger Leaping Gorge trek. Eight works if you drop the trek. Adding the Yuanyang rice terraces or Xishuangbanna pushes it towards a fortnight.

### Can you take a train from Kunming to Shangri-La?

Yes, since 26 November 2023, when the Lijiang–Shangri-La railway opened. The National Railway Administration gives the fastest Kunming–Shangri-La journey as four and a half hours, and Lijiang–Shangri-La as one hour eighteen minutes.

### Should I fly straight to Shangri-La?

It is possible and it is the one shortcut this route is built to avoid. Shangri-La is around 3,200 m, roughly 1,300 m above Kunming, and the whole point of stopping at Dali and Lijiang first is to arrive there having already spent several nights climbing.

### Do you need a permit for Shangri-La?

No. Shangri-La is in Yunnan province rather than the Tibet Autonomous Region, so the permit regime that governs Tibet does not apply. That is exactly why this route works as the accessible alternative for travellers who cannot arrange a Tibet tour.

### When is the best time to visit Yunnan?

Spring and autumn — roughly March to June and September to November — for the corridor as a whole. The Yuanyang terraces are the exception: they are at their most photogenic from December to March, when they are flooded.

### Is Yunnan cheaper than eastern China?

Generally yes. Guesthouses, food and local transport all cost noticeably less than in Beijing or Shanghai, which is why ten days here budgets closer to ¥6,400 than to the ¥8,800 [the classic ten-day triangle](/itineraries/china-10-day-itinerary/) works out at.
