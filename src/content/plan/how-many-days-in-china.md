There is a reliable formula for a China trip that fails: pick the six famous cities, allocate two nights each, connect them with overnight trains, and assume the Great Wall is an afternoon.

The reason it fails is scale. The Forbidden City complex is 720,000 m². The Terracotta Army sits in three separate hangars 40 km outside Xi'an. Zhangjiajie National Forest Park is 4,800 hectares with four separate entrances. These are not morning stops.

How many days you need is the second question, though. [The first is whether you have solved the four infrastructural problems](/plan/first-time-china-travel-guide/) — payment, connectivity, navigation and entry — because a visitor who has not cannot use the days they book.

## Minimum nights per destination

| Destination | Minimum | Comfortable | What the extra day buys |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Beijing | 3 nights | 4–5 | A second Great Wall section, the 798 art district, a hutong day |
| Shanghai | 2 nights | 3 | Museums, the former French Concession on foot, a water town |
| Xi'an | 2 nights | 3 | Han Yang Ling, a full day cycling the walls and the Muslim Quarter |
| Chengdu | 2 nights | 3–4 | Leshan Buddha or Mount Qingcheng, teahouse afternoons |
| Guilin & Yangshuo | 3 nights | 4 | Longji rice terraces as a proper day, not a dash |
| Zhangjiajie | 3 nights | 4 | Tianmen Mountain plus two days in the forest park |
| Hangzhou | 1 night | 2 | Longjing tea villages, the canal, Xixi wetlands |
| Suzhou | 1 night | 2 | Three gardens instead of one, plus Tongli |
| [Hong Kong](/destinations/hong-kong/) | 3 nights | 4 | Lantau, Sai Kung, a hiking day |
| Yunnan (Kunming–Dali–Lijiang) | 6 nights | 8–10 | Shangri-La, Tiger Leaping Gorge |
| Tibet (Lhasa + around) | 5 nights | 7–8 | Altitude acclimatisation is not optional |
| Xinjiang | 7 nights | 10–14 | The distances are enormous |

**Add travel days.** A high-speed rail transfer between two cities consumes roughly half a usable day once you account for checkout, getting to the station 40 minutes early, the journey and the trip to the new hotel. Two cities four hours apart are not "the same day".

:::key The 3+1 rule
For most travellers: **three nights per major city, plus one travel day per city change**. A four-city trip is therefore 12 nights minimum before you add anything scenic. This is why 14 days is the answer to "how long for a first trip".
:::

## By trip length

### 5 days — one city, done properly

Not a China trip; a city trip. Choose Beijing if you want the imperial and historical China of the imagination, or Shanghai if you want the modern one and a couple of water towns. Add a day trip and you will come home satisfied rather than exhausted.

Best for: business trip extensions, visa-free transit stopovers.

### 7 days — two cities

**Beijing + Shanghai** (four nights and three) or **Beijing + Xi'an**. The 4.5-hour train between Beijing and Shanghai makes the first pairing painless; Beijing–Xi'an is the more coherent historical route. Resist adding a third. See the [7-day itinerary](/itineraries/china-7-day-itinerary/).

### 10 days — three cities

The classic triangle: **Beijing → Xi'an → Shanghai**, three or four nights each. Everything is connected by high-speed rail and you get the Great Wall, the Terracotta Army and the Bund without moving every other morning. The [10-day itinerary](/itineraries/china-10-day-itinerary/) lays it out day by day.

Alternative for repeat visitors: **Chengdu → Chongqing → Zhangjiajie**, or a full ten days in Yunnan.

### 14 days — the sweet spot

**Beijing → Xi'an → Chengdu → Shanghai** is the standard, and it is standard because it works: imperial history, ancient capital, pandas and Sichuan food, then contemporary China. Fourteen days absorbs one bad-weather day and one slow morning without wrecking the plan. Full detail in the [14-day itinerary](/itineraries/china-14-day-itinerary/). With three weeks you can add the southwest or the Silk Road without rushing — see the [21-day itinerary](/itineraries/china-21-day-itinerary/).

### 21 days — add scenery or go west

Three weeks lets you keep the classic route and add either:

- **Scenery:** Guilin and Yangshuo, or Zhangjiajie, in the middle of the trip
- **Depth:** a full Yunnan loop — Kunming, Dali, Lijiang, Shangri-La
- **The Silk Road:** Xi'an → Lanzhou → Dunhuang → Turpan → Kashgar, which needs the whole three weeks on its own. See the [Silk Road itinerary](/itineraries/silk-road-china-itinerary/).

### A month or more

At this length the question changes from "what can I fit" to "what do I want to understand". Slow down: a week in one province, time in second-tier cities such as Quanzhou, Kaili or Yinchuan, and space for the parts of China that never make a highlight list.

## Common mistakes about time

**Underestimating Beijing.** People allocate two nights and end up choosing between the Great Wall and the Forbidden City. Three nights is the floor; four is right.

**Treating Chengdu as a panda stop.** The pandas are a morning. The city — teahouses, Sichuan food, the Wuhou and Qingyang temples, day trips to Leshan and Qingcheng — is the reason to stay.

**Overnight trains as free time.** On non-high-speed routes a sleeper genuinely saves a night. But a 22:00–07:00 sleeper leaves you tired and unable to check in until 14:00, so it costs part of the next day.

**Ignoring queue time.** At the Terracotta Army in summer, security plus the shuttle plus the Pit 1 crowd is 90 minutes before you have seen anything.

**Forgetting the closures.** Many museums close Mondays, and the Forbidden City does outside peak season — but not all of them, and the Shanghai Museum's two buildings differ from each other. Check the specific building before you build a day around one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need in Beijing?

Three nights is the practical minimum — one day for the Forbidden City and Tiananmen area, one full day for the Great Wall, and one for the Temple of Heaven, Summer Palace or the hutongs. Four nights lets you add the 798 art district or a second Wall section without rushing.

### Is one week enough for China?

Enough for two cities done well — typically Beijing and Shanghai, or Beijing and Xi'an. It is not enough for the classic four-city route; attempting that in seven days means spending roughly a third of the trip in transit.

### How long should I spend in Shanghai?

Two nights covers the Bund, the former French Concession, Yu Garden and one museum. Three nights adds a water town such as Zhujiajiao or Tongli, or a day trip to Suzhou, which is 25 minutes away by train.

### Is 10 days enough for China?

Yes, for a well-chosen three-city route such as Beijing, Xi'an and Shanghai. Ten days is a genuinely good first trip; it is the shortest length at which you see both imperial and modern China without feeling rushed.

### How many days do you need for the Great Wall?

One full day. Mutianyu and Badaling are day trips from central Beijing (2.5–4 hours round trip in transport, 3–4 hours on the Wall). Hiking sections such as Jinshanling to Simatai need a very long day or an overnight in a village guesthouse.
